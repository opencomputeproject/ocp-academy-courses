**Title**\
FCSA: Architecting Interoperable Chiplet Systems

**Short Description**\
Apply FCSA to chiplet system architecture, interfaces, boot, security, and evidence for interoperable integration.

**Long Description**

Use Foundation Chiplet System Architecture (FCSA) 1.1.0 to review a chiplet system from architectural roles and interfaces to coordinated boot and maintained trust. A running accelerator integration case, narrated diagrams, knowledge checks, and practical workbook exercises connect the specification to supplier questions, integration evidence, and product requirements. Relevant 2026 OCP Global Summit presentations provide additional context.

**Overall duration: 85 minutes**

**Module 1: From chiplet opportunity to system architecture — 16 minutes.**\
Define the system boundary and distinguish foundation, design, and profile requirements. Learn to interpret normative statements, compliance levels, and the evidence needed for a scoped architectural claim.

**Module 2: Choose a system type and classify its chiplets — 16 minutes.**\
Compare Compute & Hub and Compute Tile systems. Use functionality, coherent caching, address translation, and required interfaces to classify chiplets and identify missing supplier evidence.

**Module 3: Make interfaces and boot work together — 17 minutes.**\
Trace functional requirements through protocol and transport mappings, compatible versions, and activity states. Compare boot-image loading models and distinguish sequence control, resource management, and security responsibilities.

**Module 4: Build security around the composed system — 18 minutes.**\
Connect chiplet-local attestation evidence to system-level composition and policy decisions. Apply product-specific threat analysis and explain why updates, resets, debug changes, and recovery can require revalidation.

**Module 5: Evaluate integration readiness and future profiles — 18 minutes.**\
Assemble an integration evidence worksheet covering interfaces, boot, trust, lifecycle, and scalability. Examine transaction adaptation and combined snoop-filter demand, then distinguish published FCSA requirements from proposed Physical AI work.
