Module two moved from product names to specification thinking. Treated water and P G twenty-five both have a place, but they carry different thermal properties and operating burdens. Published P G twenty-five is ready-to-use at twenty-five to twenty-eight percent propylene glycol by weight, with product-specific inhibitors and compatibility evidence.

The water-based coolant table gives measurable fresh-product limits for solids, dissolved material, conductivity, pH, hardness, turbidity, and bacteria. After commissioning, those properties become part of a broader baseline and treatment plan. Inhibitor chemistry matters because it shapes corrosion protection and deposit risk. Compatibility matters because the fluid touches more than the cold plate.

A good single-phase fluid specification is evidence-backed, system-specific, and designed to be operated.
