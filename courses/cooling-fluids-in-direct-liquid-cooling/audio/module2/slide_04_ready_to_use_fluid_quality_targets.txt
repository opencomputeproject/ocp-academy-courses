The published water-based coolant base specification turns fresh-product quality into measurable acceptance limits. The selected values on screen apply to ready-to-use coolant before it enters service. They include total suspended solids below five parts per million, total dissolved solids below two thousand, conductivity below two thousand microsiemens per centimeter, pH from eight to ten point five, hardness below thirty parts per million as calcium carbonate, turbidity below five N T U, and bacteria below one colony-forming unit per milliliter.

These are product requirements, not a complete operating treatment plan. Once the coolant is in service, conductivity and T D S become trend signals for dissolved material and contamination. Hardness can point to makeup water quality or leaching sources. Turbidity and T S S can reveal particles that load filters or threaten microchannels. Bacteria counts reveal biological risk, while inhibitor targets remain product-specific.

The learner does not need to memorize every threshold today. The important habit is to verify incoming product against its certificate of analysis, then compare the operating loop against its baseline and treatment plan.
