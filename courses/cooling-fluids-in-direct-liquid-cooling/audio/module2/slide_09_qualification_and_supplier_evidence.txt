A fluid selection should leave evidence behind. Both published base specifications require a certificate of analysis for each shipment. For P G twenty-five, that certificate includes concentration, or refractive index with measurement temperature, and pH at a minimum. The technical data sheet should report measured viscosity, heat capacity, density, and thermal conductivity across the expected operating range. The owner should also keep the safety data sheet, treatment plan, compatibility evidence, batch identity, and supplier recommendations.

The published corrosion tests provide important evidence, but they do not exhaustively qualify every plastic, elastomer, adhesive, connector, or operating condition. The fluid supplier, I T supplier, contractor, and service provider should align on the exact wetted materials and operating conditions. This is especially important in a multi-vendor rack environment. A reusable liquid cooling infrastructure may hold different hardware over multiple refresh cycles. Without shipment evidence, product data, and traceable records, each refresh recreates uncertainty.
