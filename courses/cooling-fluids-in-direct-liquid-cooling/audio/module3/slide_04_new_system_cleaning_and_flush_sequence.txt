For a new system, begin with a documented cleaning plan based on fabrication history, expected contaminants, system metallurgy, and supplier guidance. A clean-water flush can suspend loose construction debris, but it does not prove that oil, grease, flux, weld residue, or corrosion products have been removed. Where those contaminants may be present, qualified specialists should determine whether degreasing, chemical cleaning, or passivation is needed. Any cleaner must be compatible with every wetted material and completely removed before final fill.

The O C P operating guideline describes a thirty-minute room-temperature water circulation step, first with cold plates isolated and then connected. Treat that as one debris-flushing step inside the approved cleaning plan, not as the complete cleanliness requirement. Replace filters if needed, then move to the final fluid fill. For P G twenty-five, use the ready-to-use product without on-site dilution, mixing, or preparation.
