Routine checks must fit the formulation. For P G twenty-five, prioritize pH, refractive index for glycol concentration, and appearance. Interpret refractive index using the supplier's specific fluid data; it is not a direct measurement of inhibitor health. Filter loading and turbidity add evidence about cleanliness.

Conductivity is a supporting trend signal for inhibited glycol, not normally its primary health test. Additives and temperature affect the reading, and a stable value does not prove that corrosion protection is intact. Bacteria testing and treatment also follow the coolant-specific plan.

Scheduled laboratory analysis complements those checks with inhibitor levels, reserve alkalinity where appropriate, and dissolved metals measured by I C P. Select copper, iron, aluminum, and other elements from the wetted-material inventory. Compare baselines and trends; one detectable result alone does not establish active corrosion.
