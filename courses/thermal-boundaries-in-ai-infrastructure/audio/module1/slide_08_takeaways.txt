Begin with the limit you can demonstrate. Total power, local heat flux, and temperature margin answer different questions. A larger downstream cooling rating does not prove that acquisition at the device will work.

Then classify the boundary. Efficiency concerns growing overhead. Economics concerns the full cost of keeping the method. Physics concerns whether achievable conditions can maintain the required operating state.

The avoidance zone explains how an incumbent can continue after its total-cost advantage has weakened. Adoption also depends on who pays the cost, who controls the design, and who must accept the new dependencies.

Finally, distinguish the debts. Complexity debt grows as the incumbent is stretched. Preparation debt grows when the alternative's readiness is deferred. A useful transition plan tracks both, with named organizations and concrete evidence for the next action.
