The course follows three connected decisions. First, identify which boundary the current cooling method is approaching. Second, determine what heat the architecture actually covers and what remains unresolved. Third, evaluate the economics, reliability, and evidence needed to bring a new arrangement into service.

We assume you're familiar with servers, racks, and basic facility operations. Cooling terms will be introduced as they're used. The optional Liquid Cooling Technologies Overview offers an introductory route if you'd like more preparation.

The specialist courses remain useful throughout. Fluids covers chemistry and operating practice. Two-Phase D L C covers phase-change loops and their fluids. Deschutes and Brazos examine particular open cooling implementations. Here, those courses are optional routes to detail. The questions in this course can be answered from its own teaching.

Keep one practical question in mind: what is limiting this deployment, and which evidence would justify changing the architecture?
