The thermal constraint can move after an upgrade. Solving the processor hotspot may leave the distributed load as the next problem, so account for what the architecture captures and what remains.

Acquisition, transport, and rejection are separate jobs. A downstream capacity claim cannot prove device-level acquisition, and the I T and facility sides have different owners and lifecycle clocks.

Read numerical limits with their conditions. The approximately four-kilowatt cold-plate discussion concerns a constrained reference architecture. Flow, temperature rise, pressure, geometry, and mechanical behavior belong in the same assessment.

Finally, match surface and volume approaches to their coverage. Microfluidics can extend acquisition capability but still needs downstream heat removal. A justified combination has evidence at its interfaces, and every new dependency has an accountable owner.
