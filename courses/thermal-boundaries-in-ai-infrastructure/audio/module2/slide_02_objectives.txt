By the end of this module, you'll be able to identify what remains unresolved after a cooling change. A processor may have enough thermal margin while memory, networking, storage, or power-conversion components continue to stress the surrounding system.

You'll also locate the limiting step along the thermal path. That lets you distinguish a heat-acquisition problem from a transport or rejection problem, and avoid treating a downstream capacity rating as proof of chip-level capability.

The reference cold-plate example develops a third skill: reading a boundary with its conditions. Flow, coolant temperature rise, geometry, pressure, and mechanical behavior belong together in that assessment.

Finally, you'll compare the coverage of surface and volume methods and identify where complementary tools may help. The objective is a justified architecture hypothesis, followed by evidence for the actual hardware and operating conditions.
