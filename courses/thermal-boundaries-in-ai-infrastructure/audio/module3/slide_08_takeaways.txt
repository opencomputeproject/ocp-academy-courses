Use the framework as a sequence of questions. What thermal job is limiting the deployment? Which boundary is being approached? What does extending the current method cost, and what dependencies would the alternative introduce?

Then examine the evidence. Full ownership cost crosses budgets and lifecycles. Reliability results depend on configuration and assumptions, and individual component failures are different from shared outages. A cost model becomes decision support when its inputs and limits remain visible.

Finally, prepare the handoffs. Shared interfaces, qualified suppliers, service capability, and observable controls help turn an option into something the ecosystem can deploy. Commissioning supplies the witnessed evidence that the assembled system behaves under realistic load and fault conditions.

Readiness is a choice the industry can make before the next transition becomes urgent. Keep the toolbox useful by making its interfaces and evidence ready, while matching each method to the heat and site it must serve.
