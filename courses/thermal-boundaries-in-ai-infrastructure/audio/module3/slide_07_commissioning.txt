A steady temperature at one operating point proves that point. It does not prove the behavior of the assembled cooling system as load changes or faults occur.

Commissioning must connect each stimulus to an observable response. For a load step, agree the starting state, change in load, ramp, measurement locations, allowable excursions, and return-to-band criteria before the test. The power and cooling sides should witness the record together, because a dense-system load change is both an electrical and a thermal event.

Fault evidence is separate. Leak detection, isolation, a lost sensor or communication path, and a shared equipment failure need the response expected for the specific architecture. Do not assume every fluid or cooling method should trigger the same action. The approved system and site procedure determine what must happen.

Verify the seam as well as the products. Flow and pressure must be appropriate across the served domain, not only at the nearest rack. Telemetry from acquisition, transport, and rejection must reach the controls and people responsible for acting on it. Where methods are combined, their handoffs need system-level evidence.
