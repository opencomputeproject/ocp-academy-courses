Total cost of ownership is wider than the cooling equipment purchase order. The thermal path helps locate where that cost lands and who can act on it.

Acquisition costs are closely tied to the I T hardware: cooling surfaces, packaging, in-system plumbing, and redesign as the thermal target changes. Transport includes the interfaces and equipment at the seam, such as manifolds, coolant distribution, and an immersion system's integrated circulation arrangement. Rejection includes the facility plant and the infrastructure that ultimately removes or reuses the heat.

The purchase can look expensive at one boundary while reducing cost elsewhere. A denser arrangement may use less building area for a given deployment. Warmer useful return temperatures may change rejection opportunities. Maintenance, service labor, repairs, and interruptions also belong in the comparison.

Heat reuse has conditions of its own. A warm return is an opportunity only if its temperature, the receiving application, and the local commercial arrangement support useful recovery. It is not an automatic credit to every liquid-cooling proposal.

The paper's cost case annualizes I T capital over five years and facility capital over fifteen. Those are declared modeling assumptions, not a required replacement schedule for a site. Their purpose here is to make the lifecycle mismatch visible.

The linked Fluids course describes much of the qualification and operating work that a cost estimate must include. Do not omit that labor because chemistry and maintenance occur outside the equipment quote.

Build the comparison around the same service objective, operating conditions, and stated accounting assumptions. Then keep both the total and the distribution of cost visible.
