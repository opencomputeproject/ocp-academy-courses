Project workstreams are usually the best place to begin because that is where the Community's real problems are discussed.

Start by identifying the projects and workstreams most relevant to your organization's expertise. Then involve the engineers, operators, or specialists who can understand the details and contribute to the work. This isn't a job for a marketing representative looking for a place to insert a product message.

Once your team joins the regular calls, listen first. Learn what the group is trying to accomplish. Notice the constraints, the decisions already made, the questions that remain open, and the people who have been carrying the work. That context will show you where help is welcome and where an idea may already have been explored.

After listening and participating, useful opportunities tend to emerge naturally. Your team may have data, implementation experience, a design approach, or a connection that helps the group move forward. Offering that help builds awareness and trust because it responds to a real need.

Leadership can follow later. Project and Steering Committee roles are volunteer positions that carry responsibility to the Community. They make the most sense after someone has established a record of service, judgment, and follow-through.
