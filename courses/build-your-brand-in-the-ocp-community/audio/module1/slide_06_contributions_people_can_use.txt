# pronunciation: OCP -> "O C P"
A contribution doesn't have to be a complete hardware specification or a set of design files. Those are important, but the Community can benefit from many forms of shared knowledge.

A technical foundation might be a specification, a requirements document, a reference architecture, or a design. Proven practice might take the form of a tested configuration, implementation guidance, or a best-practices document. Evidence might be a case study, a success story, or a milestone that shows what worked and what others should know.

Education matters too. White papers, seminars, videos, and structured learning can turn one team's experience into a resource that helps many people. Eligible products and facilities may also pursue recognition through current O C P programs. The exact program details can change, so the durable principle is alignment: connect the offering to approved Community work and follow the current process.

Whenever possible, build with others. A contribution involving several organizations is stronger evidence that the result addresses a shared need rather than one company's promotional goal. Collaborative work is often easier for the Community to trust, accept, and amplify because it demonstrates the behavior O C P is designed to encourage.

The question to ask is not, "What can we announce?" Ask, "What can we share that will help someone solve a problem?" That shift turns expertise into Community value.
