# pronunciation: OCP -> "O C P"
O C P channels are curated. Before asking the Foundation to publish or promote something, test the story against the Community's editorial filter.

The strongest content is grounded in real O C P activity. It may report progress from a project or workstream. It may explain a contribution such as a specification, reference architecture, or white paper. It may show how an O C P-aligned product, service, or facility helped solve a practical problem.

Case studies, success stories, and milestones are especially useful when they include evidence and a lesson that others can apply. Partnerships are valuable because they show organizations collaborating across company boundaries. A story involving several Community members often demonstrates broader relevance than a single-company announcement.

What doesn't fit is a generic commercial. A message that could be sent to any mailing list, with no connection to O C P work or Community needs, doesn't reward the audience for its attention.

Dirk's guidance is a good discipline for any communicator: speak when you have something meaningful to say.

You don't need to fill a posting schedule for its own sake. Bring the Community progress, evidence, and insight.

Then choose the channel that lets that value land clearly. When the story is useful and relevant, amplification has a purpose.
