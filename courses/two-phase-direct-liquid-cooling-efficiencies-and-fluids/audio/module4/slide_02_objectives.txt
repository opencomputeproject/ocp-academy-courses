# pronunciation: COA -> "C O A"; TCS -> "T C S"; SDS -> "S D S"; AHRI -> "AH-ree"
By the end of this module, you will be able to describe the operational playbook for a two-phase dielectric cooling system.

You will identify startup-readiness items, including C O A retention, safety checks, temperature-window checks, hardware inspection, and clean component handling.

You will explain why filling and purging matter, especially when service or server additions introduce small volumes of air into the loop.

You will interpret the routine operating signals: vapor pressure, vapor temperature, liquid pressure, liquid temperature, and buffer liquid level.

You will also connect those real-time signals to the annual fluid-purity requirement under AHRI Standard seven hundred. Purity monitoring, documented replenishment, and inventory records now belong explicitly in the lifecycle plan.

Finally, you will connect maintenance, leak response, deployment patterns, reuse, refurbishment, and disposal to traceable records.

Operations are where a technology earns trust. The goal is a system that is observable, auditable, and uneventful to run.
