# pronunciation: SDS -> "S D S"; AHRI -> "AH-ree"; COA -> "C O A"
Records are what let a liquid cooling system survive changes over time.

The detailed guideline notes that the fluid usually has no expiration date and may not require routine replacement. The new Base Specification adds an important condition: fluid purity must be monitored annually.

Use the purity criteria and methods associated with AHRI Standard seven hundred, twenty twenty-four edition, and retain the results. Long fluid life is therefore an evidence-based operating outcome, not permission to stop checking the fluid.

Inventory also needs a history. The Base Specification calls for replenishment when minor evaporative losses or leakage reduce the charge. Record each addition and reconcile it with buffer level, leak indications, repairs, and recovered fluid.

The lifecycle record should include the fluid source, C O A, analysis results, replaced components, and the materials of construction that contact the fluid. If a seal, tube, pump, valve, or cold plate changes, the compatibility basis may need review.

At decommissioning, drain-back, reuse, refurbishment, recovery, and disposal should follow supplier guidance and applicable requirements.

The fluid may remain useful for a long time. The evidence must remain with it for just as long.
