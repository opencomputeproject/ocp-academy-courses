# pronunciation: COA -> "C O A"; TCS -> "T C S"
Startup is the moment to catch problems while they are still inexpensive.

Begin with product quality. Retain the Certificate of Analysis, or C O A, for the received fluid so its identity and condition remain traceable.

Then verify the correct temperature window. The detailed guideline calls for a room-ambient check from five degrees C to thirty-five degrees C before startup. The Base Specification separately defines fluid operation from four degrees C to seventy-five degrees C and fluid storage from minus twenty degrees C to sixty degrees C. These limits answer different questions and should not be substituted for one another.

Safety and operational checks come next. Review the applicable requirements and service instructions. Confirm that buffers, valves, ports, and controls are in their intended startup state.

Hardware inspection is practical and visual. Look for bent tubes, cracks, damaged connectors, incorrect installation, or other defects before fluid moves.

Cleanliness is just as important. Products in the T C S loop should match the controlled wetted-material list and remain clean and sealed until installation.

The startup mindset is simple: verify the fluid, verify each temperature condition, verify the parts, and verify cleanliness before the system becomes harder to inspect.
