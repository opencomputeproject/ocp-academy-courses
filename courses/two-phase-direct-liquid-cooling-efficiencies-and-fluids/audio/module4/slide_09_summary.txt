# pronunciation: DLC -> "D L C"; COA -> "C O A"; SDS -> "S D S"; AHRI -> "AH-ree"
Module four turns two-phase D L C into an operating checklist.

Start with readiness. Retain the C O A, review safety requirements, distinguish ambient, fluid-operating, and storage temperature windows, inspect hardware, and keep components clean until installation.

Fill the system deliberately, then monitor vapor pressure, vapor temperature, liquid pressure, liquid temperature, and buffer liquid level as a set.

Purge when non-condensable gases move the system away from expected pressure-temperature behavior. The detailed guideline defines a greater-than-twenty-five-percent vapor-pressure deviation as a condition requiring purge.

Add the longer-cycle requirement. Monitor fluid purity annually according to AHRI Standard seven hundred. Document replenishment for minor evaporative losses or leakage, and keep the analysis with the fluid history.

Respond to leaks without panic and without neglect. Dielectric fluid changes the immediate electronics risk, but detection, repair, S D S guidance, recovery, and records still matter.

Finally, keep component changes, wetted materials, reuse, refurbishment, and disposal auditable.

That combination of real-time signals and lifecycle evidence is what makes the system scalable.
