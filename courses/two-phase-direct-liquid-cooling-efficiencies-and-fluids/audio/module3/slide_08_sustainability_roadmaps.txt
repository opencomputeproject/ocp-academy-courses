# pronunciation: ODP -> "O D P"; GWP -> "G W P"; PFAS -> "P FAS"; ASHRAE -> "ASH-ray"; 2L -> "two L"
Sustainability has to be handled with precision.

The OCP Base Specification sets G W P below seven hundred and prefers zero or near-zero O D P. It also requires the selected fluid to meet applicable local and global requirements.

The safety baseline has changed from the narrower expectation in the earlier detailed guideline. The Base Specification allows Class one or Class two L flammability with Class A toxicity. Class two L is therefore a current option for suitable use cases, not only a future possibility. It still requires careful code, ventilation, detection, service, and site-safety review.

Some fluorinated fluid families may also raise P FAS questions. The OCP EMEA panel highlighted why fluid roadmaps matter: chemistry, regulations, and customer requirements continue to evolve.

A responsible procurement review should ask what the supplier can document about G W P, O D P, chemical family, containment, recovery, purity, compatibility, and end-of-life options.

The practical goal is not a single perfect fluid. It is a fluid whose thermal performance, safety classification, environmental profile, material evidence, and lifecycle controls fit the project.

Environmental improvement does not replace engineering proof. Both have to hold at the same time.
