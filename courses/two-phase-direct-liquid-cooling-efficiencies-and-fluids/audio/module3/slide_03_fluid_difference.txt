The fluid in a two-phase system is not an interchangeable detail.

It must boil and condense in the intended temperature and pressure range. It must remain electrically non-conductive and chemically stable. It must also be compatible with the metals, polymers, elastomers, seals, pumps, Q Ds, heat exchangers, manifolds, and cold plates that it touches.

The OCP Base Specification turns those needs into a vendor-neutral compliance baseline. The detailed fluid-use guideline then carries the engineering further into qualification, handling, monitoring, service, and end-of-life practices.

That distinction matters because a fluid cannot be evaluated from a boiling point or dielectric label alone. Its thermal performance, material behavior, environmental profile, safety classification, purity, and lifecycle controls all interact.

A well-selected dielectric fluid can reduce some water-loop concerns inside the server, including conductivity risk, water-driven corrosion, and biological growth. But those benefits depend on treating the fluid, the T C S hardware, the controls, and the operating plan as one system.

The practical question is not simply, “Is this fluid dielectric?” It is, “Can this exact fluid perform safely and predictably with this exact system for its intended life?”
