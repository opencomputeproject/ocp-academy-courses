Start with the roles of the two OCP documents.

The Base Specification sets a boiling-point range of minus fifty degrees C to sixty degrees C at one atmosphere, with application-dependent selection. The detailed fluid-use guideline uses the corrected range of minus twenty-six point three degrees C to sixty degrees C. These are not competing corrections. One is the current compliance envelope, and the other is a more specific selection range in the detailed guidance.

The Base Specification also requires latent heat of vaporization of at least one hundred kilojoules per kilogram and fluid density above eight hundred kilograms per cubic meter at twenty-five degrees C. Heat transfer occurs through nucleate boiling or evaporation, and performance must remain stable after testing that simulates five years of operation.

For safety, toxicity is Class A under ASHRAE Standard thirty-four. Flammability may be Class one or Class two L, depending on the application and its safety review.

The environmental baseline is G W P below seven hundred, with zero or near-zero O D P preferred and compliance with applicable requirements.

Finally, separate the temperature windows. Fluid operation spans four degrees C to seventy-five degrees C. Storage spans minus twenty degrees C to sixty degrees C.

These values are a starting screen. The deployment still needs compatibility, stability, purity, leak response, and system-level evidence.
