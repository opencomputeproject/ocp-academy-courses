Compatibility starts with a simple question: what does the fluid touch?

Wetted materials include obvious parts such as cold plates, heat exchangers, and tubing. They also include pump seals, quick-connect seals, valve seats, buffers, manifolds, service ports, and internal C D U surfaces.

The Base Specification requires supplier-tested compatibility for all wetted materials in the cold-plate loop. The detailed guideline adds another layer by making the system owner responsible for the compatibility basis of the selected fluid and the complete liquid-cooled rack.

Those responsibilities fit together. Supplier evidence anchors compliance, while the deployment team controls the real configuration.

If a seal, connector, pump, tube, valve, or cold plate changes, the wetted-material set changes. The dielectric label does not make the replacement automatically compatible. The team may need new evidence for the same fluid and operating conditions.

An unsupported statement that a material is “compatible” is not enough for a fleet decision. Keep the test records, material identities, fluid identity, operating conditions, and change history together.

Treat the wetted-material list as a controlled engineering artifact. It belongs in design reviews, supplier submittals, commissioning records, maintenance procedures, and change control.
