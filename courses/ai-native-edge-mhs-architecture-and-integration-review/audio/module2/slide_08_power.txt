# pronunciation: HPM -> H P M; MHS -> M H S; CCI -> C C I; KOZ -> keep out zone; DC-SCM -> D C S C M; CFF -> C F F; PCIe -> P C I Express; NIC -> Nick; HSIO -> high speed I O; PICPWR -> pick power; PCB -> P C B; M-PIC -> M Pick; M-XIO -> M X I O; M-PNP -> M P N P

Power review starts with the ingress path and the actual board budget. Cabled ingress and egress use the specified pick power connector families: two by six with twelve sidebands, or two by three with six sidebands. The far power zone must include the body of at least one two-by-six connector. It must also contain enough connectors to meet the full H P M power budget when used for ingress. A proposal using only two-by-three connectors therefore misses an explicit condition, even if someone adds their nameplate ratings together.

Published connector ratings don't establish what the board or system delivers. Each implemented fixed riser must receive at least eighty-seven watts. That minimum supports one seventy-five watt P C I Express C E M card plus the specified conversion and miscellaneous allowance. The connector's higher published rating isn't permission to assume the board supplies that higher power.

Review the power-distribution board, wiring, loading, voltage drop and implemented interfaces using the product design and companion specifications. M Pick governs relevant power and sideband details. The source delegates additional electrical requirements to its M H S family references.

Your record should connect the required condition to a selected connector, a budget and evidence of delivered power. If any part is missing, describe the gap. Physical presence and an adequate connector rating alone can't close the power review.
