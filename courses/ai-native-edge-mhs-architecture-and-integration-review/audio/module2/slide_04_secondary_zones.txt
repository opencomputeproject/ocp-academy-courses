The secondary side has four zones with different purposes. Zone one is the general component region. Its universal component-height restriction is one point six millimeters, except where the defined zones or permitted local exceptions apply.

A tall-component exception must remain local. Each instance must not exceed four hundred square millimeters, and separate instances must not be closer than ten millimeters. The recommended combined board-and-component height of five point two millimeters is compatibility guidance. Don't silently convert that recommendation into another universal requirement, or treat the permitted exception as a guarantee of chassis clearance.

Zone two protects the chassis-hook interface. Its required zero-height keep out areas include floating far-edge locations across board depths. A selected tray may not use a particular area, but the board can still need that area for a different supported system. Through-hole power or signal leads aren't permitted in these hook keepouts. The separate allowance for specified alignment posts and grounded retention barbs must be read precisely.

Zone three provides for secondary support beneath areas that may deflect during assembly. Zone four provides the CPU backing-plate allowance. That allowance depends on board thickness and other tolerances. The source's stack-up is an example, not a universal chassis construction. Review the actual parts and their worst-case relationship before closing a clearance finding.
