Begin with this simplified fabric. Electrical packet switches connect groups of endpoints through an electrical interconnect stage. Following one optical link through that stage requires optical-to-electrical conversion, packet processing, and conversion back to light.

Now identify the layer that an optical circuit switching architecture changes. We replace the selected interconnect stage with a fabric of configurable optical paths. This is an architectural example, not a port-for-port drawing of a particular production network.

The surrounding electrical switches remain important. They still handle packet functions while the optical fabric provides selected connectivity between them. A controller can revise those connections as the deployment requires. This does not mean that every endpoint has a permanently dedicated path to every other endpoint.

Finally, consider an endpoint speed upgrade. The optical switching path does not interpret the bit rate, so it can avoid the electronic switching limits of the replaced stage. Engineers must still verify transceiver compatibility, optical bandwidth, insertion loss, and the complete link budget. Rate transparency is a valuable property, not a waiver of system engineering.
