Optical reconfiguration can redirect a path around a failed element or connect a shared spare. If enough compatible healthy resources and optical capacity remain, that may restore the intended connectivity and bandwidth. This makes the physical layer an active part of the resilience design.

Several dependencies remain. A system must detect and locate the fault, choose a valid replacement path, apply the change, and verify the result. Higher layers may need to retry communication, restore a checkpoint or restart work. An optical bypass cannot recreate compute state that was lost with a failed accelerator or server. The 2026 zero-touch discussion adds a useful operational lens: validate the actual peer and end-to-end path, and plan rollback when the new state does not match the intended service.
