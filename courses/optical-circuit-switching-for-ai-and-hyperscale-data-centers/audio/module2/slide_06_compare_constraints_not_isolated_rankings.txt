Use a comparison matrix to expose questions, not to declare a permanent ranking. Port count, also called radix, tells you how much connectivity the switch can expose. Insertion loss tells you how much optical margin each permitted path consumes. Switching time must cover the relevant transition and settling conditions. Power should distinguish changing a state from maintaining it, while including the surrounding system.

The categories on screen summarize the original technology landscape. Real products can cross those broad categories. Ask for the exact port configuration, wavelength range, path-loss distribution and measurement method. Then compare devices against the same workload and service requirement. A very fast device with insufficient optical margin or connectivity can still be the wrong choice.
