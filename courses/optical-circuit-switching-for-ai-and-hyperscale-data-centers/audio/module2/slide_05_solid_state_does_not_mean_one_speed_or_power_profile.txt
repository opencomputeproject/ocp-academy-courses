Liquid crystals manipulate polarization without mechanically steering a mirror. Silicon photonics can change the path within integrated waveguides. Metasurfaces control a wavefront using many small tunable elements. All avoid some forms of mechanical movement, but their operating characteristics remain different.

Within silicon photonics, electro-optic and thermo-optic actuation are especially important to distinguish. Electrical changes and thermal changes do not have identical response times or holding-power requirements. Cascaded switching elements can also add optical loss as a design scales. Semiconductor optical amplifiers may compensate some loss, but introduce their own power, noise and integration considerations. Evaluate the complete implementation rather than assuming that chip-scale integration automatically supplies high radix, low loss and the fastest possible switching at once.
