Begin with the useful communication interval. If the circuit can be established before a long phase begins, millisecond reconfiguration may be entirely appropriate. If connectivity must change repeatedly within a short phase, control and settling overhead become more demanding.

Next, determine the number of simultaneous connections and the required port arrangement. Trace the longest or highest-loss permitted optical path. Finally, include power, operational maturity, fault handling and monitoring. The result is a set of requirements a supplier can answer with measurements. This process is more informative than selecting the fastest family first and discovering later that its radix or optical budget does not fit the architecture.
