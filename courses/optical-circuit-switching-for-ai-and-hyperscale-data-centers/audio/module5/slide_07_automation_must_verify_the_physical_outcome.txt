An automated control request is not the same as an operationally verified path. The zero-touch fabric discussion extends automation across a lifecycle: discover devices and peers, establish intended connectivity, check the resulting path, and respond when observed behavior differs from the plan.

That distinction matters because a logically valid command can still produce an unusable service if cabling, peer identity or optical conditions are wrong. Validation and rollback give the operator a controlled response. Effective automation therefore includes evidence about what actually happened, rather than merely recording that a command was sent. This is a useful contribution area for teams with deployment experience: supply realistic failure cases and clearly defined acceptance checks.
