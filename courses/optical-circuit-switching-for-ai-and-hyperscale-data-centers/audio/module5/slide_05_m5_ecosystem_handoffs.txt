An optical component becomes useful in a deployed system only when the surrounding ecosystem can understand and control it. Start with a technology supplier. The supplier describes the supported ports, optical behavior, and operating limits of the device.

A systems integrator connects those capabilities to orchestration software. The software needs an unambiguous way to describe a requested circuit and to interpret the state that the device reports. Shared data models help both sides attach the same meaning to that information.

An operator requests a connection and checks that the resulting physical state matches the intended service. Hardware capability, software control, and operational evidence must all agree. A working optical component alone does not establish interoperability across an entire system.

Open specifications, reference architectures, and validation work make these handoffs easier to repeat across organizations. Experience from suppliers, integrators, operators, and researchers can then inform the next revision. This is an illustrative workflow, not a particular published OCP interface. The practical opportunity is to contribute clear requirements and evidence at the handoff your team understands best.
