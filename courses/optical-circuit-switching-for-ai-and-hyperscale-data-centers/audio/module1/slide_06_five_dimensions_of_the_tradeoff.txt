A circuit changes several design decisions at once. First, it avoids packet queuing inside the transparent optical switch, while propagation and endpoint delays remain. Second, removing an electrical processing stage can lower power, but the controller, actuation and transceivers still consume energy. Third, packet inspection and quality of service must be provided by systems that can interpret the traffic.

Fourth, a transparent path can support different data rates and protocols within its optical operating limits. Fifth, loss accumulates along the path because a passive optical switch does not regenerate the signal. These properties explain why optical and electrical switching often work together. A good design assigns each function to a component that can perform it, then checks the combined result against the application's needs.
