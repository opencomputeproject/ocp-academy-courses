A data-rate-agnostic optical path does not decode the bit rate to decide where the light travels. That can allow an operator to update electrical endpoints while retaining the optical switching layer. It is an attractive separation of upgrade cycles.

It is not a promise that any future transceiver works with any installed optical path. Engineers still verify supported wavelengths, optical bandwidth, connector and fiber arrangements, insertion loss, and receiver requirements. The same caution applies to protocol transparency: carrying a signal does not supply the protocol's packet services. A useful design question is therefore, what must change at the endpoints, and what evidence supports retaining the optical path? That question turns a broad benefit into a testable upgrade plan.
