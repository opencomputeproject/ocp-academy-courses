# pronunciation: OCS -> O C S; EPS -> E P S; OEO -> O E O; TPUv4 -> T P U version four; GPU -> G P U; GPUs -> G P Yous; ACTINA -> Actina; MoE -> M O E; OCS-BCube -> O C S B Cube; gNMI -> g N M I
Optical circuit switching, or O C S, establishes a selected optical connection between ports. The signal remains light through that switching path. Electrical packet switching, or E P S, instead uses electronic logic to make forwarding decisions about packets. When an optical link enters an electrical switch, a receiver converts the signal to electrical form; transmission onto another optical link converts it back to light. This is optical-electrical-optical, or O E O, conversion.

Avoiding that conversion at a selected network stage can remove its electronic processing and associated power demand. The transmitting and receiving endpoints still need their own electronics. A hybrid network can therefore retain packet switching around an optical circuit layer. The useful comparison is between complete architectures with equivalent requirements, rather than between an isolated optical component and an entire electrical network.
