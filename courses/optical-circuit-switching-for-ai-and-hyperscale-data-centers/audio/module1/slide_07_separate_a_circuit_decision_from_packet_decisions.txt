A controller decides which ports should be connected. That connection can remain useful for a communication phase, a job, or a longer deployment interval. While it is established, the optical path carries the signal without deciding where each individual packet should go.

Suppose several senders need the same receiver at once. A circuit alone does not schedule those competing demands. The design may allocate different connections, wait for a different interval, or use surrounding packet switching and traffic control. This is why circuit setup time and data-transfer latency are different measurements. One describes changing connectivity. The other describes using an established path. Keeping the two separate prevents a fast transfer path from being mistaken for instantaneous reconfiguration.
