The central idea is now in place. Optical circuit switching changes connectivity while keeping the signal in the optical domain through the switch. Its lack of packet buffers removes queuing at that component. It does not remove all sources of delay or contention in a network.

The strongest benefits appear when an architecture can avoid an electrical stage and keep useful circuits established long enough to justify reconfiguration. Optical loss and endpoint compatibility remain practical constraints. Use these boundaries as a checklist whenever you encounter a claim about lower power, faster links, or easier upgrades.
