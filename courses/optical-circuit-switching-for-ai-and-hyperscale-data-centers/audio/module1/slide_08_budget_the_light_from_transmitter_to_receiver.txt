Insertion loss is the reduction in optical power caused by introducing a component into a path. Fibers, connectors and optical switches all contribute to the link budget. The receiver must still receive a usable signal after those losses, with an appropriate operating margin.

The source course emphasizes that practical optical fabrics often avoid long cascades of switches. Treat that as a design implication, not a universal limit of exactly one or two hops. The allowable path depends on the components, wavelength, transceiver capability and complete optical budget. Adding amplification or regeneration changes the architecture and its tradeoffs. Start with the end-to-end path that the application requires, then establish whether every permitted circuit can meet the optical conditions.
