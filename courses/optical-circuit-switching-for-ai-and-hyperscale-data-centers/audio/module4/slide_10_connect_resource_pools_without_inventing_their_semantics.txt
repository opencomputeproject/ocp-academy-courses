Resource disaggregation separates pools of compute, memory, storage or accelerators so systems can compose resources for a task. Configurable optical connectivity can support that composition across a larger physical footprint. Avoiding an electrical switching stage may reduce part of the transport overhead.

An optical path does not, by itself, provide cache coherence, memory access semantics, isolation or a suitable latency guarantee. Those functions depend on endpoints, protocols and software. Dense wavelength division multiplexing, or D W D M, carries multiple wavelength channels on a fiber and can help use its capacity efficiently. It does not remove propagation delay or the optical budget. The useful question is which resource-pooling requirement the optical fabric helps satisfy, and which requirements the remaining system must still meet.
