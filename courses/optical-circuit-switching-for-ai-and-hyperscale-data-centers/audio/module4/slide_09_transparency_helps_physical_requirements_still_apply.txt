Quantum networking uses physical quantum states rather than simply reading and retransmitting classical bits. The no-cloning theorem says that an arbitrary unknown quantum state cannot be copied perfectly. It is not the statement that every measurement always destroys every state. Measurement effects depend on the state and measurement performed.

A conventional packet switch does not transparently forward an encoded photonic qubit through its usual receive, process and retransmit pipeline. An optical circuit can avoid that classical conversion, making it a useful candidate for quantum interconnection. But loss, noise, phase stability and polarization behavior still matter to the encoding. O C S does not automatically preserve any quantum state under all conditions. The quantum network must validate the entire path and its protocol requirements.
