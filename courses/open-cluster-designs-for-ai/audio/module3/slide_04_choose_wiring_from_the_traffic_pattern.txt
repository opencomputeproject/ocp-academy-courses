# pronunciation: AI = quoted A I; xPU = ex pee you; OPG = O P G; XOC = X O C; SONiC = sonic; RoCE = rocky; CRD = C R D; RDMA = R D M A; Clos = Kloh (rhymes with go or slow; silent s)

Watch where the traffic goes. With single-homed Kloh wiring, the scale-out interfaces of a server attach to the same leaf. Traffic between servers on that leaf can remain local. Rail-optimized wiring groups interfaces by accelerator affinity. Within a first-hop rail domain, matching-rail traffic can remain on its leaf, while traffic between rails on different leaves crosses the spine. Both arrangements can use a Kloh fabric. Here, we hold the schematic hardware constant and change the attachment pattern. Neither pattern wins for every workload. The useful question is how much traffic can stay local under the expected communication pattern and placement. A scheduler that knows the first-hop domains can help preserve that locality. The animation shows paths, not measured performance. Real results also depend on the fabric, host configuration, congestion, and workload behavior.
