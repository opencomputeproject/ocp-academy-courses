Count the ports and their rates before assigning a benefit to two planes. One four-hundred-gigabit port provides a nominal four hundred gigabits per second. Two two-hundred-gigabit ports provide the same aggregate nominal rate. Two four-hundred-gigabit ports provide eight hundred in aggregate. In a dual-plane design, each port attaches to a different fabric plane. That separation can support maintenance and failure isolation. It does not automatically double bandwidth. If one of two equal-rate planes is unavailable, half the nominal aggregate port capacity remains. Whether the application continues successfully depends on its networking support, the failure, the remaining capacity, and the operating procedure. Keep those as acceptance questions. Compare the actual port configuration, not the words single-plane and dual-plane by themselves.
