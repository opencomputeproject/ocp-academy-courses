The head end is more than a login server. It provides client access and supports functions such as scheduling, image and container repositories, observability, and supporting storage. Hardware recovery also needs a reachable management path. Their resource requirements depend on the selected applications and operating model. The January architecture provides numerical examples, while explicitly asking designers to verify them for the target workload. Treat availability requirements by function. Two instances in a drawing are not proof that their power, network paths, state, or recovery procedures are independent. Identify the service owner and ask how capacity, failover, and restoration will be demonstrated. That makes the head end part of the design review, rather than equipment added after the compute order.
