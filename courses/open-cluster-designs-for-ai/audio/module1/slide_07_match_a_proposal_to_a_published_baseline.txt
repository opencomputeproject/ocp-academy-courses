Consider a proposal for liquid-cooled rack-scale compute that cites the January air-cooled server design. Before accepting its equipment list, identify what changed. The compute domain may now span a rack. The number and placement of network interfaces may differ. So can power, cooling, cable reach, storage attachment, and the management inventory. The April fabric references do discuss both air and liquid cooling, but their stated compute unit is still an eight-accelerator server with scale-up inside the enclosure. A cooling-independent logical fabric is not a complete qualification of a different compute form factor. Pause and identify the assumptions you would put on the review list. Then keep the references that still apply and ask for evidence at the changed boundaries.
