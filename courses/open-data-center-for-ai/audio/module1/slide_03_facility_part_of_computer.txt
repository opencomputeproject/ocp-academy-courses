Start with the rack in the foreground. A different accelerator system arrives. Its power demand, liquid flow, weight, or maintenance needs can differ even when the nominal rack width looks familiar.

Follow the electrical path upward to the row busways. More distribution hardware still needs adequate upstream supply. Now follow the liquid supply and return. The row manifold and each branch must support the equipment's flow and operating conditions. Finally, look at the aisle and overhead space. A system that connects can still be impossible to move or maintain.

As the view opens to the hall, these are no longer isolated rack decisions. They affect shared electrical and thermal infrastructure and ultimately the utility connection. This is the whitepaper's system-level idea: the facility becomes part of the computer. Early interface planning reduces late surprises, but every candidate system still needs verification.
