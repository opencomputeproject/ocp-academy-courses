## Title

Open Data Center for AI: Applying Specification 0.7

## Short description

Design for heavier racks, higher density, and future suppliers—without expensive facility redesign.

## Long description

AI infrastructure has made the facility part of the computing system. A rack that cannot traverse the building, land within structural limits, reach compatible power and cooling, or adapt to a future supplier becomes an expensive redesign—regardless of its compute performance.

Open Data Center for AI: Applying Specification 0.7 turns the specification into a practical facility-review lens. Learners discover which constraints can block deployment before equipment reaches the row, how to apply density and capacity figures at the correct facility level, why a full-row manifold and a rack connector are separate design decisions, and how standardized interfaces preserve implementation and supplier choice.

The course uses the [published Revision 0.7 specification, effective August 27, 2026](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/open-data-center-spec-revision-0-7-0-4-pdf), supported by the Open Data Center for AI Strategic Initiative whitepaper and OCP EMEA Summit panel. It reflects expanded collaboration from AMD, Google, Meta, and NVIDIA. A requirements ledger preserves the source qualifications, while a printable compatibility-review worksheet turns the lessons into a practical evidence record.

### Module 1 — Why Open Data Center for AI, Why Now

Approximately 11 minutes, including 6 minutes 4 seconds of narration.

See why next-generation AI systems make the facility part of the computer. Trace the specification's evolution from the earlier Google-authored 0.5 baseline to wider collaboration, understand fungible infrastructure, and connect the initiative’s five workstreams. Practice deciding which facility boundaries can be established before the accelerator supplier is selected—and which candidate-specific evidence still matters.

### Module 2 — The Facility Compatibility Envelope

Approximately 10 minutes, including 5 minutes 2 seconds of narration.

Identify physical conditions that can stop deployment before a rack reaches the row. Follow rack-and-sidecar loading through dock, doorway, turn and lift checks; keep dimension targets attached to their measurement datums; and distinguish a two-busway floor-to-beam minimum from a ceiling-finish height. Use an illustrative row-length calculation to see why linear density and actual equipment demand must be checked separately.

### Module 3 — Power and Cooling at AI Density

Approximately 12 minutes, including 5 minutes 52 seconds of narration.

Interpret density and capacity at cluster, hall, row and rack levels. Separate the hall’s four-power-block minimum from undefined block ratings and row busway choices. Distinguish blended air/liquid capabilities from the local peak split. Work through a 300 kW screening case in which the same two-inch path passes at one flow ratio and fails at another, then separate full-row manifold capacity from individual rack connections.

### Module 4 — Apply 0.7 Without Freezing Innovation

Approximately 12 minutes, including 5 minutes 3 seconds of narration.

Separate requirements from Appendix reference concepts and compare one design choice at a time: fanwall versus in-row cooling, then two versus four busways. Examine the evidence needed for different rack implementations without assuming universal connectors or telemetry standards. Finish with a filled constrained-ceiling example and a reusable worksheet that records the source, site condition, proposed variation, evidence, accountable owners and unresolved decisions.

## Learner payoff

By the end of the course, learners can spot costly incompatibilities earlier, challenge capacity numbers that are being applied at the wrong level, avoid turning reference concepts into accidental requirements, and use a shared interface model to review multi-supplier designs without freezing innovation.
