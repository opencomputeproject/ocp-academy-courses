By the end of this module, you'll be able to trace an H B F access from the X P U to the Nand stack.

You'll identify the Base die, the Core dies, and the through-silicon-via channels that join them. You'll explain why H B F uses sixteen independent host channels rather than one monolithic interface.

You'll also describe the U C I E path. That includes the streaming protocol, the mainband and sideband functions, and the AXI link layer that carries read and write transactions.

Then you'll connect protocol behavior to Nand geometry. Reads use aligned transfers from sixty-four bytes through four kibibytes. Writes assemble a complete four-kibibyte device logical unit.

Finally, you'll explain how global addresses become channel-local addresses, and why interleaving across AXI ports and host channels is necessary for maximum bandwidth. The goal is to see one complete system, not a disconnected stack of interface acronyms.
