High bandwidth doesn't remove the lifecycle characteristics of Nand. Reliable H B F operation is a contract between the device and host software.

The reliability table defines a ten-year life span or an endurance indicator of one hundred percent, whichever comes first. Actual endurance depends on the workload and product. The Base die exposes maximum and average program-and-erase counts so the host can estimate remaining life and adjust placement.

Data health also requires refresh. The reliability table guarantees twenty-four hours of power-on retention at 85 degrees Celsius. The software guide recommends periodic refresh at product-defined intervals, typically every twenty-four to forty-eight hours. Read disturb may require earlier action. The host can inspect read counts and respond when the Base die reports increased risk.

Error status turns those risks into actions. Depending on the code, software may retry a read, refresh a block, replay writes from a known copy, wait for temporary recovery, or retire unusable capacity. Durable copies therefore remain important.

Temperature adds another state machine. H B F operates from zero through 105 degrees Celsius at the junction. It moves through normal, light-throttling, severe-throttling, and shutdown modes. Severe throttling stops normal input and output while maintenance access remains available.

Security is optional and product-specific. The architecture defines power-on erase and sanitize mechanisms, but high-bandwidth inline protection isn't assumed. H B F is difficult to remove from the package, yet physical integration alone isn't a complete data-at-rest policy.

For permanent storage, use persistent devices such as S S Ds. Treat H B F as a managed local tier with refresh, recovery, and a durable source of truth. That is the host-device contract.
