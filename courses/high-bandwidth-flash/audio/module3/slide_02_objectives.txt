By the end of this module, you'll be able to evaluate an H B F data-placement strategy, not just describe the hardware.

You'll explain why H B M and H B F use separate memory management, and how software works with sixteen independent local address spaces. You'll distinguish the easy path for parallel reads from the stricter rules for page-oriented, sequential writes.

You'll identify the responsibilities shared between the Base die and host software. Those include wear monitoring, zone remapping, refresh scheduling, error recovery, thermal response, and optional data-at-rest controls.

You'll compare two serving patterns. Full-channel interleaving maximizes bandwidth for a model. Dedicated channel groups trade some aggregate bandwidth for isolation and controlled capacity allocation.

Finally, you'll choose different placement policies for model weights and K V cache. That decision balances performance, endurance, and capacity utilization. These are the choices that determine whether the architecture's theoretical bandwidth becomes useful application behavior.
