H B F software manages sixteen independent local spaces alongside, not inside, the H B M memory pool. A layout that activates many channels can reach high read throughput. A layout concentrated in one channel cannot.

Reads can proceed broadly and complete out of order. Writes require complete four-kibibyte units, sequential progress inside each Nand block, and explicit handling of failures and replay. Wear leveling, refresh, capacity retirement, and thermal response all involve the host.

A I placement then builds on those rules. One model can stripe across all channels for bandwidth. Multiple models can share that pattern or use dedicated channel groups for isolation. Weights favor read-dominant interleaving. K V cache introduces ongoing writes and may need its own partition.

H B F is therefore more than a device specification. It is a hardware-software contract. The system gains a new tier only when interface behavior, data placement, lifecycle management, and durable storage work together.
