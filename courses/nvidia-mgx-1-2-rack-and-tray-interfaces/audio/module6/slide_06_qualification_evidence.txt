Qualification becomes more useful when each requirement has an owner and an acceptance condition. The contribution requires suppliers to conduct a comprehensive test plan to ensure specifications and reliability over the product lifetime, and calls for a full report for evaluation and approval. The listed minimum busbar tests support that broader responsibility; they are not a complete substitute for it.

Manufacturing evidence applies to actual assemblies. It includes the specified liquid and electrical checks, appropriate tested conductor pairs, and traceability labels. Keep the grounding variant and unit revision linked to the results.

Rack integration evidence covers the completed configuration. It includes maximum-load thermal behavior and the support or loading evidence for the actual interface arrangement. A component report may support this review, but it does not automatically establish the assembled rack's performance.

Material and safety evidence also needs the correct scope. Review the actual coolant and wetted surfaces, the required fluid documentation, and the compliance requirements referenced by this contribution. The course preserves the standards and editions at their recorded scope. A deployment's certification review must establish the requirements applicable to its actual product and jurisdiction.

An acceptance matrix should therefore identify the requirement, source page or drawing, configuration, evidence owner, test conditions, result, and unresolved item. If the implementation changes, reassess applicability rather than merely retaining the old passed stamp.

This structure helps distinguish missing evidence from a genuine requirement failure. It also makes open assumptions visible before an engineering claim is accepted.
