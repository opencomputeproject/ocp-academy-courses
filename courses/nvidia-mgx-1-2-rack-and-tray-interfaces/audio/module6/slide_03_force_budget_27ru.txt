A force budget needs one defined case. The twenty-seven-rack-unit source drawings distinguish switch and compute trays, and mating from unmating. The force tables are labeled in pounds. Keep that case identity and unit attached to every value you use.

Take the switch maximum-mating row in Figure eighty-one as an illustrative exercise. It lists two universal quick-disconnect contributions of eighty-two pounds each, four interconnect contributions of one hundred two point three pounds each, and a busbar contribution of twenty-two point five pounds.

If all those listed maxima are assumed to act together, the quick-disconnect subtotal is one hundred sixty-four pounds. The interconnect subtotal is four hundred nine point two. Adding the busbar contribution produces five hundred ninety-five point seven pounds. This is derived arithmetic under an explicitly stated simultaneous-loading assumption.

It is not a measured tray insertion force, a frame capacity, or proof that a particular support arrangement is adequate. Real engagement depends on the chosen interfaces, their states and sequence, the assembly, and any additional forces relevant to that implementation.

The source support details show alignment and mounting features around the cartridge, including the top and bottom positioning arrangements and reinforcement structure. Trace the force locations into those supports. Then identify the structural evidence needed for the actual configuration.

The review separates two conclusions: what a defined example's listed contributions add to under your assumption, and what the manufactured system has demonstrated. Keep both conclusions visible so illustrative arithmetic cannot be mistaken for validation.
