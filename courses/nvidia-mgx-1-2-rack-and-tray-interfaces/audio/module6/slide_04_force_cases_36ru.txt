The thirty-six-rack-unit examples use newtons, while the twenty-seven-rack-unit diagrams use pounds. A comparison must preserve both the unit and the example. Converting units alone does not make two connector populations or support arrangements equivalent.

The switch maximum-mating row in Figure eighty-three lists two quick-disconnect contributions of three hundred sixty-five newtons, four interconnect contributions of ninety newtons, and one busbar contribution of one hundred newtons. Under the same illustrative simultaneous-loading assumption, the subtotal is one thousand one hundred ninety newtons.

The thirty-six-rack-unit compute case in Figure eighty-four uses nineteen newtons for each of the four interconnect contributions. With the same listed quick-disconnect and busbar maxima, its derived subtotal is nine hundred six newtons. The difference follows from the documented case, not just the cartridge height.

The twenty-seven-rack-unit compute maximum row likewise differs from its switch row. Its corresponding derived subtotal is four hundred thirty-three point seven pounds. All four subtotals remain illustrative arithmetic, with the same stated assumption about the listed maxima acting together.

Review the actual mounting and alignment drawings with each case. A changed connector, guide, bracket, cartridge height, or support spacing may change which evidence applies. Don't select a lower subtotal from one case and a structural conclusion from another without justification.

The engineering record should identify the source case, units, interface population, loading assumption, and implementation-specific support evidence. That creates a traceable comparison instead of a table of apparently interchangeable numbers.
