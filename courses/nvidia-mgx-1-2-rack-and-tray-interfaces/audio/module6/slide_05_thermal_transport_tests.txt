A rack-level thermal test and a packaged transport test establish different kinds of evidence. Keep their setups and acceptance conditions separate.

During rack integration, the contribution calls for a full rack operating at maximum load, with enough time to reach thermal steady state. Thermocouples are placed on the outer surface at the maximum touch-temperature hotspot and internally near the hottest busbar clip region. Those locations address different temperature limits. A measurement at a convenient cool surface would not answer either question reliably.

The packaged L eleven requirements address rack-and-roll deployment, with the option for liquid-charged systems. The listed vibration requirement includes two hours at one to two hundred hertz and zero point five four G R M S, with no top load. Another two-hour air-vibration requirement references the stated A S T M method.

The contribution also lists a six-inch rotational-drop condition, four incline impacts at four feet per second, and a twenty-two-degree tip test in each direction. The detailed methods must be read with the referenced documents and the actual packaged implementation. This course identifies the required evidence; it does not provide a lifting or live-test procedure.

For acceptance, identify which configuration was tested, how it was instrumented or packaged, and which source conditions were evaluated. Separate operating temperature, shipment conditions, and handling claims. A common rack family or an earlier test report is useful only when its configuration and conditions apply to the system being accepted.
