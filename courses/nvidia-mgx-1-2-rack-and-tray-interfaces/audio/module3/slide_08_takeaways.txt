The bay review begins with allocation. A name tells you the occupied rows, depth class, chassis positions, and type. Compare that allocation with the actual chassis before assuming that a wider or taller module is permitted.

Required widths include the combination boundary, and the suggested contents remain examples. Short-to-long compatibility is asymmetric. The Type C power-supply-optimized short variant has its own depth and must be identified explicitly.

Retention adds another interface. Read the latch location from the bay datum and check any protrusion in the completed system. The fanless Type B and E examples then add liquid and electrical connection dimensions that cannot be replaced by generic depth values.

You should now be able to decode one multi-row bay name, compare two populations, and identify the exact location, depth, retention, or connection requirement that makes a proposed choice valid or incompatible.
