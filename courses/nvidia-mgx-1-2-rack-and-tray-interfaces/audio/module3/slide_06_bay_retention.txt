A bay that fits the available volume still needs a compatible retention interface. The contribution identifies two approaches. A single spring latch and catch can retain the bay internally. An external camming latch handle provides another method. The chassis may support a short bay, a long bay, or both by providing the applicable positions.

Read the retention feature from the bay datum. The spring-latch location and the cantilever or camming arrangement have their own dimensional relationships. A module depth comparison that omits these locations does not establish that the latch will retain the assembly.

The datum surface also separates the specified envelope from geometry that may extend outside it. For example, some expansion-card form factors include a feature that projects beyond the datum surface. The contribution makes the system designer responsible for that protrusion. It must be reviewed against the actual chassis and adjacent modules.

For a substitution, keep four records connected. Identify the bay envelope. Locate the retention feature from the specified datum. Check the matching chassis catch or latch position. Then review any allowed or required protrusion in the completed system.

The two retention approaches should not be treated as physically identical just because both secure a module. Their interface geometry and the supporting chassis design establish which one applies. A clear review explains how the module is located and retained, which dimensions control that relationship, and which supplier or assembly evidence confirms the final engagement.
