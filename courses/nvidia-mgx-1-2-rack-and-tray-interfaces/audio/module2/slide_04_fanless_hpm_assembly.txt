The fanless examples show two ways to integrate the same broad functions. The first has two host processor modules and an integrated cooling and input and output zone. That zone replaces the fan region. Its manifold and midplane assembly distribute liquid through blind-mate M Q D connections and distribute input and output or management signals between modules and host processor modules.

The full-size example makes different choices. The chassis features used for rear installation of two micro M G X assemblies are removed, allowing the full-size module to install on M G X T-pin locations. The bay modules connect through coplanar connections rather than the separate midplane arrangement. Liquid distribution is integrated into the host processor module itself.

The supplied three-dimensional assembly gives a useful view of that integration. Separating its geometry reveals the board context, cooling structures, manifold, and connector regions. The separation is an instructional exploded view, not an approved disassembly sequence or a statement about operating joints. The source geometry is preserved; the display positions are intentionally expanded.

Use the comparison to identify interface ownership. In the dual-module example, a discrete manifold and midplane assembly carries important distribution functions. In the full-size example, some of those functions sit within the larger module. The location of a function determines which assembly changes when a different module is selected.

Before accepting a configuration, identify the actual chassis mounting details, bay interfaces, and liquid-distribution arrangement. Common family terminology does not make the two internal implementations identical.
