Electrical acceptance requires the test condition, the connection topology, and the result. A generic passed label cannot establish that the published requirement was evaluated in the intended way.

The voltage-drop requirement is less than zero point two five volts at the busbar current rating. The source is placed at the top and the loopback at the bottom. The measurement includes the total send and return path. A measurement across a shorter segment or only one conductor would answer a different question.

A dielectric withstand test, often called high-pot, evaluates the electrical insulation under an applied test voltage. The manufacturing requirement applies to one hundred percent of busbar assemblies. It specifies a minimum of one point two kilovolts, a ten-second ramp, a thirty-second hold, and maximum leakage of ten microamperes.

Read the tested pairs and the exception. Power-to-return is identified separately from power-to-chassis and return-to-chassis. The latter conductor-to-chassis statement is not applicable to the hard-grounded version. Record the actual grounding variant when interpreting the test evidence.

The reliability requirement includes fifty mating and unmating cycles with the specified connector without wearing out the plating. Units also carry part number and revision, date code, and a high-pot stamp. Those records connect a manufactured unit to the reviewed configuration.

The exercise is to compare the evidence for isolated-return and bonded-return variants. Identify which test-applicability statement changes, and preserve the configuration alongside the results. This is a requirement interpretation, not a live test procedure.
