An insulating layer and a thermal-transfer layer serve different purposes. The reference design shows G T S three four eight one five one H T S for insulation and T G P three five zero zero for the thermal layer. The latter starts at one point five millimeters and is compressed to one millimeter.

The fastening arrangement must achieve the required compression while retaining the components. It must also avoid removing copper in the high-power connection positions. A nominal layer thickness in an exploded model is therefore not the finished condition. The assembled fastening and compression evidence matters.

The thermal requirements include limits for surfaces accessible to fingers and hands. Internal conductors must remain below the relevant busbar clip temperature rating under the system's worst-case thermal conditions. Clip suitability must be evaluated for the actual system. Venting and perforation geometry have their own structural and access constraints.

The busbar power-map discussion states that cooling should keep the maximum temperature at one hundred five degrees Celsius or below. The displayed dissipation map is an example configuration, not a prediction for every rack. Do not replace a clip-specific limit or a touch limit with that example number.

The section reveal separates insulation, thermal transfer, and cooling regions so their relationships can be followed. The heat indicators explain the path schematically; they are not a measured temperature map. A useful acceptance record states the selected layers, their assembled compression, the electrical isolation evidence, and the temperatures measured at the relevant surfaces and interfaces.
