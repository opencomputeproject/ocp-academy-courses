The slide rail does more than carry the tray during service. It is part of the mechanism that positions the tray for interconnect and liquid engagement. A change to the slide interface can therefore affect both mechanical support and rear mating alignment.

The contribution establishes preferred T-pin locations referenced from chassis Datum A. Using those locations enables a common slide rail interface across the ecosystem. A variance requires a unique rail design, even when the tray's outside dimensions look familiar.

The blind-mate slide features support and align the tray to the interconnect cartridge and the rack manifolds. The specified latching surface enables latch designs for blind-mate liquid implementations and interoperability between the slide and chassis. Treat the rail, T-pins, chassis, latch, and rear interfaces as related features. Checking each part in isolation misses the relationship that establishes the final position.

Capacity is a separate question. The specification directs the implementer to ensure that the particular supplier implementation safely supports the weight of the compute or switch tray being installed. The rack's overall payload figure cannot be substituted for this rail-level evidence.

During a substitution review, retain the chassis reference, compare T-pin positions and geometry, verify the alignment and latching features, and obtain the relevant load capacity. Document which features are common and which depend on the selected supplier. A standard mounting pattern reduces unnecessary variation, but it doesn't remove the need to validate the complete supported and engaged assembly.
