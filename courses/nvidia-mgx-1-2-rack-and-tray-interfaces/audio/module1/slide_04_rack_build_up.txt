The rack additions are easiest to understand as a sequence of interface needs. Begin with the Open Rack version three base structure. M G X adapts it to support nineteen-inch E I A equipment on a one-rack-unit pitch. The front region also provides space for input and output cabling, management connectivity, latching, and slide components. That space serves a mechanical and service purpose as well as a wiring purpose.

The rear extender is an optional addition that increases rack depth to twelve hundred millimeters. It protects rear components such as cable bracing and manifold fittings, while maintaining the existing rear hole pattern for doors or seismic bracing as needed. A drawing that includes the extender should not turn this optional feature into a universal requirement.

The supports and stiffeners establish locations for the rack manifolds and interconnect cartridge. The manifolds provide the rack-side liquid connections. The cartridge region provides a controlled volume and mounting structure for a selected interconnect implementation.

The rack supports an I T payload up to four thousand pounds. That is a rack-level capacity statement. It does not establish the rating of an individual slide, bracket, or connector support. Each of those still needs evidence for its actual use.

Treat the developing assembly as a reference chain: base structure, mounting features, supports, mating interfaces, and the installed tray. When a proposed component changes one link, review the affected dimensions and loads. The engineering question is which interfaces remain controlled after the change, and what evidence demonstrates that control.
