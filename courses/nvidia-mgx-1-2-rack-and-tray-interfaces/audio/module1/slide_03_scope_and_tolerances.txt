A compatibility review starts by locating the requirement, its reference, and its applicability. M G X version one point two defines common rack and tray interfaces for power, liquid, mounting, and retention. It also defines space and mounting for tray-to-tray interconnects. It does not prescribe every connector or the electrical architecture of every fabric. Power shelves, top-of-rack and management switches, cooling distribution units, and input and output or power cabling design are outside this contribution's scope.

The power rack reference design presented by Jared Huntington at the twenty twenty-six O C P Global Summit supplies useful system context. Its eight-hundred-volt distribution link and power converters sit around the rack and tray interfaces reviewed here. We will keep that implementation example distinct from the requirements of this contribution.

The version boundary matters. The specification describes one point two as derived from, but not fully compatible with, M G X one point one. A familiar family name is therefore not evidence that an earlier part is interchangeable.

A datum is the reference used to establish a feature's location or orientation. Blind mating means the interfaces engage without direct manual access to the mating connection. That makes the complete reference chain important, rather than just the nominal position of one connector.

Table one gives default tolerance categories. Hole center to hole center is plus or minus zero point one two seven millimeters. Hole center to edge is plus or minus zero point two five four. Edge to edge returns to zero point one two seven. Feature size is plus or minus zero point one zero zero millimeters. Board thickness is plus or minus ten percent of nominal.

For a hole-to-edge check, use the hole-to-edge category unless the actual dimension specifies something else. Record the controlling drawing and any separately specified tolerance before combining dimensions into an illustrative stack-up.
