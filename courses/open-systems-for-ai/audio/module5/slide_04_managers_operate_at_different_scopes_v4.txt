# pronunciation: BMC -> "B M C"

Management occurs at several scopes. A node-level controller observes and controls functions within a system. A rack-level manager can coordinate information and supported actions across equipment. Cluster and facility systems bring additional context, policies and responsibilities.

A baseboard management controller, or B M C, is a common way to implement local hardware management. The exact architecture varies, and some equipment exposes management functions through other arrangements. Define the actual boundaries rather than assuming every device contains the same controller.

Workload scheduling and orchestration decide where and how applications run. Hardware management reports equipment conditions and exposes supported control functions. A scheduler may use that information to avoid unhealthy equipment, while a management controller may execute a supported equipment action. The interaction needs a clear contract.

For each scope, ask what is observed, which decisions are made, which actions are permitted and who owns failures. Include the power, network and software dependencies of the manager itself. A hierarchy diagram is useful only when it also tells operators where responsibility sits and how an unresolved event is escalated.
