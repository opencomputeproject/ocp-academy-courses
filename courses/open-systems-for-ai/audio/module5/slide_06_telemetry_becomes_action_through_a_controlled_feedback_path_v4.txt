Follow a cooling anomaly through the management system. A sensor reports an unexpected condition. The first task is to establish what was observed, when it was observed, and whether the data can be trusted. Missing or stale telemetry can change the decision.

The management path carries that observation to a system with the relevant policy and authority. The policy defines which conditions call for an action, what limits apply and when a person or another system must be involved. A shared interface does not create that policy automatically.

An authorized action then follows the supported control path. Depending on the design, the response might involve equipment controls, a workload change or escalation to an operator. The course does not prescribe one response for every cooling system; the implemented control design defines what is appropriate.

Finally, verify the result. Did the command succeed? Did the measured condition recover? If the action failed or the data remains uncertain, the response needs a defined next step. Close the loop with observation, decision, action and verification. Reliable automation depends on the complete path, including the behavior when one of those stages is unavailable.
