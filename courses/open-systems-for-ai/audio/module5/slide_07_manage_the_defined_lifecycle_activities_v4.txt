Management supports a sequence of lifecycle activities. Deployment brings equipment into its intended environment and establishes the initial conditions. Composition, where the architecture supports it, combines resources into the required arrangement. Provisioning prepares the resources and services for use.

Configuration sets supported operating parameters. Operation then monitors the system, handles changes and responds to events. These activities are related, but they should not be treated as one undifferentiated automation step. Each has prerequisites, permissions and evidence of success.

Keep commissioning, repair and retirement visible around that sequence. A resource that can be provisioned still needs a verified physical and operational handoff. A failed component needs an authorized service process. Retiring equipment can require data handling, access removal and updates to asset records.

For our changed accelerator tray, ask how the replacement is discovered, checked, configured and returned to service. Confirm how its firmware and capabilities become visible to the relevant tools. The goal is a lifecycle path that people can understand and test, with clear ownership when a stage cannot complete.
