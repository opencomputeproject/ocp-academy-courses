# pronunciation: CDU -> "C D U"; IT -> "I T"

A coolant distribution unit, or C D U, connects cooling responsibilities. In a liquid-to-liquid arrangement, it transfers heat between the technology cooling system serving the I T equipment and a facility water system. A heat exchanger separates the two fluid circuits, so the fluids do not need to mix.

Follow the technology side first. Coolant supplies the equipment, takes up heat, and returns toward the C D U. A pump and controls support the intended conditions. The facility side carries that transferred heat onward to the rest of the site's cooling and rejection system.

The boundary needs agreement on temperatures, flow, pressures, fluid chemistry, materials and control behavior. A nominal cooling-capacity number is not enough. Ask which conditions establish that capacity, what happens during a disturbance, and which team responds to an alarm.

Liquid-to-air equipment uses a different rejection arrangement, transferring heat to air rather than a facility liquid circuit at that boundary. It still requires a destination for the heat and enough supporting air-side capacity. Whichever architecture is selected, draw the complete path and name the owner of each handoff. The heat does not disappear when it leaves the chip.
