Choose the cooling arrangement and the fluid's phase behavior as separate decisions. Cold-plate cooling keeps fluid within a contained path attached to selected components. Immersion places suitable equipment in a dielectric fluid bath. Each arrangement creates different equipment, material and service requirements.

Single-phase operation keeps the working fluid liquid as it transports heat. Two-phase operation uses evaporation and condensation as part of that process. These phase descriptions can apply to different cooling arrangements; they are not simply alternate names for cold plates and immersion.

Compare implementations using the actual system boundary. Ask which components are cooled, which materials contact the fluid, how heat reaches the facility, and how equipment is installed and serviced. Performance gains require a defined comparison and operating conditions. A fluid bath alone does not establish zero water use for the entire facility.

Research into microstructures, advanced cold plates and higher heat-flux designs expands the available options. Keep research results distinct from qualified equipment that can be procured for a deployment.
