# pronunciation: AI -> "A I"

Power density changes more than the number on a rack label. A higher electrical load increases the demand on distribution, conversion, protection and heat removal. It can also change the space available for cables, cooling equipment and maintenance.

Keep chip, tray, rack, row and facility quantities separate. A chip power figure is not a rack rating. A rack rating is not a guarantee that every row position can support it. Adding racks can change the diversity assumptions and redundancy arrangements used at a larger boundary.

Roadmaps toward higher rack power are useful planning context. They help the community identify interfaces and infrastructure that need development. A roadmap should not be presented as a requirement that every A I deployment must meet, or as proof that existing equipment supports it.

For our running deployment example, ask the supplier for the selected configuration's load assumptions. Ask the facility team how that load fits normal operation, maintenance and fault conditions. Then confirm the heat-removal and service implications. Density is useful when the complete supporting system can sustain it, rather than when one enclosure can physically hold more equipment.
