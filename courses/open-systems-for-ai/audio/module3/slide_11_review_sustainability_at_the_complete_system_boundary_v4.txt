Sustainability needs a complete system boundary. An improvement near the chip may change energy use elsewhere. Moving heat into liquid can enable different rejection temperatures or reuse opportunities, but the complete cooling plant determines the facility result.

Review energy, water where relevant, materials and operating life together. Include conversion and pumping, remaining air cooling, maintenance and the equipment needed to support the chosen arrangement. A useful comparison states the workload, the environment and what is included in the measurement.

Repair and reuse matter too. A modular design can make replacement easier, but the service process, compatible parts and documentation determine whether that benefit is realized. A total-cost comparison should include the assumptions behind operating, maintenance and replacement costs.

The O C P community explores these questions through research, reference designs and implementation work. Use the A I Open Data Center Summit track for current examples, with the status and boundary of each example kept visible. For your deployment review, ask how the proposed system performs throughout its lifecycle and what evidence supports the comparison.
