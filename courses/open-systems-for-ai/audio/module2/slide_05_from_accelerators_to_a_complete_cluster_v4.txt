# pronunciation: HBM -> "H B M"; xPU -> "X P U"; AI -> "A I"

Start at one accelerator. It has processing resources, nearby memory and interfaces for exchanging data. A compute tray combines accelerators with host processing, local connections and other supporting components. A node is a system boundary whose exact packaging depends on the architecture.

Racks provide a physical enclosure for those nodes and their shared infrastructure. A pod groups equipment for a particular architectural purpose, often around a tightly connected accelerator domain. A cluster combines these resources with the communication, storage, management and software needed to execute workloads.

For this course, we use X P U as a generic name for an A I accelerator. High-bandwidth memory, or H B M, refers to memory closely associated with the accelerator. Neither term tells you how many devices must fit in a rack or which communication semantics the system supports.

Keep physical and logical boundaries separate. A communication domain may extend across several racks. A rack may hold equipment serving different domains. The application may divide its work differently again. When someone says pod or node, ask which boundary they mean and what functions they include. Clear definitions prevent mistakes in capacity, cabling and responsibility.
