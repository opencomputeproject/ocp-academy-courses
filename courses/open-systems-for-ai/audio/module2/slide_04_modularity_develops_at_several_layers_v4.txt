# pronunciation: DC-MHS -> "D C M H S"; OAC -> "oak"; MGX -> "M G X"; AI -> "A I"

Modularity appears at several layers of an A I system. Rack work defines structures and shared infrastructure. Modular server work separates functions within a system. Rack-and-tray interfaces describe how equipment connects to supporting services. Each contribution has a particular boundary.

Open Rack, D C M H S and M G X illustrate different parts of that picture. Earlier accelerator-module work also helped establish the value of common building blocks. Treat those historical examples as background rather than assuming they represent the current direction of every workstream.

One current example is the Open Adapter Card, or oak. It addresses the space between conventional adapter cards and complete rack-scale systems, with relevance to enterprise and edge A I. The workgroup is developing its specifications, so its current material should be read with its stated review status.

These examples are not interchangeable product families. Their value here is showing how an open contribution can define a useful integration boundary. Before choosing equipment, identify the exact specification, revision, supported options and qualification evidence for that boundary. A familiar platform name is a starting point for investigation, not a compatibility guarantee.
