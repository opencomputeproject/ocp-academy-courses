Adding equipment expands the system only when the supporting interfaces can expand with it. The first limit may be electrical power. It may instead be heat removal, link reach, cable density, physical access or the capacity of the management system.

Consider a team that has enough floor space for another rack. That space does not establish that the distribution system can supply its load, that the cooling path can remove its heat, or that the intended network topology can reach it with the required performance. Each boundary has its own evidence.

Separate three kinds of information. An illustrative architecture shows how a system might be arranged. A supplier rating states a capability under specified conditions. A measured workload result shows what a particular configuration delivered. Those statements answer different questions and should not be substituted for each other.

Use expansion reviews to expose the next constraint before procurement. Check normal operation, maintenance and fault conditions. If one path is removed for service, the remaining system still has to behave as intended. Useful capacity is the capacity the complete deployment can support, operate and maintain.
