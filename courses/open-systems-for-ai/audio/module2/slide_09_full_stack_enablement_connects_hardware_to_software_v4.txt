Hardware interfaces are only one layer of a working cluster. Firmware initializes and exposes equipment capabilities. Management services report state and support controlled actions. Drivers and communication libraries make hardware usable by software. Scheduling and orchestration place and coordinate the work.

Reliability support connects these layers during normal operation and failure. A fault must be detected, described and handled by the components responsible for it. A recovery action may need coordination between hardware management and workload software, even though those are different responsibilities.

Open interfaces can reduce integration work across the stack. They do not automatically produce one software abstraction that hides every difference among accelerators and systems. Supported functions, performance behavior and recovery procedures still depend on the implementation.

Ask which layers are available for the proposed configuration, which versions have been tested together, and who supports the complete combination. Include installation, update and recovery procedures in that discussion. A design becomes deployable when the software and operational path are as explicit as the physical hardware path.
