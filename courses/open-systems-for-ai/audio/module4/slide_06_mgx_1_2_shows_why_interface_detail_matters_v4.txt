# pronunciation: MGX -> "M G X"

M G X version one point two provides a current example of why interface detail matters. At an introductory level, follow four boundaries: mechanical fit, electrical delivery, liquid connections and management integration. Each boundary connects equipment to the supporting rack system.

Mechanical compatibility includes more than fitting an outline. Alignment, insertion, retention and service access matter. Electrical compatibility includes the intended delivery and connection arrangement. Cooling compatibility includes the actual fluid interfaces and operating conditions. Management integration defines the supported connections and responsibilities needed to observe and control the equipment.

These boundaries have to agree together. A tray can fit mechanically and still be incompatible electrically or thermally. Supporting one option in a specification does not mean a product supports every option. Shared interface language makes those differences easier to identify; it does not remove them.

The Academy course on M G X rack and tray interfaces provides the detailed drawings, interface information and qualification discussion. Use that course when you need to inspect the implementation requirements. Here, apply the four-boundary review to our proposed tray change and ask for evidence at every connection before the configuration is accepted.
