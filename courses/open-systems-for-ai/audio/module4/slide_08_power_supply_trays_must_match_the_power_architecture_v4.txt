Power supply trays connect the rack's electrical architecture to the load. Depending on the design, conversion, distribution, backup and monitoring functions may sit in different places. Locate each responsibility before assuming what a particular tray must provide.

Begin with the load's requirements. Follow the input path through the selected conversion and distribution arrangement to the equipment. Then follow the management path that reports state and supports the intended control actions. The physical power path and its supervision need to agree.

Efficiency and redundancy depend on conditions. Ask at which operating point efficiency is stated, how sharing or redundancy works, and what happens when a component is removed or fails. A rated output value does not answer all of those questions.

An adjacent sidecar can change where conversion and backup equipment sit, releasing space elsewhere. It does not remove the need to qualify the connections, cooling, protection and service process. In the integration review, make the normal path and the unavailable-component case explicit. The goal is a supported power architecture, with clear evidence for both delivery and response.
