A switch tray provides communication capabilities between other parts of the system. Its job depends on the domain it serves. A scale-up switch connects resources intended for tightly coupled accelerator communication. A scale-out switch supports communication across a larger collection of nodes or domains.

Review the required bandwidth, latency, reach and port roles. The number of ports alone does not describe useful performance. Traffic patterns, congestion handling, cabling and software support all affect how a workload uses the network.

Keep technology boundaries explicit. A familiar physical connector or link technology does not establish identical communication semantics. Supported protocols, routing behavior and implementation options must agree with the selected architecture. The same care applies when equipment from different suppliers is combined.

Resilience also needs evidence. Redundant connections, switches and paths help only when the topology and software use them correctly. Verify what happens during a link failure or maintenance operation, and how the management system reports it. A switch-tray review should describe the communication service the workload needs, the physical implementation that provides it, and the behavior expected when a component is unavailable.
