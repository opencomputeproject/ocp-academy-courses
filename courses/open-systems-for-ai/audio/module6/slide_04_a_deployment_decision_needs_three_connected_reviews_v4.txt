# pronunciation: AI -> "A I"; IT -> "I T"

Consider a fictional proposal to expand a dense A I cluster. The team has selected accelerator trays and switching equipment, and the supplier reports attractive component performance. Before accepting the deployment, bring three connected reviews together.

The physical review checks the site, power, complete heat path and service access. The I T review checks the exact equipment configuration, interfaces, communication, memory and software support. The management review checks telemetry, permissions, supported actions and recovery dependencies.

Now look for a missing handoff. The tray supplier may provide a coolant requirement without confirming the facility conditions. A network diagram may omit a management path. An operational response may depend on software that is unavailable during the fault it is meant to handle.

For this proposal, a response command has been acknowledged, but nobody has demonstrated that the condition recovered. Fault-response verification is still missing. Keep that item open and assign an owner to supply the evidence.

Acceptance is an integration decision based on the intended workload and operating conditions. A collection of capable components becomes a deployable system when those requirements and responsibilities agree.
