A shared interface gives different suppliers a common boundary to design against. It might describe a mechanical envelope, an electrical connection, a cooling connection or a management capability. Agreement at that boundary can reduce repeated integration work and give customers more implementation choices.

That agreement leaves room for innovation inside each component. Suppliers can improve performance, efficiency or serviceability while keeping the interface recognizable. Operators and integrators can compare those implementations using common information rather than starting every discussion from a different set of assumptions.

The specification is the beginning of the evidence. The selected equipment still needs to demonstrate that it works together under the intended conditions. Two products may use the same standard name and support different options, ratings or revisions. A claim of openness does not settle those differences.

Think of the open ecosystem as a collaboration among operators, suppliers, integrators and facility teams. The useful outcome is a system whose boundaries are documented, whose responsibilities are understood, and whose configuration has been qualified. Common interfaces make that work more repeatable.
