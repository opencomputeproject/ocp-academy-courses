# pronunciation: OCP -> "O C P"

The O C P tenets give a design review a wider purpose. Efficiency asks how well the system uses resources for the useful work it delivers. Impact asks whether a contribution solves a meaningful problem for the community. Openness makes the design information and collaboration accessible.

Scalability asks what happens as the deployment grows or changes. The relevant limit may be network reach, power delivery, cooling, physical access or operational complexity. Sustainability brings the lifecycle into that discussion, including operating energy, water where relevant, materials, repair and reuse.

Use the tenets as questions, rather than labels applied after a product is selected. What evidence supports an efficiency claim? Which interface enables another supplier to participate? How will the system be serviced? What assumptions sit behind a total-cost comparison?

No single metric answers all five questions. A design may improve one outcome while creating another tradeoff. Make the boundary and assumptions visible so the team can judge the complete result. That connects the community principles to practical engineering decisions.
