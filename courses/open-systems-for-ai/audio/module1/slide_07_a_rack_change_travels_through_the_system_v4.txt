Imagine that an integration team selects a new accelerator tray for an existing cluster design. The performance case looks attractive, and the tray fits the available rack space. The change still needs a complete review.

Start with electrical power. Check the load profile, conversion, distribution and backup arrangement. Then follow the heat. The cold plates, coolant connections, distribution unit and facility heat-rejection path must support the intended operating conditions. Remaining air-cooled components still need a suitable environment.

Next, check the physical and network interfaces. Consider connector reach, cable routing, bend space, lifting access and how a technician removes the tray. A maintenance action should not unexpectedly disconnect another service or block the cooling path.

Finally, ask how the changed equipment is observed and controlled. The management system needs the required telemetry, fault indications and supported actions. Each domain contributes evidence, and the integration team checks the combined configuration.

The lesson is a repeatable habit. Follow a change across the boundaries before accepting it. A local improvement becomes useful when the complete system can support it.
