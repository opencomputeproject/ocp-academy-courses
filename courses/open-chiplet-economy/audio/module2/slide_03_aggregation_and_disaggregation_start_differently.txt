Package-level aggregation combines functions that may already exist as separate chips. Compared with connecting them across a printed circuit board, shorter package connections can reduce the required signal drive and the occupied space. The existing interfaces and die forms may constrain the result.

Disaggregation begins with a system partition. A function that could have remained on a monolithic die becomes a chiplet with an explicit interface. This allows more deliberate process selection and reuse, but it requires the architect to account for the new communication path. Neither approach guarantees a lower-cost product. First identify which problem the boundary is intended to solve.
