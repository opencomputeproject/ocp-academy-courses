A boundary is attractive only if the resulting communication is affordable. Begin with the workload. How much data crosses, in which direction, and with what burst pattern? Then ask how long the consumer can wait and how the sender learns that the receiver has space.

Peak interface bandwidth is not enough. Framing, flow control, and contention reduce usable throughput. A memory-intensive workload may be constrained by round-trip latency or the availability of buffers. Put these assumptions into the architecture model before selecting a package. A partition that reduces silicon cost but misses the response-time requirement has not solved the product problem.
