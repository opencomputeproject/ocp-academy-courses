Start with the connection requirements. Input-output pitch sets how closely connections are placed on the die. Escape pitch and routing determine how those connections can leave the attachment region. Signal-bump dimensions and the depth of the physical interface affect the layout and manufacturing options.

Next, consider the route between dies. Distance and acceptable insertion loss constrain the channel and its materials. These requirements can force more sophisticated routing or assembly. In a real product, die shape, warpage, and thermal constraints also matter. Do not mistake a teaching list for a complete package-design checklist.
