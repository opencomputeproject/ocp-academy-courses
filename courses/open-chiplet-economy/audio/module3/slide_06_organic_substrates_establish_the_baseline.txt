An organic substrate routes signals and power between the dies and the outside of the package. Its materials, build-up layers, and attachment method determine what can be connected economically. A cored structure includes a central supporting core; a coreless structure uses a different mechanical stack.

There is no universal winner. A large package may place a premium on flatness and mechanical stability. A dense attachment region may require finer routing and tighter tolerances. Evaluate the complete stack with the manufacturer rather than selecting a label alone. This baseline matters because advanced local interconnect can still sit on a larger organic substrate that provides external routing.
