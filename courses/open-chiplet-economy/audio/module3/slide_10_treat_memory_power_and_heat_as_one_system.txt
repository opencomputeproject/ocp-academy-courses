High Bandwidth Memory, or H B M, illustrates why package design is a system problem. The base die contains communication and control functions, while the stacked memory imposes its own physical and thermal constraints. Increasing bandwidth can change power demand and the available area for other functions.

Heat removal and power delivery must be designed together. A layout that improves one connection may make another path harder. The recent Summit discussion of custom memory emphasizes this co-design across the base die, die-to-die power, and package thermals. Use that relationship as the lesson. Product-specific performance projections are not general guarantees for every memory stack.
