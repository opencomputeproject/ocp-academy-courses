An interface comparison needs consistent definitions. Energy per bit depends on the circuitry included and on the traffic level. A lightly used link can look different from one carrying sustained payload. Latency depends on where measurement begins and ends, and whether it includes adapters, buffering, or retry.

Bandwidth density also needs a denominator. A value per millimeter of die edge cannot be compared directly with a value per square millimeter of interface area. State directionality and usable payload as well. The electrical-interface metrics contribution provides a structured way to record these conditions. Use it to ask comparable questions, rather than combining unrelated headline numbers.
