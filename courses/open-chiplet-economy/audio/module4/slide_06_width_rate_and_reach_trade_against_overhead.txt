A link can increase raw bandwidth by adding lanes or increasing the rate per lane. Each choice has consequences. More lanes consume connections and die edge. Higher signaling rates may place tighter demands on the channel, clocking, and receiver.

Serial and parallel approaches make different tradeoffs. A forwarded-clock parallel connection can be efficient over a short distance, while a serial approach can reduce the number of signal connections and serve other reach requirements. Avoid treating either category as universally superior. Include protocol overhead, flow control, and the intended traffic pattern when estimating useful bandwidth. Then verify the design against the actual package channel.
