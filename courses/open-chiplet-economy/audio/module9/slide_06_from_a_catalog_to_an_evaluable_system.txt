Federated simulation explores a useful idea: bring an agreed simulation request to the supplier's model rather than requiring the supplier to hand over the model. An integrator could exchange boundary conditions and receive permitted results while the detailed implementation remains under supplier control.

That arrangement needs more than a secure container. Model identity, version, authorization, query limits, and permitted outputs all affect confidentiality and trust in the result. The Summit proposal describes an initial proof-of-concept direction rather than an approved universal service. Its teaching value is the relationship between shared analysis and protected implementation. Accuracy and confidentiality both require explicit policies and evidence.
