Wafer-level testing probes the individual dies before the wafer is diced. It screens functionality and electrical behavior at that stage. Additional die-level screening can be appropriate before expensive assembly. Package substrate checks can occur in a parallel manufacturing flow; a package substrate is not the same thing as the silicon wafer.

After assembly, new connections and new failure opportunities exist. Package testing must address those inter-die paths and the accessible functions. System-level testing then checks integrated behavior under relevant operating conditions. These stages complement each other. Passing wafer sort does not prove the final package, and a final system test does not replace economical early defect screening.
