# pronunciation: NIC -> Nick

One OCP Nick three point zero design should work with both airflow orientations. Keep the card orientation fixed and compare where the air enters. In the hot-aisle case, air approaches from the card-edge or heatsink side. In a typical rear-mounted server application, upstream components have already warmed that air. The specification uses fifty-five degrees Celsius as a typical local inlet condition, with hotter cases also defined.

In the cold-aisle case, air approaches from the faceplate and network-connector side. A front-mounted card receives air closer to the system inlet temperature, typically twenty-five to thirty-five degrees Celsius in the reference conditions. These are two installation cases, not a fan reversing direction during operation.

In both cases, airflow is directed over the top side of the card. Component placement must assume no cooling airflow underneath. The top-side heatsink and its approach airflow therefore carry the thermal design burden.

The specification's analysis finds the hot-aisle case more challenging, but the actual cooling capacity depends on the card, local inlet temperature, velocity, and system design. Do not turn a reference comparison into a guaranteed power-headroom percentage for every Nick.
