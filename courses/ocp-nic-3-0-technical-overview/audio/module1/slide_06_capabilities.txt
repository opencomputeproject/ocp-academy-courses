# pronunciation: NIC -> Nick; PCIe -> P C I E; SFF -> S F F; DSFF -> D S F F; DRAM -> D RAM; DPU -> D P U; NC-SI -> N C S I; FRU EEPROM -> Fru E E Prom

A common connector does not force every OCP Nick three point zero card into the same host architecture. Start with a single host and one sixteen-lane P C I E link. A different supported design can connect two eight-lane endpoints to one host. Another can connect four hosts through four separate four-lane groups. These are alternative configurations, not a live change from one arrangement to another.

The card and baseboard must support the chosen topology. Lane count alone does not prove compatibility. D S F F extends the interface to as many as thirty-two lanes, and the specification includes Gen six signal-integrity guidance. That does not mean every product implements Gen six.

The wider platform also supports more than basic packet movement. Larger board layouts can accommodate D RAM and SmartNick or D P U functions. N C S I provides sideband management, and the Fru E E Prom supplies identification and inventory data. Standard thermal fixtures help compare implementations. The form factor simplifies field replacement, but live hot swap depends on the baseboard and system implementation. The common interface enables choices; the actual card and platform determine which choices are available.
