# pronunciation: NIC -> Nick; PCIe -> P C I E; DSFF -> D S F F; SFF -> S F F; PRSNTB -> presence B; FRU EEPROM -> Fru E E Prom; OEM -> O E M; BIF -> bifurcation; LFF -> L F F; MHS -> M H S

Version one dot six is best used as an implementation checklist. Consider D S F F discovery. Presence B encoding first identifies the card as D S F F. Presence alone does not declare every supported P C I E link subdivision.

While the card is in ID Mode, the baseboard reads the relevant Fru E E Prom O E M records: D zero, D one where implemented, and D two. The baseboard also checks record versions. D zero and D one describe supported subdivision choices and their bifurcation encodings. D two declares additional capabilities, including the encoding width. These values are declared by the card, unlike the predefined S F F decoder.

The baseboard then selects a configuration supported by both sides and drives the corresponding bifurcation signals. Do not substitute a generic fixed encoding or assume that a physically fitting card supports the host's preferred lane arrangement.

Other implementation checks remain important. Gen six signal-integrity work must use the OCP-specific fixture and insertion-loss context. Power-state tables must be read with their interface-support conditions. Extended O E M and Flex I O records use D two and D three to avoid M H S allocation conflicts. Finally, L F F is deprecated and retained in Appendix nine for historical context; active designs should focus on S F F and D S F F.
