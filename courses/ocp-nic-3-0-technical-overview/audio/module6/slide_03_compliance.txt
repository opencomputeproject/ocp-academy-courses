# pronunciation: NIC -> Nick; RoHS -> rose; REACH -> reach; WEEE -> waste electrical and electronic equipment; EMC -> E M C; FCC -> F C C; CISPR -> C I S P R; VCCI -> V C C I; ESD -> E S D

Version one dot six separates required compliance from additional recommendations. Required environmental compliance includes China and E U Rose, REACH, the waste electrical and electronic equipment directive, and other listed substance, packaging, and battery requirements.

The applicable electromagnetic requirements depend on where the product is deployed. Examples include F C C Class A in the United States, European emissions and immunity requirements, C I S P R thirty-two-based requirements for Australia and New Zealand, and V C C I Class A in Japan. C I S P R and V C C I requirements are not generally optional when the corresponding geography applies.

Product safety and electrostatic-discharge immunity are also required. The specification identifies emissions and immunity testing that must be completed at system level, so Nick vendors need to work with system vendors. Qualifying a card does not replace evaluation of the integrated system.

Additional recommendations include halogen and arsenic limits and an F C C Class A emissions margin of ten decibels. Keep required obligations, recommended margins, and system-level responsibility distinct when planning compliance evidence.
