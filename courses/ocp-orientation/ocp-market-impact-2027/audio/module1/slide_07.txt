You’ve found room for another rack. Your usual supplier has a long lead time. A second supplier says its equipment will fit. What would you like them to send you?

In their power keynote, A B B and Eaton used the example of a connector. An electrical system can stay in service through several generations of computing equipment. A shared interface gives the people designing the next generation something concrete to work with.

Follow the power path into the rack. Here, where two pieces meet, a drawing gives both teams a way to check the dimensions and the electrical requirements. Then they can ask which combinations have actually been tested.

I D C describes a practical advantage for buyers when parts are scarce. With a shared specification, they have somewhere to start when they look for a second source.
