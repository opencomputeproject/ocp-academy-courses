## 01 · How many people does it take to build one cluster?

[curious] How many roofs does it take to cover one computer?

More than thirty structures, spread across different locations. Suddenly you need someone who understands fiber, someone who knows the buildings, and someone who can get all those people talking.

Some design chips. Some run facilities. Some ask who’s going to pay for it all. As you meet a few of them, keep one seat at the table in mind. Who would you bring?

## 02 · What does OCP count?

Picture a purchase order on a desk. A data center operator needs servers for a new hall. The operator pays an equipment supplier. A shared design helped shape those servers. Where, exactly, does that purchase show up?

O C P commissioned I D C to study what buyers around the world spend on equipment based on O C P-recognized designs. Servers account for the largest part. The study also covers equipment such as switches, racks, power systems and cooling. Think of the dollars as traveling from a buyer to a seller. That’s the transaction the study is trying to measure.

[thoughtful] Here’s an interesting wrinkle. A company can use an openly shared design even if it has never joined O C P. The design can travel farther than the membership list. That’s part of what makes the market worth measuring. And in twenty twenty-six, the people doing the measuring had quite an update.

## 03 · The forecast that moved

In twenty twenty-three, the opening address at the Global Summit picked up on a change. A I was already prompting the industry to rethink how much data center infrastructure it would need.

A year later, I D C put a number on one part of that story. Its twenty twenty-four study projected seventy-three and a half billion dollars in spending on O C P-recognized equipment in twenty twenty-eight.

Now move the target year forward once, to twenty twenty-nine. Leave it there. In the twenty twenty-five study, I D C expected buyers to spend two hundred ninety-five billion dollars that year.

[curious] Imagine you’re at the next Summit, waiting for the new estimate. Move the marker to where you think it landed. Make your guess.

[with quiet surprise] About eight hundred billion dollars. Same target year. About two point seven times the earlier forecast. I D C points to two changes. Buyers are investing more in A I infrastructure, and O C P designs are finding their way into more of that equipment.

One year further out, I D C projects eight hundred ninety-two billion dollars in twenty thirty. That would be about fifty-one percent of spending across the categories the study measures. When you hear that number repeated, take the year and the scope along with it.

## 04 · What does a shared design buy a team?

A rack is on order. The delivery date is circled. The facilities lead has a question. Will the new equipment work with what’s already in the room?

One way to lose a week is to discover that question on the loading dock. With a shared drawing, the buyer, the installer and the facilities lead can sit down together while there’s still time to change the plan. They can check clearances, ask which connections have been tested, and agree on who will support the installation.

A separate study by S G Strategy asked organizations about the value they get from taking part in O C P. It reported that infrastructure deployment could be accelerated by up to fifteen percent. The number describes a range of experience. The conversation on the loading dock is one way to picture what saved time might look like.

[inviting] Think about your own team. Which question would you most like answered before delivery day?

## 05 · What can another team build from this rack?

At the Summit, A M D’s Lisa Su described a choice her team made for Helios. They used Open Rack Wide. On stage, she traced the work back to the partners, including Meta, who helped turn the rack architecture into something customers could use. The power shelves and the way the equipment is managed are part of that story, too.

Now look at a different rack. Ian Buck walked through the rack and tray designs Nvidia was contributing through M G X one point two. Watch the tray slide toward the back of the rack. Think about the person who has to reach those connections when a tray needs service.

[thoughtful] A buyer and a technician can look at the very same drawing and notice different things. The buyer wants to know which suppliers can work to the interface. The technician wants to know how the connection comes apart on a Tuesday night when the system is in use. Choose a perspective and see what catches your eye.

## 06 · When a cluster outgrows its roof

Come back to Prometheus. Inside one building, the computers already have to exchange information quickly enough to work as a team. Now put some of those computers in another building. The training job still needs them to act like one cluster.

Santosh Janardhan described how Meta brought connections from different buildings together in a regional network. Follow the light from one roof to the next. Along the way, somebody has to specify the fiber, choose the switches and figure out what happens if a path goes down.

Bloomberg Intelligence sees that change in the market around each A I rack. Adding computers also creates demand for the equipment that connects them. A network designer can look at a map like this and see a whole second shopping list.

[curious] Pick a link between the buildings. What would you want to know before that link became part of a training run?

## 07 · What if the next supplier’s connector fits?

You’ve found room for another rack. Your usual supplier has a long lead time. A second supplier says its equipment will fit. What would you like them to send you?

In their power keynote, A B B and Eaton used the example of a connector. An electrical system can stay in service through several generations of computing equipment. A shared interface gives the people designing the next generation something concrete to work with.

Follow the power path into the rack. Here, where two pieces meet, a drawing gives both teams a way to check the dimensions and the electrical requirements. Then they can ask which combinations have actually been tested.

I D C describes a practical advantage for buyers when parts are scarce. With a shared specification, they have somewhere to start when they look for a second source.

[inviting] You’re the buyer. Pick what you’d request first. See which question it helps you answer.

## 08 · Would you run that headline?

## 09 · Where should the answer come from?

Picture a camera above a moving production line. A bottle passes underneath. The camera spots a crooked cap. A machine downstream has a moment to divert the bottle. Where should the image go for an answer?

One path leads to a computer beside the line. The other leads to a remote data center. Give the remote path a little more distance and you can see why the team might want to try running the model on site. But who will service the local computer? And how much space and cooling will it need?

This is inference, putting a trained model to work on a new input. O C P’s A I Computing Continuum Project, or A I C C, works on open hardware for places like this factory. A computer beside the line has to fit the space, power and cooling the factory can spare.

At the Summit, O C P’s George Tchaparian asked for help on three fronts as the community looks to twenty twenty-seven. Give designers more choice of A I chips. Design the computing equipment and its facility together. And make open infrastructure useful wherever inference needs to happen, including places like this factory.

[curious] Which question would you ask the factory team first?

## 10 · Find your first conversation

Start with something you already know. If you buy or maintain equipment, the Rack and Power Project is a place to bring a detail you wish had been on a supplier’s drawing. If you connect buildings, the Networking Project is a place to compare notes on what changes when a cluster moves beyond one site.

If economics or policy is your field, take a look at Heat Reuse. A data center may have heat to offer. A neighbor may have a use for it. People still have to work out who pays for the connection and what happens when demand changes. There are workstreams for exactly those questions.

And if you run a factory, an enterprise site or a telecom facility, the A I Computing Continuum Project wants to understand where equipment will actually have to work.

[warmly] Pick the conversation you’d like to try. You’ll find a question to start with and a link to the people already working on it.
