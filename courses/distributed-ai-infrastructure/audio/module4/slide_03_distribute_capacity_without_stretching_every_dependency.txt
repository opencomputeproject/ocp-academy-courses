# pronunciation: AICC -> "A I C C"; AI -> "A I"

Distributing capacity does not require stretching every dependency. A site can host a complete training group while another site runs a separate job. Regional inference replicas can answer requests locally and receive model artifacts through a controlled distribution process.

Other designs deliberately split a computation across sites. In that case, a remote exchange may sit directly on a training step or request's critical path. The latency and failure behavior of that exchange become part of the execution budget.

A data pipeline creates another relationship. Preprocessing at one site can feed a consumer elsewhere, with buffering that absorbs some short-term variation. That is different from a collective that requires remote contributions before advancing.

A I C C, the A I Computing Continuum, examines Scale-Across, Sync-Across and Reach-Across as distinct purposes with different service objectives. Use that course to explore the broader placement models. For this review, ask a narrower question: what must cross, how often, and what must wait for it?
