A private connection can give a team more control over its connectivity. It does not remove the need for an operating contract.

Specify the capacity available to the workload and the conditions under which it is available. Examine contention, traffic shaping, actual paths, loss, maintenance behavior and failure recovery. A path that meets an objective in normal operation may not meet it after traffic moves to a smaller backup connection.

Also identify who owns each boundary. Who detects a fault, who can change routing, and who is responsible for restoring the service? Encryption, access controls and monitoring still need explicit design. Private is not a complete security or resilience specification.

Then test the workload over the selected paths. Useful evidence includes measured latency and variation, sustained supply or transfer behavior, and the service outcome under a relevant failure.
