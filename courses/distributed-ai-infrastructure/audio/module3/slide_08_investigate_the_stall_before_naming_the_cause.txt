A long training step is an observation. Its cause still needs investigation.

Start by locating the late required work. Did an accelerator take longer to compute? Did an input path fail to supply data? Did queueing, retransmission or another communication cost extend a collective? Or does the apparent ordering problem come from timestamps that aren't aligned closely enough for the comparison?

Correlate evidence around the affected steps. Job progress and collective durations tell you where useful execution slowed. Network and device measurements test possible mechanisms. Facility signals can help when the hypothesis concerns power or thermal behavior.

Choose measurement resolution for the event you are trying to observe. A coarse average may hide a short disturbance; collecting every available counter at maximum frequency does not automatically identify the cause.

The example team should bring a causal explanation supported by several consistent observations. Avoid turning one suspicious timestamp or one low-utilization chart into a diagnosis before checking alternatives.
