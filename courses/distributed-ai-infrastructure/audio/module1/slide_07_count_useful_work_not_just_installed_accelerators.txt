Installed capacity and useful progress answer different questions. A count tells you how many accelerators are present. It doesn't tell you how much training or serving work they complete.

For a training job, useful measurements might include elapsed step time and completed training progress over a defined interval. For inference, they might include requests or tokens delivered while meeting the service's latency objective. Keep the workload and measurement boundary explicit.

Now examine the supporting resources. Can storage and preprocessing supply the input? Can memory hold the required working set? Can the fabric carry the exchanges without excessive waiting? Does the electrical and cooling envelope support the proposed equipment and operating condition?

A busy device is not automatically doing useful work. It might be repeating an operation or working on a request that misses its service objective. Before approving an expansion, compare useful progress with resource behavior. That is how a hardware inventory becomes an engineering scaling decision.
