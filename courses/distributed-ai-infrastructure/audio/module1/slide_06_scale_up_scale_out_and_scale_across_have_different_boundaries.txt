Scale up, scale out and scale across help us name different communication scopes. Their exact boundaries depend on the architecture.

Scale up commonly describes a closely connected compute domain in which accelerators cooperate through a high-bandwidth interconnect. That domain need not match the outline of one physical rack.

Scale out connects compute domains into a larger cluster. The fabric must support the workload's traffic pattern, including the exchanges that sit on its critical path. Adding more domains changes the demand on that fabric and on shared services.

Scale across extends the discussion between sites or infrastructure domains. The work crossing that boundary might be training data, model artifacts, a dependent computation, or requests to a remote service. These are different cases.

Treat the terms as a way to organize questions, rather than a promise about distance or a prescription for a particular protocol. Ask what crosses each boundary, how often it crosses, and whether the next useful operation must wait for it.
