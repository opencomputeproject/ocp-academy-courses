Keep three ideas together. First, identify the workload before assigning it a coordination model. Independent serving and synchronous training don't have the same dependency boundary.

Second, a late required participant can delay dependent work. That does not automatically mean corrupted state or a failure of every service at the site.

Third, judge an expansion by useful progress and supporting capacity. Accelerator count alone cannot establish a scaling result.

Carry those questions into the example team's review: who must coordinate, what must they exchange, and which evidence shows that the proposed system can support the work?
