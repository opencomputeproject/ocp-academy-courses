Observation becomes useful when it explains the progress of the workload. A green device count does not establish that a training group is advancing or that an inference service is meeting its objective.

Begin with the outcome: step completion and training progress, or service throughput and latency. Then correlate it with the signals that can test the suspected cause. Collective duration, accelerator behavior, queues, retransmissions and input supply may all matter. Power or cooling measurements become relevant when the hypothesis concerns the physical envelope.

Measurement cadence should fit the behavior. A slow average can hide a short event, but more frequent collection is not a diagnosis by itself. Keep the operation identities and timestamp uncertainty needed to connect observations across systems.

Finally, separate observing a condition from authorizing an action. A controller needs a defined policy and scope before it isolates hardware, changes membership or starts recovery. The telemetry supplies evidence; the operating contract determines what the system may do with it.
