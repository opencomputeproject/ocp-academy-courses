F C S A uses content classes to separate requirements from explanation. Declarations define concepts, terms, structures, or encodings. Rules describe required behavior. Both are normative. Goals, information, rationale, implementation notes, and software guidance help interpretation, but are informative. A useful reading method is to identify the statement class, read the surrounding section, and then check applicability. An isolated rule identifier is not enough context for an integration decision.

Compliance levels add another dimension. Level zero includes the basic functional and interface rules for the stated chiplet or system type. Level one adds requirements and includes the applicable level-zero rules. Full compliance includes the applicable remaining requirements, including rules without a displayed level. A higher level does not make lower-level obligations disappear. Applicability still depends on the implementation and the feature being considered.

In a supplier discussion, replace an unqualified compatibility claim with a precise statement of the specification version, system or chiplet type, level, interfaces, and evidence. Also identify the design and profile requirements needed by this product. A declaration of compliance is a claim to investigate against those obligations; the course itself provides no certification process. Keep the version and level attached to the scope so that different teams can evaluate the same claim.
