# pronunciation: F C S A; input output; nonvolatile memory; chiplet root of trust for attestation.

Work from a concrete threat rather than a list of security features. Suppose an unauthorized accelerator is substituted during integration. Identify the threatened asset and adversary, then ask what identity and composition evidence would expose the substitution. Authentication alone is incomplete if any authentic device is accepted: the system must compare the observed chiplet and its role with the authorized composition. The policy also needs a defined response when that comparison fails.

Now consider an accelerator whose debug state permits access inconsistent with operation. Evidence must describe the relevant state, and the system needs an enforced response if the state changes. Merely logging the event does not show that sensitive assets are protected. A restricted bootstrap state, isolation, recovery, or another product-defined action needs validation against the particular threat and operating constraints.

F C S A uses Control, Accept, Suppress, and Transfer as the four treatment categories, abbreviated cast. Choose a treatment with a reason. Explain the initial likelihood and impact, the mitigation mechanism, the remaining risk, and the evidence supporting the conclusion. The chapter's generic ratings are starting points, not measurements of your product. Do not copy a lower residual rating without the evidence and validated response that justify it.
