The worksheet joins the decisions from the course without pretending to replace the specification. Start with the system software boundary and intended configuration. Record the specification version, system type, chiplet types, and compliance level. Then link every critical interface to its function, protocol, transport, version compatibility, and state availability. Mark unresolved mappings and additional design or profile requirements explicitly.

Record boot ownership separately from security acceptance. Identify the image source, loading model, synchronization authority, and authentication responsibilities. Include the system-control roles and the policy governing reset and power changes. For trust, request evidence of identity, firmware and lifecycle state, topology and policy bindings, freshness, and the response to a relevant change. These entries need an owner and a verifiable artifact, not a promise that another team will handle them.

Finally, review transaction-property differences and aggregate capacity at the planned scale. Include the assumptions under which the evidence holds. If an artifact covers two chiplets in one configuration, it does not automatically establish a larger or differently configured system. State what would require repeating the review.

This is a course aid for structuring questions. It is not a procurement standard or an exhaustive compliance checklist. Use the linked specification sections to expand each row into the applicable requirements and the product-specific evidence needed to assess them.
