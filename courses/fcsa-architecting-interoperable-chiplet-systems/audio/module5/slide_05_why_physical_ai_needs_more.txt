The Physical A I workstream presentation asks how a shared architecture can serve systems that sense and act in the physical world. The proposal identifies six constraints: deterministic timing, sensor ingress, reliability and safety, security and trust, power and thermal efficiency, and multi-chiplet scalability. These are motivations for architectural work, not newly published F C S A rules.

The presenters map those constraints into ten candidate areas, including closed-loop control, deterministic execution, real-time interconnect, security for physical action, energy management, heterogeneous compute, functional safety, safety management and observability, timestamped sensor data and fusion, and multi-chiplet scalability. The important lesson is how deployment needs become visible architectural obligations across components.

Consider a robotics variation of our accelerator case. Correct computation may still arrive too late for the control loop. Fault detection may still need a bounded reporting, containment, and recovery response across the package. Security evidence may need to preserve the authority to command physical action. Those questions require product-specific requirements and evidence; they cannot be answered by the package's chiplet count or a foundation-level label alone.

The Summit material presents an extension workstream and charter, with proposed future deliverables. It does not certify a product, define A I algorithms, or replace the physical and link layers. Keep that status visible when using its ideas. The foundation-to-design-to-profile model explains where additional requirements can be developed without attributing them prematurely to version one point one point zero.
