The supplier now offers a reusable accelerator with a different boot arrangement, and the product team wants more coherent requester chiplets. A second deployment adds a robotics control loop. Treat these as three changes with overlapping evidence needs, rather than a single administrative update to the parts list.

For the boot change, revisit image delivery, interface availability, execution control, and the firmware trust relationship. A shared storage path does not establish authenticity, and a new delivery path may alter who must authenticate which component or asset. For scale, revisit transaction-property adaptation and snoop-filter demand for the full participating configuration. For the robotics deployment, identify additional timing, fault-containment, supervision, and safety requirements that the proposed profile work is intended to address.

Spend about eight minutes on workbook exercise five. Assign an owner and a next action to each changed assumption. Separate a published requirement, an implementation choice needing agreement, and a proposed future-profile question. Then write a release recommendation in two sentences: what the available evidence supports, and what remains unresolved before the intended deployment can be accepted.

A good recommendation can be conditional. It need not assert that the composition passes or fails every possible requirement. It must identify the scope of the evidence and the decisions still needed. This is the practical value of the architecture: different teams can discuss the same obligations, assumptions, and limits before committing to a system.
