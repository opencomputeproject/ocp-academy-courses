# pronunciation: FIT -> "fit"; 10^9 -> "one billion"; Cpk -> "C P K"
Hardware failures follow the familiar bathtub curve.

Infant failures appear early. Process controls, screening, and burn-in are intended to catch them before equipment reaches the datacenter.

Random failures occupy the long, relatively flat middle of the service life. They occur without a predictable schedule and are modeled as a constant rate. Fit means failures in time: the number expected per one billion device-hours.

Wear-out rises late in life. Design and qualification should push that region beyond the planned operating window.

Scale-up makes the random region especially important. Current pluggable optics may contribute fifty fit or more. A large Pod multiplies that rate across many endpoints. The SROI goal is two fit or less per optical link end, approaching the behavior expected from highly integrated silicon.

Integration can help because it reduces discrete components and mechanical interfaces. A controlled semiconductor process can also improve consistency. The paper notes that new optical technologies should first build a track record in more fault-tolerant applications, particularly before they are placed in C P O or other hard-to-repair locations.
