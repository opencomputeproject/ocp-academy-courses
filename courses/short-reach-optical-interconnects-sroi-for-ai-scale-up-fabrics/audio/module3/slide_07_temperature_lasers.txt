# pronunciation: FIT -> "fit"; ELSFP -> "E L S F P"; OIF -> "O I F"
The achievable failure rate depends on operating conditions and component architecture.

Optical-component fit rises exponentially with junction temperature. The paper contrasts the conventional upper region near seventy degrees Celsius with a goal near forty degrees. That thirty-degree difference can produce a meaningful reliability improvement under established acceleration models.

Lasers deserve separate attention. A transceiver may contain multiple sources, so their individual rates add to the endpoint total. The appendix sets a goal of two fit or less per laser source at forty degrees.

One mitigation is an external laser source. An O I F E L S F P-style module moves the laser to a front-pluggable location, where it can be replaced without removing the optical engine or compute assembly.

The other approach keeps lasers behind the front panel and adds redundancy. If one source fails, the endpoint can switch to a spare. The benefit depends on correlation. Two devices from the same process or production lot may share a failure mechanism, so their probabilities cannot always be treated as independent.

Temperature control and laser placement are therefore part of the system reliability architecture, not implementation details left to the component vendor.
