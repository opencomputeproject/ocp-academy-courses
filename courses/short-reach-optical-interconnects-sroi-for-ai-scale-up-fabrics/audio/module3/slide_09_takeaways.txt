# pronunciation: FIT -> "fit"; FEC -> "F E C"; CPO -> "C P O"
The core lesson is multiplication.

At scale-up lane counts, ordinary component power and failure rates become extraordinary rack totals. That is why SROI endpoints must be purpose-built rather than conventional modules copied into a new topology.

Link reliability and hardware reliability are different problems. F E C-tail events, retries, and link flaps reduce effective bandwidth and add tail latency. Dead or marginal hardware requires isolation, failover, and repair.

The hardware target approaches two fit per optical end. Reaching it depends on screening, a controlled process, qualification, operating temperature, and the laser architecture.

The recovery side matters just as much. Behind-panel efficiency must be paired with precise diagnostics, a realistic repair path, and redundancy that accounts for correlated failures.

Availability is therefore architectural. It is the result of component quality, placement, environmental control, fault isolation, resilience, and service design operating together.
