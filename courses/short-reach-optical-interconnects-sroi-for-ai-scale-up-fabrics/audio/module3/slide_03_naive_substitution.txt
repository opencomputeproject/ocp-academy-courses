# pronunciation: GPUs -> "G P Yous"; 800G -> "eight hundred G"; FIT -> "fit"
The fastest way to see the system problem is to replace copper one module at a time.

The whitepaper uses a representative rack with seventy-two accelerators. Its local copper network contains more than five thousand differential pairs carrying two-hundred-gigabit signals.

Now replace those paths with conventional retimed eight hundred G optical modules. The rack would require one thousand two hundred ninety-six modules. At twelve and a half watts each, the added power approaches sixteen kilowatts. That is optical-module power alone, before the rest of the system absorbs the thermal and power-delivery consequences.

Reliability multiplies the same way. If each module contributes about seventy-five fit, the aggregate system rate approaches one hundred thousand fit. The exact operational outcome depends on architecture and redundancy, but the direction is decisive. A module class designed for ordinary front-panel networking cannot simply be copied more than a thousand times into a synchronous compute fabric.

SROI therefore requires purpose-built endpoints with much lower power, much lower failure rates, and greater integration. The architecture must remove duplicated functions rather than multiplying them.
