# pronunciation: FIT -> "fit"; FEC -> "F E C"; CRC -> "C R C"; CPO -> "C P O"
By the end of this module, you’ll be able to explain why reliability must be evaluated after the link count is multiplied across the Pod.

You’ll start with the whitepaper’s seventy-two-accelerator example and see why conventional retimed optics fail both the power and fit budget.

You’ll separate link reliability from hardware reliability. Link events create F E C pressure, retries, link flaps, and tail latency. Hardware events create underperforming or dead lanes that require isolation and repair.

You’ll interpret the infant, random, and wear-out regions of the failure curve, and understand why the target approaches two fit per optical end.

Finally, you’ll connect operating temperature and laser architecture to the reliability floor. Then you’ll bring placement, diagnostics, redundancy, qualification, and repair together as one availability design, especially for N P O and C P O behind the front panel.
