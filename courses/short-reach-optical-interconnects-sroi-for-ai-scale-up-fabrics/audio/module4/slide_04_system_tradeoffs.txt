# pronunciation: NPO -> "N P O"; CPO -> "C P O"; ASIC -> "A sick"
No placement dominates every axis.

Power improves as the optical engine moves toward the A sick. Shorter electrical paths need less equalization and lose less energy before conversion.

Serviceability moves the other way. A front-panel module has a small field-repair unit. N P O may turn the board into the repair unit. C P O can make the package or integrated assembly the repair unit.

Density favors deeper integration. N P O and C P O can better match the linear bandwidth escaping the A sick and reduce pressure on the front panel.

Reliability evidence may favor established solutions at first. A new high-density engine can look excellent in design analysis but still lack enough field hours to satisfy a synchronous Pod.

Supply-chain structure matters as well. A tightly integrated optical engine can concentrate packaging knowledge, source qualification, and proprietary interfaces. Open sockets and multi-source agreements can reduce that risk, but they must mature.

The decision should begin with the non-negotiable constraint. A serviceability-first deployment chooses differently from a power-limited or density-limited deployment, even when both ultimately target the same SROI reach.
