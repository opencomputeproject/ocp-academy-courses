# pronunciation: CPC -> "C P C"; PAM4 -> "PAM four"; PAM6 -> "PAM six"; 448 -> "four hundred forty-eight"; SerDes -> "sir-dez"
Copper does not vanish at four hundred gigabits per lane. Its useful reach becomes much shorter.

The most favorable topology is co-packaged copper, or C P C. It minimizes die, package, and board loss before the signal reaches the cable.

The paper reviews a four hundred forty-eight gigabit PAM four channel study that scales package and cable assumptions from an earlier generation. Under those assumptions, about one meter of C P C-to-C P C cable may be possible.

The authors are careful about the uncertainty. Future package loss and sir-dez capability are not yet established. Public measurements of shorter C P C assemblies still show severe loss near the relevant frequencies. The one-meter conclusion may therefore be optimistic.

As a practical forward-looking estimate, the paper suggests that half a meter may be more realistic. That reach is still valuable inside a chassis or tightly integrated rack, especially if PAM six lowers the electrical Nyquist frequency.

The architecture becomes tiered. Copper remains the best medium at the shortest distance. SROI carries the compute fabric when the physical system grows beyond that shrinking electrical envelope.
