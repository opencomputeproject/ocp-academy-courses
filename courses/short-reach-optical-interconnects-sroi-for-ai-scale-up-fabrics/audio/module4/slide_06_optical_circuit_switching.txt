# pronunciation: OCS -> "O C S"; MEMS -> "mems"; LCoS -> "L C O S"
Optical circuit switching extends SROI beyond fixed transport.

An electrical scale-up switch terminates the incoming link, processes the signal, and drives a second link. That creates two electrical I O boundaries, with their associated power and latency.

An O C S establishes a dedicated optical path between endpoints without electrically terminating the data inside the switch. The path can be reconfigured to route around a failure, right-size hardware for a job, or create isolation between tenants.

The tradeoff moves into the optical budget. The end-to-end link now includes two fiber segments plus switch insertion loss. Crosstalk, port count, and switching speed depend on the technology.

Robot-arm systems can reach very large port counts but switch slowly. Free-space mems, liquid-crystal, and piezoelectric systems operate around millisecond time scales with moderate loss. Integrated waveguide switches can move toward nanoseconds or microseconds, but usually at higher insertion loss and smaller port counts.

O C S is therefore not a free bypass. It is a programmable fabric option whose loss, speed, scale, and reliability must align with the workload and the optical link margin.
