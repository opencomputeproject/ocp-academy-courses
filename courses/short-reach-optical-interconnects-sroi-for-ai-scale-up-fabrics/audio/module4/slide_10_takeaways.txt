# pronunciation: LPO -> "L P O"; RTLR -> "R T L R"; NPO -> "N P O"; CPO -> "C P O"; OCS -> "O C S"; WDM -> "W D M"
The roadmap is a portfolio, not one universal module.

L P O and R T L R provide the serviceable first step for multi-rack deployment. N P O and C P O become more attractive as power and density pressure increase, provided reliability evidence, repair models, and open interfaces mature.

O C S can make the scale-up topology programmable, but its insertion loss, crosstalk, scale, switching speed, and reliability become part of the end-to-end design.

At four hundred gigabits per lane, optics favors PAM four. Higher modulation orders pay a steeper optical noise and margin penalty.

Slow-and-wide W D M offers another route by trading lane speed for parallelism. Copper remains useful at the shortest tier. As rates advance, that tier may contract to around half a meter.

Broad adoption requires coordinated system action. The community must define SROI reach categories, create tiered qualification, establish interoperable optical objectives, and align optical readiness with scale-up protocol roadmaps. These are ecosystem decisions, not isolated component choices.
