# pronunciation: LPO -> "L P O"; RTLR -> "R T L R"; NPO -> "N P O"; CPO -> "C P O"; CPX MSA -> "C P X M S A"
The paper recommends a staged path rather than one abrupt migration.

The first tier uses L P O and R T L R for initial multi-rack Pods. These options preserve front-panel service and draw on familiar pluggable operations. They still reduce power compared with a fully retimed module, making them a practical entry point when repair access is the leading constraint.

The second tier moves toward N P O or C P O. These architectures improve power, density, and signal integrity, but they need stronger qualification evidence, a well-defined repair model, and more mature open interfaces.

The transition is driven by evidence. Field reliability data must show that integrated optical engines can meet the Pod’s failure budget. Packaging and assembly processes must reach stable volume. Multi-source efforts such as C P X M S A must reduce dependence on one proprietary stack.

This two-tier model also protects deployment schedules. System architects can introduce optical reach without waiting for every integrated technology to mature, while preserving a roadmap toward the efficiency needed at higher lane counts.
