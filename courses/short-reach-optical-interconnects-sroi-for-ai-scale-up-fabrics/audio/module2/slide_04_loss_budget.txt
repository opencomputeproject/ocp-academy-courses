# pronunciation: PCIe -> "P C I E"; PAM4 -> "PAM four"; IEEE 802.3dj -> "I triple E eight-oh-two point three D J"; 212.5 -> "two hundred twelve point five"; 53.125 -> "fifty-three point one two five"
Loss budget explains why the reach contracts so quickly.

The whitepaper compares two electrical interfaces. P C I E generation seven carries one hundred twenty-eight gigabits per second with PAM four. Its Nyquist frequency is thirty-two gigahertz. The paper uses an approximate pin-to-pin loss budget of minus twenty-four decibels, with about minus eleven decibels allocated to a one-meter cable assembly.

I triple E eight-oh-two point three D J carries two hundred twelve point five gigabits per second with PAM four. The Nyquist frequency rises to fifty-three point one two five gigahertz. The example total budget is about minus forty decibels including packages, with about minus seventeen decibels for the one-meter cable.

These values come from different specifications and budget definitions, so they should not be compared as if every row were measured at the same reference plane. Their architectural message is consistent: high-frequency copper loss is severe. Package, board, connector, and cable losses all consume one finite allowance.

When the route grows, the architect can reduce board loss, add conditioning, shorten the cable, or move the electrical-to-optical conversion closer to the chip. The budget forces the placement decision.
