# pronunciation: O/E -> "optical to electrical"; LPO -> "L P O"; NPO -> "N P O"; CPO -> "C P O"; ASIC -> "A sick"
Optical-engine placement decides how much of the path remains electrical.

At the faceplate, a pluggable module gives the best field serviceability. A failed module can be replaced without opening the system. The tradeoff is a long electrical path from the A sick to the front of the box. A linear pluggable can reduce retiming power, but it still depends on that host path.

Placing the engine near a backplane connector shortens the external service motion without solving all of the board loss. N P O moves optical conversion close to the package. The electrical trace becomes shorter and cleaner, while the optical engine remains a separate component on the board.

C P O moves conversion onto the same package or substrate as the A sick. That gives the strongest signal-integrity and energy advantages because the electrical path is extremely short.

The service model moves in the opposite direction. Faceplate optics are easy to replace. N P O may require board removal. C P O can require package or factory-level repair.

The continuum is one boundary moving closer to the source. Power and density improve as electrical reach shrinks. Repair scope and integration risk usually increase.
