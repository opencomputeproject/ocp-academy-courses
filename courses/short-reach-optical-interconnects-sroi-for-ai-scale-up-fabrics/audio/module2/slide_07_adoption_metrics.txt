# pronunciation: pJ/b -> "picojoules per bit"; FIT -> "fit"; SerDes -> "sir-dez"; FEC -> "F E C"
The paper reduces broad adoption to a set of measurable system thresholds.

For optics-only power at one link end, the threshold is single-digit picojoules per bit. The integrated goal is five or less. When sir-dez and optics are counted together, the crossover target is ten or less, with a goal of seven.

Cost must trend toward passive copper on a per-bit basis when the full system is considered. That comparison includes active-copper premiums, cooling, power delivery, fiber infrastructure, and the physical cost of longer copper assemblies.

Hardware reliability must approach two fit per optical link end. Transient reliability aims for zero link flaps in one million device-hours, with zero uncorrectable errors and careful monitoring of the F E C tail.

Latency is expressed as an architectural gain: remove at least one retimer. The paper uses roughly fifty nanoseconds for a retimer and five nanoseconds for each meter of fiber. Ten meters of additional fiber can therefore be offset by eliminating one retiming stage.

These are system targets, not a module specification. A design can only claim success when power, cost, reliability, and latency work together at the required lane count.
