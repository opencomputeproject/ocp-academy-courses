# pronunciation: LTLR -> "L T L R"; RTLR -> "R T L R"; RTRR -> "R T R R"
Retiming restores signal quality by making a new digital decision and transmitting a clean signal onward. That can extend a difficult channel, but every decision stage has a system cost.

A non-retimed path keeps power and latency lowest. It depends on excellent electrical escape and enough end-to-end margin. In the whitepaper’s terminology, a linear transmitter and linear receiver path is L T L R.

A half-retimed path, or R T L R, regenerates part of the signal chain. It provides more margin while avoiding the full penalty of retiming in both directions.

A fully retimed path, or R T R R, gives strong isolation between the host electrical channel and the optical link. It can simplify interoperability and service. The paper also notes roughly three to four times the power of a non-retimed path, plus tens of nanoseconds of latency for each stage.

There is one more subtlety. A retimer makes a hard decision. That decision can lock in an error rather than remove it. In scale-up, where lanes are numerous and retries are costly, the architect must balance power, margin, repair, and error behavior across the whole population.
