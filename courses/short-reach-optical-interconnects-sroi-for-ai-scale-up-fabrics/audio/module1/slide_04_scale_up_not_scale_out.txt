# pronunciation: XPU -> "X P U"; RDMA -> "R D M A"; microsecond -> "microsecond"
Scale-up and scale-out both connect AI compute, but they are not interchangeable tiers.

Scale-up makes multiple accelerators behave like one compute domain. It supports coherent I O or load-and-store operations, where one device can work with another device’s memory under strict ordering rules. The whitepaper uses an indicative reach below thirty meters, a round-trip target below one microsecond, and roughly one thousand gigabytes per second per X P U.

Scale-out connects more independent nodes or Pods. It normally uses message passing over Ethernet or InfiniBand, often with R D M A. The paper’s comparison uses a reach below two kilometers, round trips below ten microseconds, and about fifty gigabytes per second per X P U.

The primary datacenter network is broader again. It connects services, storage, and general endpoints across much larger populations and distances.

These numbers are directional system comparisons, not universal product guarantees. The important distinction is architectural. Scale-up carries tightly coupled memory-semantic work. It must combine extreme bandwidth with very low latency and very high reliability. A shorter version of a conventional network does not automatically meet that need.
