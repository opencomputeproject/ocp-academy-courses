# pronunciation: radix -> "raid-icks"; UALink -> "U A Link"; 212.5 -> "two hundred twelve point five"; 425G -> "four hundred twenty-five G"; 850G -> "eight hundred fifty G"
Raid-icks is the connection count a switch or topology can support. In a scale-up Pod, it directly links physical I O to the number of accelerators that can participate.

The paper gives a simple relationship. Accelerator count is switch I O bandwidth divided by the bandwidth allocated to each link. If the switch has a fixed total bandwidth, narrow links let it reach more accelerators. But each accelerator-to-accelerator path carries less data. Wider links improve communication, while consuming more of the switch’s available I O and reducing the supported population.

UALink two hundred uses lanes at two hundred twelve point five gigabits per second. One lane provides a two hundred twelve point five G link. Two lanes provide four hundred twenty-five G. Four lanes provide eight hundred fifty G. Those choices change both bandwidth per relationship and the number of relationships the switch can support.

Package escape eventually becomes the limiter. The paper identifies a scale-up switch target above one thousand twenty-four links. Higher raid-icks can reduce hops and latency, but it demands enormous linear bandwidth density around the switch package. This is why SROI is not only a cable problem. It is also an I O escape and integration problem.
