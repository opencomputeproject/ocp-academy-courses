The reason SROI matters begins with how large models are trained.

No single accelerator has enough memory or compute to hold and process a frontier model by itself. Training frameworks fracture the work across many devices. Tensor parallelism divides operations inside a layer. Pipeline parallelism assigns groups of layers to different devices. Data, expert, and context parallelism split other dimensions of the model and workload.

Those strategies create frequent synchronization. Accelerators exchange gradients, activations, parameters, and expert traffic through sustained, high-bandwidth transfers. Some collectives require all-reduce or all-gather behavior. Others create sparse all-to-all patterns. The fabric isn’t carrying occasional background traffic. It sits directly in the execution path.

That changes the cost of a slow link. When one X P U waits for data, its compute engines sit idle. When synchronized work waits for one delayed participant, the effect can propagate across the whole Pod.

The scale-up network therefore becomes a first-order performance component. Its bandwidth and latency determine how much useful work the accelerators complete, not simply how quickly two endpoints exchange a file.
