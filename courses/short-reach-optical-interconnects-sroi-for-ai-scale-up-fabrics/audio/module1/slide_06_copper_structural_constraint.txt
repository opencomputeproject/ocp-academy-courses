Copper works well when the physical system fits inside copper’s natural reach.

At current two-hundred-gigabit-class lane rates, the paper places practical passive copper around one to one and a half meters. That can connect accelerator trays and a mid-rack scale-up switch inside a single rack, especially when the design minimizes board loss.

The geometry changes when one Pod grows across racks. Real cable routes don’t follow a straight line between two chips. They pass through connectors, management spaces, rack rails, and service loops. Examples for nearby racks require roughly three to seven meters. Future scale-up domains may grow from sixty-four or seventy-two accelerators toward five hundred, one thousand, or more.

Active copper can extend some channels, but it adds power, cost, cooling, and latency. The paper treats about two and a half to three meters as the likely upper region for conditioned copper at two hundred twelve point five gigabits per second. Beyond that, optics becomes the likely architecture.

The transition is reach-driven. Optical interconnect lets the compute domain grow without forcing every rack boundary to become a compute boundary.
