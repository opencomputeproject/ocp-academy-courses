The animation shows a conceptual example of coordinated protection. A change in rack load draws support from local energy storage. Later, protection isolates a faulted branch while the healthy cooling branch stays online. That is the aim of selectivity.

Bus capacitors can discharge quickly into a D C fault. Inductance, converter behavior, and the location of energy storage all shape the current seen by the protective device. Solid state breakers, hybrid devices, and electromechanical devices involve different tradeoffs in speed, losses, and coordination.

Upstream conversion could use a conventional transformer and rectifier or a solid state transformer. The design review should check isolation and fault behavior for the actual conversion path. Storage is also a system choice. Rack-level storage can buffer fast changes in I T load. Larger storage at the distribution level can support ride-through or help the facility manage changes at the utility interface. Those roles can work together without assuming every installation needs the same response time.
