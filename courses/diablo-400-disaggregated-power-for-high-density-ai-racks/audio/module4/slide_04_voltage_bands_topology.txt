Low voltage, in the codes and standards surveyed by the white paper, goes up to fifteen hundred volts D C. Within that category, nominal voltage names the system, the rating describes how equipment is qualified, and operating limits specify what the device can see for a given time. A proposed voltage band in the white paper is a design reference. Use the applicable specification and equipment ratings for the installation.

Polarity and earthing also affect the equipment choice. A symmetrical bipolar system has positive and negative rails around a midpoint. The arrangement affects voltage to earth, whether the midpoint is carried to a load, and which poles must be protected.

In a T N S system, the source is solidly earthed and protective earth is separate from a current carrying midpoint, when present. In an I T system, the source is isolated from earth or connected through high impedance. Insulation monitoring and the planned first fault response matter there. Check the actual configuration rather than assuming a pole to pole voltage tells you the voltage to earth.
