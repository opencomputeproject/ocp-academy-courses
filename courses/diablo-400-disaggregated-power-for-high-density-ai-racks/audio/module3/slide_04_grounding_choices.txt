Grounding starts with two separate questions: what is the voltage between output conductors, and how are those conductors referenced to protective earth, or P E?

For the bipolar plus-minus 400 volts DC architecture, the midpoint may connect to protective earth through high resistance or through a solid connection. Version one dot zero dot zero explicitly allows three-wire routing plus protective earth, or two-wire routing plus protective earth with no midpoint routed to the IT rack. A two-wire cable does not, by itself, tell you where the earth reference is.

The specification also describes 800 volt distribution with two conductors and protective earth. The return reference may use high-resistance grounding for a floating output, or a solid connection for a single-ended 800 volt output. Differential 800 volts describes the voltage between the conductors. It does not mean every conductor has the same voltage to earth in every architecture.

Fault detection is a significant update. Ground-fault detection is required for both solid midpoint grounding and high-resistance midpoint grounding. The high-resistance midpoint implementation also needs voltage-balance detection. Residual-current detection is required at each output cable or connection to the IT rack.

Appendix A provides non-normative grounding tradeoffs. High resistance can limit a first earth-fault current, but requires reliable detection and a timely response. Solid grounding requires fault-current coordination and a protective bond that can carry fault current until protection opens. Neither approach removes shock risk between output poles.

Review the selected wiring, grounding, insulation, detection, and downstream converter compatibility as one system. The architecture descriptions in section eight must be reconciled with the power supply isolation requirements for the implementation being qualified.
