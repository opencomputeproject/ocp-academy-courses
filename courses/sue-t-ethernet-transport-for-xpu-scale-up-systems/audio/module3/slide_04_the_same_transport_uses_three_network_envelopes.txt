# pronunciation: SUE-T -> "S U E T"; XPU -> "X P U"; ACK -> "ack"; NACK -> "knack"; PDC -> "P D C"; PSN -> "P S N"; AXI -> "A X I"; AxID -> "A X I identifier"; AxUSER -> "A X user"; WSTRB -> "write strobe"; UET -> "U E T"; RODL -> "R O D L"
S U E T can run over three Ethernet network envelopes. These are Ethernet for Scale-Up Networks, or E S U N, the Unified Forwarding Header or U F H, and User Datagram Protocol, or U D P, over Internet Protocol, or I P, over Ethernet. The transport semantics remain above the chosen network format.

The destination network address is typically configured in the transport instance and looked up from information supplied by the accelerator. A destination accelerator identifier can, for example, select the destination media-access-control, or M A C, address or I P address. In the U F H case, that identifier may be used directly.

The receiver also needs to recognize the transport. For U D P over I P, the specification identifies Ultra Ethernet through destination port four seven nine three. It also gives EtherType hexadecimal E seven nine zero as identifying Ultra Ethernet in the described EtherType case.

With E S U N, the presence of the Ultra Ethernet transport is inferred through network-wide configuration. That is a configuration assumption that the system has to establish consistently.

Within the transport header, the packet-delivery type field identifies the selected Ultra Ethernet Transport, or U E T, type, such as Reliable Ordered Delivery for Local networks, or R O D L. Network identification and packet-delivery selection therefore answer different questions.

When integrating an endpoint, verify the selected envelope, the mapping from the accelerator identifier to network addressing, and the receive-side identification rule. The three source diagrams are useful precisely because they show the same transport payload carried inside different network headers.
