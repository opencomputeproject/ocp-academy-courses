Put the rule to work. An eligible destination queue contains one store command. The port can transmit it, and the relevant flow-control conditions allow service. A second command for that destination may arrive later, but it hasn't arrived yet.

Should the scheduler hold the first command to improve wire efficiency? Decide before continuing the explanation. The specification says a single transaction in a selected destination queue is sent. Transactions aren't delayed for packing. A possible future packing partner is not a reason to keep currently eligible work waiting.

Now change one condition. Flow control prevents service of that queue. The work must wait until the queue becomes eligible. That waiting has a defined cause: flow control. If additional commands arrive while it waits, the packing stage may later find several ready together. Their accumulation does not turn the original wait into a packing policy.

Finally, suppose several commands are available but together exceed a configured packet limit. The scheduler can transmit the commands that fit. It cannot ignore the limit merely because a larger group would amortize headers more efficiently.

The useful diagnostic is to name the cause of waiting. Queue eligibility, buffer constraints, flow-control state, and scheduling among eligible work belong in that explanation. Waiting solely for a fuller packet does not. This gives you a concrete way to distinguish a latency-preserving packing implementation from a batching assumption that the specification does not make.
