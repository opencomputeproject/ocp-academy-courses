We'll connect three parts of the packet-delivery model. The first is the queue: which destination and traffic class own the work, and when is that queue eligible? The second is the packet: which available commands fit together, and what limits constrain packing? The third is the connection: which sequence number does the receiver expect, and what happens if it doesn't arrive?

Two short cases will ask you to predict behavior before the explanation. One involves an eligible queue with only one command. The other involves a packet missing from an ordered sequence. We'll finish with connection scaling and load distribution. Throughout, keep the request and its response separate from the packet that happens to carry them.
