# pronunciation: SUE-T -> "S U E T"; XPU -> "X P U"; UET -> "U E T"; ESUN -> "E S U N"; UFH -> "U F H"; UDP -> "U D P"; IP -> "I P"
Start at the top of the stack, where the accelerator issues a memory transaction. S U E T accepts the transaction through its command interface, maps the control information, and assembles the transport data needed to reach another accelerator.

The transport uses the Unified Local profile of Ultra Ethernet Transport, or U E T. Its reliability and semantic layers give the network packet information about the connection, the command, and the associated data.

Below that transport, the specification provides three network options. These are Ethernet for Scale-Up Networks, or E S U N; the Unified Forwarding Header, or U F H; and User Datagram Protocol over Internet Protocol over Ethernet, usually called U D P over I P. The network envelope changes while the transport continues to serve the accelerator's transactions.

This layering also tells us where integration responsibilities belong. Memory registration and protection-domain management are outside S U E T. Address translation can take place in the command-mapping function or within the accelerator, but the translation service itself is accelerator-specific and outside the transport definition.

The Ethernet fabric provides the service that the transport relies on. S U E T requires lossless network service, and the packet-delivery module will explain the flow-control and retry choices around that requirement.

When you inspect an implementation, identify these boundaries first: the accelerator's memory system, the transport endpoint, and the network beneath it. Each contributes a different part of the behavior.
