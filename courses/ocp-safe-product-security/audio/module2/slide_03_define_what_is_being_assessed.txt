Two scoping decisions are easy to confuse. The first is what you're asking the provider to assess. That might be a complete device, a particular component, or a change relative to an earlier reviewed baseline. The second is the depth of review, expressed through Scopes one, two, and three.

A component review isn't automatically a Scope one review. A small security component can require physical-attack analysis, while a larger system can contain several areas with different assessment needs.

For a firmware update, a differential review focuses attention on the changes and their implications. The provider needs an agreed baseline and enough information to understand what changed, including relevant dependencies and interactions. It isn't a promise that every update will be a small or inexpensive assessment.

Prepare a brief that separates the product boundary from the requested depth. Then discuss both with the approved provider and the customers who will depend on the result.
