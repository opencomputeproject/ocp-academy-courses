First, agree the scope and the access needed to evaluate it. The vendor, provider, and relevant customers need a shared understanding of the product, firmware, and assessment boundary.

The independent provider then examines the architecture, code, and supporting evidence, and identifies findings within that scope.

The vendor addresses the findings. That can mean changing code, strengthening a design, establishing and documenting a secure supported configuration, or preparing other appropriate remediation.

The provider checks the changes and verifies the claimed fixes. Vendor action and independent verification are separate responsibilities.

Sometimes verification shows that more work is needed. The finding returns to the vendor, and the provider evaluates the revised response. This visible return is part of the review process.

The final evidence records the reviewed state, outcomes, limitations, and any remaining reportable findings. A final report doesn't mean every concern has disappeared.

Engaging during design can make this work more useful, because an architectural issue is easier to address before a device is committed to production.
