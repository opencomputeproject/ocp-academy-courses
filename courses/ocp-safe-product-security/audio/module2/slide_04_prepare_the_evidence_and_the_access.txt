Start with the product's purpose and intended use. Give the provider a clear architecture description, the relevant components, and the assets and interfaces that matter to security. A threat model helps connect those details to realistic adversaries and attack surfaces.

Prepare the source code, firmware information, and build material needed for white-box review. Explain third-party dependencies and supply a software bill of materials, often called an S bom. The provider can then understand which components are present and how inherited issues may affect the product.

Access and confidentiality need practical arrangements too. Agree how the provider will inspect the material, which environment will be used, and the appropriate engagement and confidentiality terms. If a threat model is missing, discuss how it will be developed as part of the work.

Use O C P's maintained list of approved providers. The goal is a provider with the technical capability and access needed for this product, supported by a preparation set that makes the assessment possible.
