A threat model describes the assets to protect, the adversaries and their capabilities, and the ways those adversaries might reach the system. The review scope should follow those concerns.

Scope one is a code and architecture assessment. It includes white-box examination of firmware and its security-sensitive behavior, informed by the device's design and threat model. Examples include secure boot, handling of secrets, input validation, and privileged interfaces.

Scope two includes those areas and looks more deeply at trust boundaries. Think about isolation between application processing and a security processor, or the protections used when data and control cross those boundaries.

Scope three adds resilience to physical attacks. Fault injection and side-channel resistance become important when an attacker can interact physically with security-critical components.

These aren't three pass grades. They describe increasing assessment depth for the threat model. A device can have different components assessed at different depths. The provider and vendor agree the boundary and the applicable work, rather than assigning one universal depth to every part.
