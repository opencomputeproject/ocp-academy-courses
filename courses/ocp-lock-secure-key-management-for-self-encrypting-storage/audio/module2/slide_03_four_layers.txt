The hierarchy is easier to understand when it is built from ownership toward the encryption engine.

Start with the Data Protection Key. The nominal owner of the data controls this value. In an Opal or Enterprise flow, drive firmware may recover it from a credential-protected structure. In Key Per I O, it may be the imported data-encryption key associated with a key tag. One D P K binds to a given Media Encryption Key.

Next come zero or more Multi-party Protection Keys. Each M P K represents an additional authority that must assent before the media key can be loaded. The order matters because the K M B mixes the enabled M P Ks into the secret one at a time. Using zero M P Ks preserves compatibility with ordinary Opal, Enterprise, or Key Per I O flows.

Then add the epoch layer. The Hard Epoch Key and Soft Epoch Key enter an S P eight hundred one-oh-eight K D F to produce the Epoch Protection Key. The E P K, D P K, and configured M P Ks produce the Media Encryption Key secret.

Finally, the Media Encryption Key Deobfuscation Key protects the hardware path used to place a random or derived media key into the Key Vault and then the encryption engine. It prevents compromised runtime firmware from turning a permitted decrypt operation into a key-disclosure path.

At the twenty twenty-six O C P E M E A Summit, Carl Lundin summarized the visual complexity with a memorable line: “it's just keys all the way down.” The engineering point is precise. Every layer has a different owner, lifetime, and failure consequence.
