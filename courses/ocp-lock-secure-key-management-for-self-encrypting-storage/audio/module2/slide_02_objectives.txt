# pronunciation: OCP -> "O C P"; L.O.C.K. -> "lock"; CSP -> "C S P"; MEK -> "meck"; MPK -> "M P K"; HEK -> "heck"; SEK -> "seck"; VEK -> "veck"; MDK -> "M D K"; HPKE -> "H P K E"
By the end of this module, you'll be able to trace the lock key hierarchy without treating it as one undifferentiated key ladder.

You'll identify who supplies or controls each protection key and explain whether that value is durable, volatile, or ephemeral. You'll distinguish a Locked M P K from an Enabled M P K and explain why H P K E protects its access key in transit.

You'll follow the Hard and Soft Epoch Key lifecycle, including the fuse states and ordering rules that make zeroization meaningful. You'll then compare a randomly generated, double-wrapped Media Encryption Key with a deterministic derived key and its optional checksum.

Finally, you'll connect the hierarchy to operational choices. A soft epoch transition can support a tenant boundary. A hard epoch transition supports a device leaving its previous trust environment. Those choices make sense only after the relationships among the keys are clear.
