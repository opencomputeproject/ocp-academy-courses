A demonstration can show that an integration path works without settling whether it is beneficial for a particular deployment. Build an experiment that could change your decision, including the possibility that offloading does not help.

Start with a suitable baseline and the same useful task. Record hardware, software, model or dataset, data representation, and relevant workload settings. Use equivalent correctness requirements. For approximate retrieval, compare the required quality as well as performance so a different answer is not mistaken for faster execution of the same task.

Account for setup, loading, and conversions. Then measure the execution path: bytes crossing relevant boundaries, end-to-end latency, useful throughput, and energy where available. Distinguish a one-time preparation cost from a cost paid on every request. Repeated reuse and cold-start behavior may tell different stories.

Add one difficult condition. What happens when an operation is unsupported, a device fails, or fallback is required? Identify the expected behavior and what evidence would demonstrate it. These are review questions for the broader framework, rather than guarantees supplied by the demonstrations.

The draft includes a vector-search bandwidth illustration. A bandwidth ratio is not automatically an application speedup or an energy saving. Explain the relationship that a measurement establishes, keep the system boundary visible, and report limitations that would matter to another contributor trying to reproduce the result.
