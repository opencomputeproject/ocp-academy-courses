A link flaps. Begin with the evidence, then choose the action.

Check the host- and media-side ratings for the relevant endpoints. Read every lowest-category contributor, especially when several categories tie. Inspect the measurements, masks, and effective thresholds behind the summary.

Then retrieve the event history. Was the drop preceded by an optical-power alarm, receiver condition, reset, firmware action, or configured autonomous shutdown? A shutdown record identifies the affected side, port, category, and value rather than leaving only a generic link-down symptom.

Query the remote partner through the local module, and compare its evidence with the host timeline. Confirm accessibility and time validity before treating missing or misaligned data as a healthy result.

The resulting sequence narrows the possibilities: optical-path loss, electrical margin, thermal conditions, firmware behavior, a remote endpoint issue, or an intentional configured response.

Record the action and the evidence that justified it. Cleaning, replacing a module, changing configuration, or opening a vendor case should follow a reasoned interpretation of the full path.
