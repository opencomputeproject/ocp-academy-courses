By the end of this module, you will be able to connect measurements to operating decisions.

First, you will distinguish local and remote queries, and explain why software discovers module capability before interpreting data. The specification release number and the module's telemetry-version register are different identifiers.

Second, you will classify signal quality, error and correction behavior, recovery counters, environment, and module identity. You will keep the host and media sides separate so a measurement remains tied to its interface.

Third, you will explain the health score as the minimum of supported category scores. You will also use the lowest-category bitmap to identify every category tied for the worst result.

Finally, you will distinguish flags, counter masks, threshold configuration, and autonomous shutdown. These controls have different purposes and defaults. A summary score is only interpretable when software knows which counters participate and which thresholds are effective.

The goal is to recognize what a value means, where it came from, and what follow-up evidence it suggests.
