# pronunciation: AI -> "A I"; GPU -> "G P U"; GPUs -> "G P Yous"; AICC -> "A I C C"
A research park provides a useful end-to-end A I C C scenario.

Several startups share local laboratory and data facilities. Normal inference demand fits within the site's own systems, but a new experiment creates a short, compute-intensive peak. Sensitive research data must remain under the park's governance, and purchasing enough local G P Yous for every peak would be uneconomic.

The animation begins with resource discovery. A remote accelerator service reports compatible hardware, available capacity, power conditions, and expected service behavior. Before any workload is placed, attestation and policy checks establish whether the environment is approved for the model and data.

Connectivity is then established with the required bandwidth and isolation. Model components and permitted data move according to policy. Telemetry reports queue depth, memory pressure, latency, energy, and faults while the service runs.

Demand rises at another location, and available capacity changes. The scheduler adjusts new work without interrupting established sessions. Then the wide-area link is degraded. The park's local system preserves a limited service mode while remote work is paused, redirected, or recovered according to its state requirements.

This one scenario connects many continuum concerns: remote G P U access, sovereignty, resource discovery, trust, dynamic connectivity, service objectives, energy-aware placement, telemetry, and graceful degradation.

A I C C does not yet supply one common implementation of that experience. The scenario helps the community identify which open interfaces and reference designs are still needed.
