# pronunciation: OCP -> "O C P"; IOWN -> "eye-own"; AI -> "A I"; PoC -> "proof of concept"
O C P and the IOWN Global Forum approach the continuum from complementary directions.

O C P brings an open hardware ecosystem. Its communities develop specifications, design guidelines, reference architectures, and accepted contributions that suppliers can implement. For A I C C, that work can cover servers, racks, building blocks, cooling, power, wireless components, connectivity, manageability, and the interfaces between them.

The IOWN Global Forum develops communications architecture and works from industry use cases. It brings together requirements from sectors such as financial services, manufacturing, and telecommunications, then develops reference designs, proof-of-concept activity, and techno-economic analysis.

The organizations meet in the middle. Use cases and service requirements help identify the interfaces that hardware and communications specifications must support. Candidate architectures can be tested through proofs of concept. Results can expose performance, operational, or economic gaps. Mature work can then inform contributions, products, and broader adoption.

This relationship prevents two common failures. A specification without a credible use case may optimize the wrong problem. A use case without open interfaces may produce a one-off integration that can't develop into a multi-vendor ecosystem.

The joint opportunity is to connect user needs, architecture, validation, and implementation without requiring one organization to own the entire stack.
