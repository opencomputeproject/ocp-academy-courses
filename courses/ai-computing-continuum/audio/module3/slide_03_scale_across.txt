# pronunciation: AI -> "A I"; GPU -> "G P U"; GPUs -> "G P Yous"
Scale-Across addresses capacity that can't, or shouldn't, remain inside one location.

A region may be limited by available grid power, cooling infrastructure, space, or the time required to build a new facility. Another region may have usable accelerator capacity or a better energy situation. Scale-Across treats those locations as parts of a broader resource pool.

The workload could be divided, run in parallel, or moved according to demand. A large batch-inference job might run where capacity is available. Multiple regions might process independent portions of a dataset. An interactive service could direct new sessions toward a healthy region while respecting data and latency policy.

Distance changes the economics and the engineering. Model weights and datasets may be expensive to move. A multi-node job can become sensitive to bandwidth and tail latency. Spare G P Yous in another region are not useful if the network prevents the service from meeting its objective.

Scale-Across therefore requires more than a map of available devices. It needs resource discovery, topology and capability information, data-placement awareness, secure access, telemetry, and a policy for when to move compute, data, or both.

The defining question is: can geographic distribution supply useful capacity while preserving the workload's performance, governance, reliability, and energy goals?
