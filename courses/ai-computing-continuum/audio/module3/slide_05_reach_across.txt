# pronunciation: AI -> "A I"; GPU -> "G P U"; GPUs -> "G P Yous"; GPU-as-a-Service -> "G P U as a Service"
Reach-Across connects a local user or application to an A I capability located elsewhere.

Imagine a research park whose startups need high-end accelerators for short experiments. Purchasing enough local capacity for every peak would leave expensive equipment underused. Uploading sensitive research data into a remote operator's environment may also be unacceptable.

A Reach-Across design could discover suitable remote G P U capacity, establish approved connectivity, and provide the service while data remains under the required governance. The request may still move data across the link, but the architecture makes that movement explicit and controlled rather than requiring a permanent preloaded copy.

The same pattern can support an enterprise application, an industrial site, a mobile environment, or an immersive service. The important requirement is real-time reach to a resource beyond the local deployment.

Current A I C C material describes on-demand data movement, pay-as-you-compute resources and connectivity, zero-trust sharing, and a find-connect-compute experience. These are target capabilities, not claims about a completed common platform.

Reach-Across succeeds only when discovery, attestation, bandwidth, latency, sovereignty, availability, and cost work together. The defining question is: can the remote resource feel operationally usable from the point where the service is consumed?
