# pronunciation: IOWN -> "eye-own"; AI -> "A I"
By the end of this module, you'll be able to distinguish the three Across patterns without relying on their names alone.

You'll recognize Scale-Across when the main need is additional or movable capacity across geographic regions.

You'll recognize Sync-Across when a service depends on data or system state remaining consistent across sites within a defined timing objective.

You'll recognize Reach-Across when a user, device, or local application needs real-time access to an A I resource located elsewhere.

You'll also judge the enabling requirements. These include bandwidth, latency, jitter, availability, security, manageability, and data-sovereignty policy.

Finally, you'll connect the patterns to measurable service objectives. Time to first token, token latency, tail latency, throughput, recovery behavior, energy, and cost can determine whether a remote design helps or merely moves the bottleneck.

The model is selected by the workload need, not by a preferred network technology.
