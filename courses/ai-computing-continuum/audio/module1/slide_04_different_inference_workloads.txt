The word edge is useful, but it compresses too many different environments into one label. A telecom point of presence, a corporate data center, a factory, an office, and a remote device can all be called edge. Their infrastructure needs are not interchangeable.

A I C C uses hyperscale adjacent to describe this broader spectrum outside centralized hyperscale data centers. Location is only half of the picture. The workload also matters.

Batch inference can favor throughput and energy efficiency over immediate response. Interactive services care about time to first token and the pace of the tokens that follow. Agentic systems may carry a growing conversation, call tools, and reuse a large working context. Multimodal services combine text, images, audio, or sensor data. Physical A I can require predictable response and safe local behavior. Small, quantized models may fit on constrained devices, while large reasoning models may span multiple accelerators or nodes.

These workload profiles compete for different resources. Some are dominated by compute. Others are limited by memory capacity, memory bandwidth, network behavior, or a strict power envelope.

The continuum therefore isn't a ladder leading to one ideal machine. It's a set of environments and specialized resources that can be matched to the service objective. That distinction will help us judge when local execution, regional capacity, or remote reach is the better design.
