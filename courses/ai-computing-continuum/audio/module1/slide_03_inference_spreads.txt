# pronunciation: AI -> "A I"; GPU -> "G P U"; GPUs -> "G P Yous"
Training helped drive the build-out of very large A I clusters. Inference creates a different kind of expansion because requests originate wherever people, devices, applications, and operational data are found.

Consider an industrial inspection system. A camera produces a continuous stream of images on a factory floor. Some decisions may need a small local model because the response must arrive immediately, even if the wide-area connection is unavailable. A more complex case may be sent to a nearby enterprise or telecom site with greater accelerator capacity. Large retrospective analysis can move to regional or hyperscale infrastructure, where many jobs can share substantial compute resources.

Now change the setting. A research park may need temporary access to a remote G P U while keeping sensitive datasets under local control. An office assistant may tolerate a regional round trip, but an autonomous radio or physical-control system may not. Each request brings a different combination of latency, data movement, privacy, availability, and energy constraints.

The animation follows those decisions as conditions change. It isn't showing a fixed hierarchy in which every request travels toward the largest data center. Some work stays local. Some reaches outward. Some moves between regions.

That changing placement is the heart of the continuum. The challenge is to let these environments work together through open, discoverable, and manageable infrastructure.
