# pronunciation: AI -> "A I"; GPU -> "G P U"
For years, the dominant response to growing A I demand has been scale. Add more accelerators within a system. Add more systems within a cluster. Build larger data centers.

That remains important, but it doesn't resolve every infrastructure constraint. A region may lack enough power for another large facility. An enterprise may need its data to remain in a specific jurisdiction. A real-time application may need compute closer to the point of use. A smaller site may own enough capacity for normal demand but not for a short peak.

Connectivity creates another design option. Instead of forcing every workload and dataset into one location, a system can reach suitable resources across locations. A research team might connect local protected data to temporary remote G P U capacity. A service could shift work toward a region with available power or cooling headroom. Two sites might coordinate state so one can continue when the other is interrupted.

But connectivity isn't a magic tunnel. The architecture must account for bandwidth, latency, jitter, availability, security, sovereignty, and the cost and energy of moving data. A remote resource is useful only if the entire service can still meet its objective.

That is why the central A I C C idea joins scale with reachability. Compute capacity and communications become parts of one placement decision.
