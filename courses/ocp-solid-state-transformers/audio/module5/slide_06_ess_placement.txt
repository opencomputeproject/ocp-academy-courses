Storage placement changes the system boundary. Facility-level ESS may coordinate near the medium-voltage or central DC conversion layer. Row, rack, or sidecar storage sits closer to the load and changes local fault domains, maintenance access, cabling, and fire-protection needs. Architectures may be DC-coupled or AC-coupled, distributed or centralized, autonomous or coordinated, backup-only or grid-interactive. None of these classifications is just vocabulary. Placement changes who owns charging limits, protection, telemetry, emergency response, code compliance, and approval evidence. This course uses the categories to coordinate with the SST. The companion OCP Ready ESS course provides the full deployment, technology, hazard, standards, and OCP Ready treatment.

Charging deserves explicit treatment in every placement. A rack-level battery may recharge through local conversion and add to the rack or sidecar demand. A centralized DC-coupled ESS may add demand directly to the common bus. An AC-coupled facility ESS may appear as a separate load at the upstream switchgear. Coordinated control can distribute recharge over time and across zones. Maintenance and emergency response also change with placement: isolation boundaries, signage, detection, ventilation, fire suppression, access, and replacement procedures follow the physical energy storage. OCP Ready evidence should match the actual deployment, not only the battery technology name.
