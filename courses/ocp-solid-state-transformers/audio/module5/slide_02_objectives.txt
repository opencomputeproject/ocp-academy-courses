By the end of this module, you should be able to compare the Appendix A redundancy patterns, explain how output grounding affects insulation monitoring and protection, assign SST and ESS functions across response timescales, recognize how storage placement changes fault, charging, maintenance, fire-protection, and approval boundaries, and create a verification checklist that distinguishes product requirements from system assumptions. You should also know where to continue learning through the public OCP Power Distribution work, the LVDC white paper, and the Diablo 400 and OCP Ready ESS Academy courses.

The final objective is not to choose one universal architecture. It is to expose the decisions and evidence needed for a defensible one. Two sites can use the same SST SKU and reach different redundancy or storage choices because their load criticality, utility service, available space, fire strategy, maintenance model, and grid-service goals differ. Interoperability comes from clear interfaces and testable behavior, not from making every facility identical. The public OCP documents and companion courses provide a shared vocabulary for that multi-disciplinary work.
