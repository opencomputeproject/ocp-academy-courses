Different functions live on different timescales. Local capacitance responds in microseconds to milliseconds and supports switching and very fast load edges. Converter controls act over milliseconds to cycles to regulate voltage and limit current. Energy storage can fill longer gaps, support voltage ride-through, provide backup, smooth ramps, and potentially participate in grid services when the architecture and interconnection permit it. After discharge, recharge operates over minutes or longer and must be coordinated with site demand. An SST plus ESS can behave like a medium-voltage UPS, but that is a system configuration—not proof that every SST contains storage. Define the service, response time, energy duration, power rating, and owner for each layer.

Power rating and energy capacity answer different questions. A storage system may deliver high power for a short ride-through interval yet lack energy for extended backup. Another system may store substantial energy but respond too slowly for a fast bus transient. Hybrid storage can combine technologies across those needs, while controls allocate the service. Grid-interactive operation adds metering, interconnection, dispatch, cybersecurity, and state-of-charge obligations beyond backup-only use. The OCP Ready ESS course examines technology and hazard evidence in detail. Here, the key is to define the service at the SST interface before choosing the storage architecture.
