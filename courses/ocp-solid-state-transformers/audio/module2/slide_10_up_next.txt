You can now reason about the technology without over-reading the topology. Module three turns to the specification tables themselves. We will examine the medium-voltage SKUs, exact eight-hundred-volt output requirements, efficiency and physical envelope, environment, auxiliary power, communications, compliance standards, and deliberate open items. This is where the draft becomes a product contract—and where careful reading matters most.

The next module will deliberately slow down around units and qualifiers. Values such as percent tolerance, milliseconds, megawatts, kiloamps, and kilovolts describe different phenomena and cannot be compared casually. Terms such as target, recommended, may, and TBD change the force of a value. In a real project, capture those words in the requirements database alongside the number. A copied value without its status can create a false acceptance criterion or conceal an unresolved interface.
