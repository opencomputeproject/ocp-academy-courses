This animation shows one representative three-stage path. First, an active front end converts medium-voltage three-phase AC into a controlled DC link. Second, a switched bridge drives a medium- or high-frequency transformer, providing galvanic isolation and a different voltage ratio in a much smaller magnetic structure. Third, an output conversion stage regulates the unipolar eight-hundred-volt DC bus. Sensors and controls surround all three stages. The OCP document does not mandate these exact circuit blocks or their count. It specifies the observable product behavior. An active input stage may also support power-factor control and harmonic performance. The isolated stage establishes the safety and voltage-conversion barrier, often using modular cells to manage device voltage and power. The output stage may be arranged to regulate the bus and contribute controlled fault current. A DC link between stages buffers short-term energy imbalance, but its capacitance and stored energy also affect inrush and fault clearing. At megawatt scale, topology choices influence how many modules are required, how failures are bypassed, and whether maintenance can occur without removing the entire product. Those are supplier decisions until they change a promised external behavior.
