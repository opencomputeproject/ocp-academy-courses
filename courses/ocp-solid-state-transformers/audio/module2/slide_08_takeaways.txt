The SST changes both the power path and the control boundary. It replaces the line-frequency transformer and downstream rectification concept with switched conversion and high-frequency isolation. OCP organizes the product into five components so interfaces and responsibilities remain reviewable. A three-stage path is a useful mental model, but not a mandatory topology. Silicon carbide, potential future gallium nitride, and medium- or high-frequency magnetics enable power density while introducing new EMI, insulation, thermal, and reliability work. The product succeeds only when those tradeoffs are validated at the required megawatt scale.

Notice the recurring pattern: every benefit introduces a verification question. Smaller magnetics prompt thermal and insulation tests. Active regulation prompts transient and control-stability tests. Modularity prompts failure-containment and service tests. Telemetry prompts data-quality, availability, and security review. Higher efficiency prompts measurement across load, temperature, and operating state. This does not argue against SSTs. It explains why a community specification is useful. By naming the shared boundary, OCP lets different internal approaches be evaluated against comparable external evidence.
