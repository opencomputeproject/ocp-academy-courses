Module two opens the SST technology black box. We will compare the line-frequency transformer and rectifier chain with a power-electronic alternative, then organize the SST into the five components named by the OCP specification. A representative three-stage conversion path will make the energy flow concrete. Remember the discipline for this module: the diagram explains a plausible implementation, while the OCP specification controls the interfaces and required behavior. A supplier may reach those interfaces with a different internal topology.

The word transformer can create a misleading mental picture. A solid state transformer still performs voltage conversion and electrical isolation, but it does so through multiple actively switched stages rather than only a line-frequency magnetic core. That means firmware and semiconductor control become inseparable from the electrical product. It also means loss, cooling, protection, sensing, and cybersecurity considerations move with the conversion boundary. Throughout the module, ask what new control capability is gained and what new dependency or failure mode accompanies it.
