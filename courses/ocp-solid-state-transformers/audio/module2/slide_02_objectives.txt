By the end of this module, you should be able to do three things. First, distinguish a conventional line-frequency transformer and rectifier path from an SST. Second, name the five OCP product components and explain the job performed by each. Third, follow a representative three-stage energy path and connect wide-bandgap switching and high-frequency magnetics to size, efficiency, thermal, reliability, and certification tradeoffs. You should also be able to say clearly which parts are explanatory technology context and which parts are actually required at the black-box boundary.

You do not need to design a converter to meet these objectives. The intended level is architectural: identify the purpose of a stage, understand why high-frequency isolation changes size and control, and recognize the questions that belong in an interface or acceptance review. For example, a platform engineer may care about load steps and ride-through, while a facility engineer may care about source grounding and fault duty. A supplier must connect both views inside one product. The five-component model gives those stakeholders a shared language without requiring them to use the same internal design.
