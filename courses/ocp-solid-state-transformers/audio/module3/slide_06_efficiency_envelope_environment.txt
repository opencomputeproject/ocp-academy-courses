The power-train efficiency target is at least ninety-eight percent from fifty to one hundred percent utilization. The product envelope is roughly ten to twenty feet long, eight feet deep, and eight and a half feet high. It may be installed indoors in conditioned space or outdoors. Outdoor equipment targets NEMA three R, at least a fifteen-year life, minus thirty to forty degrees Celsius before high-temperature derating, C five corrosion exposure, ninety-five percent non-condensing humidity, and operation through one thousand meters before altitude derating. Structural data address transport, seismic, snow, and wind loads. Auxiliary source one is external four-hundred-eighty-volt AC backed by the facility UPS for control and telemetry. Source two is generated internally for semiconductor actuation and cooling. Communications use Modbus TCP/IP.

Efficiency should be measured at the product boundary and under agreed auxiliary-load treatment. At five megawatts, two percent loss is one hundred kilowatts; at ten megawatts it is two hundred kilowatts. That heat must be rejected continuously near the conversion equipment. Environmental limits then affect available power and cooling margin. Outdoor installations add enclosure, corrosion, precipitation, wind, snow, solar, condensation, and service-access concerns. The external backed auxiliary source keeps telemetry and control available during facility events, while the internal source supports switching actuation and cooling tied to the converter. Test both sources and their transition behavior, not only normal operation.
