By the end of this module, you should be able to identify both medium-voltage input SKUs and their output power options, interpret the unipolar eight-hundred-volt DC requirements, describe the product’s efficiency, size, environment, auxiliary power, and communications envelope, and recognize where the draft uses recommended values or TBD placeholders. You should also be able to use the listed NFPA, UL, and IEEE references as a compliance map without assuming that a list of standards replaces an authority-having-jurisdiction review or a complete certification plan.

A practical outcome is the ability to create a traceable interface checklist. For each requirement, record the source page, nominal value, tolerance, operating range, duration, load condition, and status. Then identify who supplies evidence: the SST vendor, site electrical designer, ESS integrator, protection engineer, or authority having jurisdiction. Requirements that cross those ownership lines deserve early test planning. The output grounding choice and downstream fault-current window are good examples because product capability and application coordination meet in the same row.
