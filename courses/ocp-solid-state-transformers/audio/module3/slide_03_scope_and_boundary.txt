The specification applies to a medium-voltage solid state transformer product. It defines a supplier-deliverable black box with a medium-voltage AC input, a regulated low-voltage DC output, controls, communications, auxiliary power, and stated environmental capability. Internal conversion choices remain with the supplier. Application architecture remains with the operator and integrator. Energy storage illustrates the boundary: the document says an MVSST coupled with an ESS could function as a medium-voltage UPS, but it does not say that every SST includes storage. Appendix A shows application examples and explicitly marks them non-normative. Treat the product tables as the contract and the appendix as design context.

The distinction affects acceptance responsibility. A vendor can demonstrate the converter’s output regulation, efficiency, communications, and environmental rating. The operator must demonstrate that the selected redundancy pattern, busway, storage, and protection scheme uses those capabilities safely. A system integrator may coordinate the tests, but that does not move every application choice into the product specification. When reviewing Appendix A, ask which product assumptions the drawing depends on and which application parameters are unstated. The gaps become inputs to the facility design basis and integrated test plan.
