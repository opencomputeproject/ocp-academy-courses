The compliance section is best read as a map. NFPA references cover electrical installation, electrical safety work practices, and signaling. UL references span medium-voltage conversion equipment, power conversion, insulation coordination, grid-interactive equipment, controls, and arc-fault topics. IEEE references span seismic qualification, arc-flash calculation, interconnection, energy storage, harmonics, controls, and grounding practices. Applicability must be confirmed for the actual product and jurisdiction. At the same time, v0.3 leaves important items open: detailed output grounding, load profiles and step definitions, some fault durations, generator and renewable ramp behavior, redundancy voltage levels, and future verification criteria. Draft maturity is therefore part of the technical interpretation.

Standards references can overlap. A product may need component certification, equipment-level evaluation, installation-code compliance, arc-flash analysis, harmonic performance, seismic qualification, and grid-interconnection review at the same time. The authority having jurisdiction decides local applicability, while the supplier and design team provide evidence. Create a compliance matrix with scope, edition, test body, responsible party, and open interpretation. Do the same for TBDs. Some will become product revisions, some will become project-specific agreements, and some may require shared test data before the community can set one value.
