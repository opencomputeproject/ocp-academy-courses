AI infrastructure is turning rack power into an architectural constraint. SemiAnalysis projects a rapid shift from retrofit sidecars toward facility-level 800 volt DC conversion as new capacity is built. Treat the chart as an analyst view, not an OCP schedule or requirement. The engineering pressure underneath it is broadly useful: when rack demand reaches hundreds of kilowatts and approaches the megawatt class, every conversion stage consumes capacity, generates heat, and occupies space. A few percentage points of electrical loss can represent a large continuous thermal load. The power chain therefore becomes part of compute scaling. The OCP specification responds by defining a medium-voltage-input, megawatt-scale converter boundary rather than forecasting exactly when the market will adopt it.

The chart divides projected new data-center capacity into retrofit sidecars and facility-level conversion. Its value is directional: it frames a period in which both architectures may coexist, forcing operators to support different rack generations and power boundaries. That coexistence matters. A site may not move from legacy AC distribution to an SST in one step. It may deploy rack-adjacent conversion first, add eight-hundred-volt-native loads later, and centralize conversion only where the utility interface, protection scheme, and construction program allow. In every phase, the system objective is not voltage for its own sake. It is delivering more useful compute power with controllable loss, space, risk, and serviceability.
