The OCP specification defines a medium-voltage solid state transformer as a megawatt-scale power electronic converter. It can replace the conventional fifty- or sixty-hertz transformer plus rectifier path, and when coupled with energy storage it could function as a medium-voltage UPS. The document intentionally uses a black-box product framework: suppliers retain implementation freedom while operators align on inputs, outputs, behavior, environment, communications, and compliance. Version zero point three is a community collaboration authored by Google, Microsoft, and NVIDIA. The workstream remains semi-private while the document develops toward version one point zero, but its direction aligns closely with the public OCP Power Distribution Sub-Project. That combination—early technical alignment plus an open interface—is the value of an OCP draft.

The black-box method is especially important at this maturity level. A prescriptive circuit could freeze an early implementation and make multi-vendor development harder. An interface framework instead lets suppliers innovate inside the enclosure while the community converges on measurable outcomes. Those outcomes include nominal ratings, efficiency, fault contribution, regulation, ride-through, environmental operation, communications, and compliance. Open does not mean vague. The strongest open specification is precise where interoperability depends on a value and explicit where evidence is not yet sufficient. That is why the TBD markers in version zero point three should be visible, discussed, and resolved through testing rather than hidden in procurement assumptions.
