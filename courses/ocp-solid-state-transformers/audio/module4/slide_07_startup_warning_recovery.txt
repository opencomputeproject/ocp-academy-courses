A normal startup begins with controls available in standby, verifies permissives, precharges the DC link, and then enables the main power path into S one. Shutdown reverses the energy path in a controlled sequence before returning to standby or off. A warning may leave the unit in service while temperature, communication, or another parameter is investigated. A fault moves the unit into S two and invokes the defined protective response. During an acceptable ride-through event, the source of power may shift between grid and ESS without changing the SST state. That is an important modeling point: an electrical disturbance does not automatically equal a state transition. The control narrative, alarms, telemetry, and recovery procedure should all agree.

Consider a cooling anomaly. A rising temperature may first create S three warning while the unit derates or continues under observation. If a protective threshold is crossed, it may transition to S two fault. That sequence differs from a grid sag in which the SST remains S one and storage changes its contribution. It also differs from commanded maintenance shutdown, which follows a controlled path through disabled power and discharged energy. Integrated testing should verify the state, alarm code, output behavior, communications, and local indication together. Mismatched narratives create operational error during already stressful events.
