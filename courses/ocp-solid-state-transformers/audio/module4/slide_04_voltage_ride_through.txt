The voltage ride-through model divides disturbances by per-unit input voltage. Between zero point nine and one point one per unit, the SST remains fully functional at one hundred percent output indefinitely. From above one point one to below one point two per unit, it also remains fully functional at one hundred percent output. In low-voltage range one, input current must not exceed nominal while the output remains at one hundred percent; an ESS supplies the shortfall. In low-voltage range two, the SST draws no input current and the ESS supplies the full output. When voltage and frequency return to acceptable quality, recovery is immediate and does not require a state-machine transition. Storage coordination is therefore part of the ride-through design even though storage is not necessarily inside the SST product.

Per-unit notation keeps the regions portable across the input SKUs. One point zero per unit is the nominal input voltage for the selected thirteen point eight or thirty-four point five kilovolt class. In range one, maintaining full DC output while capping AC current means the energy deficit must come from another source. In range two, zero input current prevents the SST from attempting to draw energy from a severely depressed source. The ESS carries the entire load for the required interval. Storage power rating, energy duration, state of charge, coupling point, controls, and protection must all support that promise. This is why a converter ride-through requirement becomes a system-integration requirement.
