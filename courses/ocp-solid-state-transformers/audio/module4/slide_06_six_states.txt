The SST state model uses six labels. S zero is off. Standby, labeled S b, has control power available while the main power path is not producing output. S p is precharged, meaning the DC-link energy has been established for controlled startup. S one is on and delivering normal power. S two is fault and represents a protective shutdown or inhibited condition requiring defined recovery. S three is warning: operation may continue while the system reports an abnormal condition. The distinction between warning and fault matters operationally. It prevents every anomaly from becoming an unnecessary outage while still creating a deterministic escalation path.

States should have defined entry conditions, permitted actions, telemetry, and exit conditions. Standby may allow diagnostics and communications while the power path is disabled. Precharged confirms that stored-energy nodes are ready before high-power switching begins. On is the normal conversion state. Warning indicates degraded or abnormal operation that does not yet require a protective stop. Fault should capture a reason, isolate energy as needed, and prevent an unsafe automatic restart. Off removes operating functions according to the product design. The state names are only useful when every interface reports and interprets them consistently.
