The upstream interface is typically shown with a Wye secondary. The neutral may be low-resistance grounded or solidly grounded, matching the input table. Grounding is not a labeling exercise. It establishes the return path for ground-fault current, shapes protective-device sensitivity, affects transient overvoltage, and influences insulation coordination. The product must be evaluated against the actual source transformer, neutral treatment, cable system, surge protection, and site protection study. The figure is therefore an interface reminder: the SST sees a defined three-phase, three-wire plus protective-earth input, but the grounding behavior begins upstream in the facility system.

Low-resistance grounding intentionally limits fault current while keeping it high enough for reliable detection and selective action. Solid grounding provides a low-impedance neutral reference and may produce higher fault current. The choice must align with the source transformer and protection philosophy. Because the SST input is three-wire plus protective earth, the neutral itself is not carried as a power conductor into the product. Nevertheless, the neutral treatment shapes the phase-to-ground voltages and fault energy seen at the terminals. Surge withstand and basic impulse level must be coordinated with that real system.
