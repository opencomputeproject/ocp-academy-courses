An SST is not a passive transformer. Upstream grounding shapes the fault environment it sees. Voltage ride-through requires different input-current behavior across regions while maintaining the output through coordinated ESS support. Frequency ride-through defines continuous, timed, and may-trip bands. Recovery includes the storage recharge ramp, not only the instant the grid returns. Finally, the six-state model makes startup, operation, warning, fault, and recovery observable. Keep state, energy source, and alarm severity separate when writing controls and test cases.

The core integration concept is conservation of energy under a control limit. When input voltage falls and current cannot rise enough, another source must fill the power gap if output is to remain constant. When the source returns, the stored energy must be replenished without violating another limit. The state machine describes equipment readiness and abnormal conditions, while ride-through describes electrical behavior. Those concepts interact but are not interchangeable. Keeping them separate makes requirements, control logic, and test results easier to audit.
