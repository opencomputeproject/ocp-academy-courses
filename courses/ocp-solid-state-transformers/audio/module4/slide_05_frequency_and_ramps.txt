Frequency ride-through uses a sixty-hertz-centered table. From fifty-eight point eight through sixty-one point two hertz, operation is continuous. The adjacent bands—from fifty-seven to below fifty-eight point eight, and above sixty-one point two through sixty-one point eight—require two hundred ninety-nine seconds. Outside those bands, the SST may ride through or trip. The utility load acceptance and rejection reference is approximately twenty to fifty megawatts per minute; generator and renewable entries remain TBD. After a disturbance, an ESS that discharged must recharge, and that charging power is additive site load. Coordinated controls should therefore restore energy without creating a second ramp or peak problem.

A two-hundred-ninety-nine-second band is long enough that thermal and energy limits matter, not only fast transient control. The ESS may need to support repeated or overlapping events, and the site must decide how much state-of-charge reserve to preserve. Afterward, an aggressive recharge could violate a utility ramp agreement or create a new site peak. Coordinated control can stage recharge, account for returning IT load, and preserve reserve for another disturbance. The OCP Ready ESS course expands these control classifications and the operational fact that charging is a load, even when the storage system is usually discussed as a source.
