Module four examines the SST as an active grid participant. A static rating does not explain what happens during a voltage sag, a frequency excursion, a warning, or a fault. The specification combines grounding assumptions, ride-through regions, recovery behavior, load-ramp references, storage coordination, and a six-state operating model. The objective is to predict behavior consistently—and to recognize when the ESS or another facility subsystem must carry the load.

Dynamic behavior is also where power-electronics products differ most visibly from passive transformers. A passive device responds according to its impedance and protection. An SST can make controlled decisions within physical limits. That capability must be deterministic, documented, and testable. The converter should not depend on a remote network command to perform immediate protection. At the same time, supervisory controls need enough telemetry to coordinate storage, site ramps, alarms, and recovery. We will use the specification’s regions and states to connect those fast local and slower system-control layers.
