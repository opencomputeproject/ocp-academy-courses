# Primary-source verification — 26 October 2026

This research supports an approval proposal. It does not change the current course.

- [OCP AI Computing Continuum project](https://www.opencompute.org/projects/aicc/): the live project describes open modular infrastructure across regional colocation, enterprise premises, telco points of presence and industrial sites, beyond centralized hyperscale facilities. Its scope is broader than a single edge tier. Describe developing project specifications and their published status accurately, rather than implying every proposed architecture is already a released standard.
- [AICC Academy course](https://academy.opencompute.org/learn/courses/58/ai-computing-continuum-aicc-extending-ai-beyond-the-hyperscale-data-center): the official catalog confirms four modules addressing the rationale, open infrastructure, computing across distance and ecosystem action. The introductory DC course should establish placement vocabulary and link onward. The direct web reader returns a JavaScript shell; the official search-indexed description and local editable AICC course were available.
- [IEA, Key Questions on Energy and AI, executive summary](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary): the 2026 update supports the course's 485 TWh in 2025 / 950 TWh in 2030 figures, as an estimate and projection. It also recognizes AI factories as data centers specifically designed for AI. No replacement with an older forecast is warranted.
- [OCP, Realizing the Open Data Center Ecosystem Vision](https://www.opencompute.org/news/realizing-the-ocp-open-data-center-ecosystem-vision/): the October 2026 article connects AI facilities with power, cooling, networking, telemetry and chip-to-grid coordination, and separately highlights hyperscale-adjacent inference through AICC. Roadmaps and emerging high-density designs should not become universal requirements for every AI deployment in introductory teaching.
- [NIST cloud computing program](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/nist-cloud-computing-program-nccp): distinguish service characteristics from physical facility ownership and location. The current M1S8 already explains that its four labels overlap; the AI-factory addition should preserve and extend that distinction.

Proposed framing to evaluate with Astra: operating model, scale/placement and workload purpose are separate descriptive dimensions. “AI factory” should therefore not be inserted as a mutually exclusive alternative to enterprise, colocation, hyperscale or edge.
