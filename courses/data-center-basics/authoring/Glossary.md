# Course glossary

Definitions at verified substantive use. Black space is explicitly project-specific.

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Power | The rate at which energy is transferred or used. Electrical power is commonly expressed in watts, kilowatts or megawatts. | [Source](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/measuring-electricity.php) |
| Energy | A quantity accumulated over time. Electrical energy use is commonly expressed in watt-hours or kilowatt-hours. | [Source](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/measuring-electricity.php) |
| Redundancy | Additional components or paths that can support a required function when another is unavailable. Shared failure causes still need review. | [Source](https://technav.ieee.org/area/fault-tolerance/) |
| Concurrent maintainability | The ability to remove site infrastructure components or distribution paths for planned maintenance without interrupting the supported operations. | [Source](https://ats.uptimeinstitute.com/tier-certification) |
| Fault tolerance | A system's ability to continue proper operation despite specified component failures. The design's failure scope matters. | [Source](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/fault_tolerance) |
| Colocation | A facility service in which a customer rents space and contracted supporting infrastructure for its own equipment. | [Source](https://www.cisco.com/site/us/en/learn/topics/computing/what-is-a-data-center.html) |
| Hyperscale | An infrastructure approach designed for very large, repeatable operations and expansion. It describes scale, not one ownership model. | [Source](https://docs.equinix.com/colocation/about/colo-hyperscale-xscale-data-centers/) |
| Edge data center | Computing placed close to the users, devices or activity it serves, often to reduce delay or dependence on a distant connection. | [Source](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/data-centers) |
| Latency | Delay between an event or request and the resulting processing, availability or response. Network latency is one contributor to service delay. | [Source](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/Latency) |
| Cloud computing | A model for on-demand network access to pooled configurable computing resources that can be provisioned and released with little manual effort. | [Source](https://www.nist.gov/publications/nist-definition-cloud-computing?pub_id=909616) |
| UPS | Uninterruptible power supply: a system that supports its connected load through specified supply interruptions, commonly using stored energy while another source becomes available. | [Source](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-07/best-practice-guide-data-center-design.pdf) |
| PDU | Power distribution unit: equipment that distributes electrical power to downstream loads. Facility and rack PDUs differ in scale and features. | [Source](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-07/best-practice-guide-data-center-design.pdf) |
| AC | Alternating current: electrical current whose direction changes periodically. Conventional facility supplies commonly deliver AC power. | [Source](https://www.energy.gov/articles/war-currents-ac-vs-dc-power) |
| DC | Direct current: electrical current flowing in one direction. Server electronics require DC at their operating voltages; conversion location varies by architecture. | [Source](https://www.opencompute.org/news/powering-the-next-era-of-ai-how-google-microsoft-and-nvidia-are-standardizing-and-accelerating-the-industry-transition-to-lvdc/) |
| CRAC | Computer room air conditioner: a room-cooling unit commonly using a compressor and refrigerant circuit. Confirm the actual cooling architecture rather than relying on the label. | [Source](https://www.vertiv.com/49f609/globalassets/documents/data-sheets/vertiv-healthcareglossary-ad-en-na-web_277554_2.pdf) |
| CRAH | Computer room air handler: fans move return air over a chilled-water coil supplied by the facility cooling plant. The air handler has no refrigeration compressor; the water carries heat back to the plant. | [Source](https://www.trane.com/commercial/north-america/us/en/about-us/newsroom/glossary/crah.html) |
| CDU | Coolant distribution unit: equipment that manages coolant delivery and, in many designs, separates the equipment coolant loop from the facility loop through heat exchange. | [Source](https://www.opencompute.org/projects/cooling-environments/) |
| Cold plate | A heat-transfer component attached to a hot device, with internal coolant passages that carry heat into a liquid loop. | [Source](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-07/best-practice-guide-data-center-design.pdf) |
| Containment | Physical separation of cold supply air from warm exhaust to reduce mixing and direct airflow through the equipment. | [Source](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-07/best-practice-guide-data-center-design.pdf) |
| CPU | Central processing unit: a processor that executes instructions. Its performance and power needs depend on architecture, configuration and workload. | [Source](https://www.arm.com/glossary/cpu) |
| RAM | Random access memory: working memory used by running software. Conventional server RAM is volatile and is distinct from persistent storage. | [Source](https://www.kingston.com/en/memory/kingston-glossary) |
| GPU | Graphics processing unit: a processor designed for parallel operations, used for graphics and many accelerated computing workloads. | [Source](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/products/docs/processors/what-is-a-gpu.html) |
| SSD | Solid-state drive: persistent storage using solid-state memory. Endurance, latency, capacity and power vary by product and workload. | [Source](https://www.micron.com/about/micron-glossary/nvme) |
| HDD | Hard disk drive: persistent storage using magnetic media on rotating disks, accessed by mechanical read/write heads. | [Source](https://www.macrium.com/blog/how-do-hdds-work) |
| NVMe | Non-Volatile Memory Express: specifications for host communication with non-volatile memory across transports such as PCIe and network fabrics; not a physical connector. | [Source](https://nvmexpress.org/) |
| Rack unit (U / RU) | RU, also written U, is the conventional rack height unit: 44.45 mm (1.75 in). Open Rack uses OU (OpenU): 48 mm (about 1.89 in). These are different vertical pitches, so 1 RU and 1 OU are not interchangeable. Panel heights allow clearance. Neither unit specifies rack width or depth; equipment needs compatible mounting. | [Source](https://blogs.cisco.com/datacenter/powering-ai-ready-data-centers-with-n9000-support-for-orv3) |
| Open Unit (OU) | OU, also called OpenU, is the Open Rack vertical equipment pitch: 48 mm (about 1.89 in). A conventional U or RU is 44.45 mm (1.75 in). The two pitches are not interchangeable; verify the rack and equipment mounting standard. | [Source](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/google-implementation-orv3-spec-1-pdf#page=10) |
| Virtualization | Software abstraction of physical computing resources, allowing separate execution environments to share a physical host. Capacity and isolation still require deliberate management. | [Source](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-125Ar1.pdf) |
| Virtual machine | A software-defined computing environment with virtual hardware and its own operating system and applications, supported by a hypervisor. | [Source](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-125Ar1.pdf) |
| Hypervisor | Software that presents virtualized hardware resources to virtual machines and manages their execution and separation on a physical host. | [Source](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/hypervisor) |
| Container | An application execution environment isolated through operating-system mechanisms. Containers typically share the host kernel and need resource and security controls. | [Source](https://glossary.cncf.io/container/) |
| DCIM | Data center infrastructure management: tools used to monitor, model or manage data-center resources such as assets, capacity, power and cooling; capabilities vary by implementation. | [Source](https://www.vertiv.com/49f609/globalassets/documents/data-sheets/vertiv-healthcareglossary-ad-en-na-web_277554_2.pdf) |
| BMS | Building management system: controls and monitoring for building services, commonly including mechanical and electrical systems. Its scope and integration with DCIM depend on the site. | [Source](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-07/best-practice-guide-data-center-design.pdf) |
| PUE | Power usage effectiveness: total data-center energy divided by IT equipment energy for the same period and defined boundary. It measures facility overhead, not useful computing output. | [Source](https://www.thegreengrid.org/node/372) |
| WUE | Water usage effectiveness: water use normalized by IT equipment energy, commonly liters per kilowatt-hour. State the boundary: site water and source water are different measures. | [Source](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-07/best-practice-guide-data-center-design.pdf) |
| CUE | Carbon usage effectiveness: greenhouse-gas emissions attributable to data-center energy divided by IT equipment energy, commonly kg CO₂-equivalent per kWh. State emissions factors and boundaries. | [Source](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-07/best-practice-guide-data-center-design.pdf) |
| REC | Renewable energy certificate: an instrument representing the renewable attributes of one megawatt-hour of qualifying electricity generation. It does not track physical electrons to a facility. | [Source](https://www.epa.gov/green-power-markets/renewable-energy-certificates-recs) |
| PPA | Power purchase agreement: a contract to purchase electricity under agreed terms. Physical and financial arrangements differ, and renewable-attribute ownership needs separate attention. | [Source](https://www.epa.gov/green-power-markets/physical-ppa) |
| Zombie server | In energy management, a powered-on server that performs no useful work. It differs from deliberately reserved standby capacity and needs investigation before removal. | [Source](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-07/best-practice-guide-data-center-design.pdf) |
| AI factory | Infrastructure and software organized around developing and running AI at scale; a workload and design lens that can overlap facility ownership, scale and placement categories. | [Source](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary) |
| Training | The process of adjusting a model using data. | [Source](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary) |
| Inference | Using a trained model to produce an output from an input. | [Source](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary) |
| AI Computing Continuum (AICC) | OCP project developing open, modular infrastructure approaches for AI beyond hyperscale facilities, including regional, enterprise, telco and industrial environments. | [Source](https://www.opencompute.org/projects/aicc/) |
| White space | The IT equipment area, including servers, storage and network equipment. Some local power and cooling equipment can also be in this area; a raised floor is not required. | [Source](https://www.delta-americas.com/en-US/landing/Architecture-Up-Front-Spaces) |
| Grey space | Electrical and mechanical support areas serving IT equipment, such as switchgear, UPS systems, pumps and cooling plant. Physical boundaries and included systems vary by project. | [Source](https://www.delta-americas.com/en-US/landing/Architecture-Up-Front-Spaces) |
| Black space | A project-specific area label. This course’s example uses it for the site and utility interface; other organizations use it for connectivity or broader support areas. Confirm the site definition. | [Source](https://www.delta-americas.com/en-US/landing/Architecture-Up-Front-Spaces) |
| Busbar | A conductor assembly used to distribute electrical power. In the illustrated Open Rack DC system, it carries DC from the power shelf to compatible equipment; it does not convert AC to DC. | [Source](https://www.opencompute.org/projects/rack-and-power/) |
| Rectifier | A device that converts alternating current to direct current. Modular rectifiers in a rack power shelf can provide DC to a common rack busbar. | [Source](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/ocp-open-rack-v3-power-shelf-rev-1-0-1-pdf) |
