# pronunciation: RACK -> Rack; EACK -> E ack
Recovery becomes easier to reason about when every decision names its evidence. Internal window capacity tells you what the receiver can track. E ack widths tell you what one wire snapshot reports. Received state tells you whether network repair is needed; acknowledged state governs completion of packet-delivery work.

The distance method protects against ordinary reordering with both distance and recency checks. Rack instead uses reported received state, a known bitmap horizon, and transmission timing. One early method is selected at initialization. A tail loss probe supplies feedback after inactivity, with older peers using the acknowledgement-request fallback.

Finally, identify the retry owner before drawing a recovery loop. Push R N R uses initiator retransmission, while pull R N R is redelivered locally at the target. Resync repairs a sequence hole without erasing a transaction error. These distinctions let you read a trace without calling every outstanding packet lost or every acknowledgement a successful operation.
