# pronunciation: RACK -> Rack; EACK -> E ack
A quiet tail creates a different problem: the sender may have too little feedback to make a useful loss decision. Tail Loss Probe, or T L P, sends a probe after a period with no transmitted or received packets. It tries to restart feedback, rather than declaring every quiet packet lost.

Reset to a new illustrative case. P S N four hundred is acknowledged, and four hundred one remains unacknowledged. Last activity occurred at time four microseconds. Assume a smoothed round trip of four, a probe factor of two, and a minimum probe timeout of six. The timeout is the maximum of eight and six, so eight microseconds.

At time twelve point one, inactivity exceeds eight. The sender retransmits the lowest unacknowledged packet in that window, four hundred one, as the probe. It sets both A R and E R. If both request and data windows have outstanding work, probing is considered per window. The returned feedback can then support Rack recovery when its conditions hold.

Compatibility changes the feedback expectation. A version one point zero or one point one receiver ignores E R. T L P falls back to A R-triggered acknowledgement behavior; an immediate E ack is not guaranteed. A R still respects packet acknowledgement semantics, including U L P acknowledgement for Push Data.

The larger receive window is a separate compatibility issue. With a version one point one receiver, cap the transmit window at its two hundred fifty-six positions for performance. Do not infer five hundred twelve-position peer capacity from a version one point two sender. The backup retransmission timeout remains available.
