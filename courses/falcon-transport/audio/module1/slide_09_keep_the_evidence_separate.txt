The central skill is to keep layers and evidence separate. Initiator and target describe a transaction. Transmitter and receiver describe one packet direction. A connection identifier selects connection context, a request sequence number identifies transaction order, and packet sequence numbers belong to separate request and data windows.

For a push, packet receipt precedes target upper-layer acknowledgement, which permits the returning acknowledgement and initiator completion. For a pull, the request acknowledgement is distinct from the response data coming back.

Resource independence protects forward progress. Version one point two still reserves the receive metadata needed to match a pull response, while allocating its network receive buffer on arrival. Failed admission doesn't become successful delivery.

Finally, negotiated capabilities and interoperability limits determine what a connection can use. Keep the specification revision separate from the base-header Version field. These distinctions give you a reliable starting point for the loss-recovery and multipathing modules.
