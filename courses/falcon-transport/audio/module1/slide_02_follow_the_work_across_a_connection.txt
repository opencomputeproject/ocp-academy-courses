A reliable transport is easier to understand when we separate the responsibilities it connects. Here, you'll learn to distinguish a Falcon transaction from a network packet, identify the roles at each endpoint, and explain what changes when a request pushes data or pulls data back.

We'll trace two short examples with concrete sequence numbers. Then we'll connect those examples to resource independence and compatibility between versions. The animations deliberately spread events apart so you can inspect their consequences. Their spacing is explanatory, not a measurement of network latency.

This course has three modules. Module one examines transaction architecture. Module two examines reliable delivery and loss recovery. Module three examines congestion control and multipathing. Allow about twenty minutes per module, or sixty minutes for the full course. The optional links provide further context and are outside that estimate.

Our scope is the transport protocol. Upper-layer operation mappings, application interfaces, drivers, hardware implementation, and programming details stay outside the course. You can pause narration, inspect a completed diagram, and replay a sequence whenever a distinction needs another look.
