By the end of this module, you will be able to separate a packet's round trip from time spent at the receiver, explain the different jobs of the fabric and Nick congestion windows, and follow how an acknowledgment reopens capacity on a particular path.

We begin with the measurement contract between Falcon's datapath and its Rate Update Engine. We then connect Swift's control decisions and congestion signaling to the information actually returned by the network. Finally, we follow a four-path scheduling example and distinguish concurrent multipathing from a protective change of route.

Module one established Falcon's transactions and packet delivery. Module two explained selective repair and loss recovery. Here we use that context without repeating every packet format. This is a protocol course, not a tuning exercise or an implementation lab. The numerical examples help you reason about behavior; they are not recommended deployment settings. Allow time to pause at the resulting diagrams and check the arithmetic yourself.
