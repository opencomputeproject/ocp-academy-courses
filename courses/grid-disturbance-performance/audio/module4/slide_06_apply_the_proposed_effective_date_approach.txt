# pronunciation: OCP -> O C P; POI -> point of interconnection; PFAPR -> post-fault active power recovery; p.u. -> per unit; PSU -> P S U; UPS -> U P S; VFD -> V F D; OEM -> O E M; SVC -> S V C; E-STATCOM -> E statcom.

The O C P implementation proposal reflects the fact that some facilities may need more than a straightforward firmware update. It begins with an effective date for the disturbance-performance requirement. The document's publication date should not be silently treated as a universal utility enforcement date.

Facilities already operating, or holding executed interconnection agreements at the effective date, would not be required to comply for their lifetime under the proposal. That is the existing-capacity category shown first in the animation.

Growth above the volume already covered by an executed agreement would be subject to the requirements, but only for that incremental volume. Expanding a site would not automatically make its previously grandfathered capacity subject to retroactive application.

The proposal also supports best-effort voluntary improvement for grandfathered facilities, with utility engagement and possible incentives. Facilities entering load study and contracting after the effective date would be expected to comply when they begin operating.

The timeline uses hypothetical capacity blocks to make those distinctions visible. It does not assign a universal date or claim that a utility has adopted this approach. For a real project, identify the status and volume of the executed agreement, distinguish later incremental growth, and establish the applicable treatment with the utility.

This is why project history belongs beside technical capability in an implementation discussion. The same equipment challenge can sit within different proposed obligations depending on when and how the capacity was agreed.
