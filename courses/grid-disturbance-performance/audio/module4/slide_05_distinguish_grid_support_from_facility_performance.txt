# pronunciation: OCP -> O C P; POI -> point of interconnection; PFAPR -> post-fault active power recovery; p.u. -> per unit; PSU -> P S U; UPS -> U P S; VFD -> V F D; OEM -> O E M; SVC -> S V C; E-STATCOM -> E statcom.

The utility can also influence the disturbance that a facility experiences. The specification discusses dynamic reactive-power support in areas with concentrated data-center load. Examples include static VAR compensators, or S V Cs, and E statcoms.

These devices can support voltage during and after a fault. In the source's discussion, that support may reduce the depth of the voltage depression at the point of interconnection and help voltage recover. The facility may therefore see a less severe disturbance than it would without that support.

Keep the causal distinction clear. Improving the voltage presented to a facility is different from changing the facility's own ride-through and recovery capability. The document explicitly says this mitigation category does not itself make the data center perform according to the stated criteria.

A useful review separates the two pieces of evidence. One concerns the network and the voltage conditions it produces. The other concerns the connected equipment and its response to those conditions. They interact, but one cannot simply stand in for the other.

The diagram represents that relationship conceptually. It is not a sizing calculation for a compensator or a claim about a particular installation. The teaching point is the boundary: grid support can reduce event severity, while facility performance still needs its own assessment against the agreed interface requirements.
