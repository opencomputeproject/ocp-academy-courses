# pronunciation: OCP -> O C P; POI -> point of interconnection; PFAPR -> post-fault active power recovery; p.u. -> per unit; PSU -> P S U; UPS -> U P S; VFD -> V F D; OEM -> O E M; SVC -> S V C; E-STATCOM -> E statcom.

One candidate mitigation is a firmware change to the rectifier or power supply unit, often abbreviated P S U, serving the computing equipment. The specification discusses using stored energy, typically associated with capacitors, to support the computational load through the required disturbance behavior.

Firmware can change how equipment uses its capabilities. It cannot be assumed to create unlimited energy storage or remove the hardware constraints of the design. Whether a useful change is possible is a question for the original equipment manufacturer, or O E M, and the specific model.

A fleet may contain several models selected for different computing systems or supply arrangements. A result demonstrated on one model therefore does not establish that every unit in the fleet can receive the same update or achieve the same performance.

The source explicitly avoids a universal feasibility or rollout timeline. A practical discussion needs the equipment inventory, the manufacturer's assessment, and evidence of the behavior that the proposed change can support. Those are project-review inputs, not a new certification procedure invented by this course.

The implication is to investigate firmware as a candidate rather than assume it is the answer. Connect the change to the required voltage band, during-fault behavior, and recovery target. Then establish what has been demonstrated on the equipment that will actually be deployed.
