# pronunciation: OCP -> O C P; POI -> point of interconnection; PFAPR -> post-fault active power recovery; p.u. -> per unit; PSU -> P S U; UPS -> U P S; VFD -> V F D; OEM -> O E M; SVC -> S V C; E-STATCOM -> E statcom.

Consider a fictional facility with eighty megawatts of pre-fault computational load. The example voltage falls to zero point seven zero per unit for zero point four zero seconds, then returns to one per unit.

That voltage belongs to the half-second ride-through band. The event lasts less than half a second, so the example remains within the stated minimum. During the sag, continued grid active-power consumption is required, and proportional reduction is permitted.

For illustration, the computational draw reduces to seventy percent of eighty megawatts, which is fifty-six megawatts. That is one allowed proportional response, not a command that every compliant facility must follow. A design may maintain more power if its equipment supports that behavior.

When voltage returns to the normal band, the recovery clock starts. Ninety percent of the pre-fault eighty megawatts is seventy-two megawatts. The illustrated power trace reaches that target within two seconds of voltage restoration.

Notice what the animation does not count. It does not start the recovery clock at the beginning of the sag. It does not calculate ninety percent of fifty-six megawatts. It does not add cooling load to the computational baseline. And its smooth recovery line is an example, not a specified ramp-rate limit.

Use the same sequence with project data: qualify the event, identify the computational baseline, locate normal-voltage return, and compare measured recovery with the correct target and deadline.
