Cooling equipment can respond on a different timescale from computational power supplies. The specification describes many cooling loads as using variable-frequency drives, or V F Ds. Their ride-through characteristics and restart sequences can differ substantially from the rectifiers serving computing.

After a trip, some cooling equipment may take minutes to restore operation. A firmware change alone may not overcome the hardware and physical constraints. Equipment diversity across a facility fleet adds another implementation challenge: there may be many manufacturers, models, and configurations to evaluate.

The source explains why this does not automatically mean computing must stop during every brief cooling interruption. Facilities can have thermal inertia and be designed to sustain computational operation through short disturbances while cooling restarts. That rationale supports treating the computational-load commitment separately.

It is not a universal guarantee of thermal autonomy. The document supplies no single duration that applies to every site, workload, cooling architecture, or starting condition. Avoid turning a qualitative resilience explanation into an invented number of safe minutes.

For the recovery assessment in this course, keep the computational baseline and its two-second target distinct from cooling restoration. For the facility review, still establish the cooling response and the conditions under which computing remains supported. Separating the commitments helps the engineering discussion; it does not remove the need to understand the whole facility.
