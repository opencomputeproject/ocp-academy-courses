# pronunciation: OCP -> O C P; POI -> point of interconnection; PFAPR -> post-fault active power recovery; p.u. -> per unit; PSU -> P S U; UPS -> U P S; VFD -> V F D; OEM -> O E M; SVC -> S V C; E-STATCOM -> E statcom.

A clean classroom trace hides several choices that matter in a real project. One is the voltage measurement convention. The specification allows line-to-neutral or line-to-line voltage at the point of interconnection. Depending on the fault, those measurements can fall into different bands.

The document does not impose one universal selection algorithm for every facility. It places detailed implementation choices within the utility interconnection and project protection-coordination work. Before classifying an event, establish which measured quantity the agreed scheme uses.

Reclosing introduces another complication. A utility may open and reclose a circuit after a fault. Rapid operations over a short interval can be interpreted by data-center protection as a deeper or longer disturbance. A response that looks surprising from a simplified utility trace may reflect that equipment interpretation.

The source calls for remaining connected through qualifying faults without disconnecting merely because a running count has been reached. It also identifies counter-based schemes as an area for future work. Those statements do not provide a universal numerical count or time window that we can add to this course.

The practical task is coordination: align the measurement convention, protection settings, reclosing sequence, and expected equipment behavior. Record what is assumed and what has been demonstrated. A voltage envelope is a necessary part of the discussion, but it is not a complete model of every protection system.
