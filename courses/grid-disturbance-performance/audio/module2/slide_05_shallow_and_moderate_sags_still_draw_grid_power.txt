For a sag from zero point eight zero up to, but not including, zero point nine zero per unit, the minimum ride-through time is two seconds. The computational load continues consuming active power from the grid, but it may reduce that power in proportion to voltage.

The next band begins at zero point five zero and ends just below zero point eight zero per unit. Its minimum time is half a second. The same power rule applies: continue consuming active power, with proportional reduction permitted.

These are permissions within a requirement. Proportional reduction is allowed; the text does not require the facility to reduce its power. Nor does this permission authorize zero grid power throughout either band. The any-value permission belongs to the deeper voltage bands we'll examine next.

The endpoints are useful checks. Exactly zero point eight zero belongs to the two-second band. Exactly zero point five zero belongs to the half-second band that still requires grid active-power consumption. Rounding a measured value before classification could obscure that distinction.

For qualifying events in both bands, the recovery clock starts when point-of-interconnection voltage returns to zero point nine zero through one point one zero per unit. The target is ninety percent of pre-fault computational load within two seconds.
