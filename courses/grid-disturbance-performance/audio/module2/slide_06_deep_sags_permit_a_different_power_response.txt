# pronunciation: OCP -> O C P; POI -> point of interconnection; PFAPR -> post-fault active power recovery; p.u. -> per unit; PSU -> P S U; UPS -> U P S; VFD -> V F D; OEM -> O E M; SVC -> S V C; E-STATCOM -> E statcom.

Below zero point five zero per unit, the during-fault power permission changes. From zero point three five up to, but not including, zero point five zero, the minimum ride-through time is a quarter of a second. Below zero point three five, it is fifteen hundredths of a second.

In both of these bands, power may reduce to any value. The explanatory discussion explicitly includes zero megawatts. That is a significant distinction from the shallower bands, which require continued active-power consumption from the grid.

Zero grid draw during the deep portion of a fault is not the same claim as shutting down the computing workload. The facility's internal energy and power-conversion arrangements determine how computing is supported. The interface requirement describes what the grid sees and the recovery commitment that follows a qualifying event.

That recovery commitment still matters. An allowed temporary reduction does not become permission for an indefinite loss of demand after a qualifying fault clears. The load must satisfy the stated ninety-percent, two-second recovery target once voltage returns to the normal band.

Use the exact inequalities again. Exactly zero point three five belongs to the quarter-second band. Exactly zero point five zero does not belong to either deep-sag band. This is why a readable table and correctly labeled examples are more useful than a vague statement that low voltage allows power to disappear.
