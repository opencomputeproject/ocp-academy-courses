# pronunciation: OCP -> O C P; POI -> point of interconnection; PFAPR -> post-fault active power recovery; p.u. -> per unit; PSU -> P S U; UPS -> U P S; VFD -> V F D; OEM -> O E M; SVC -> S V C; E-STATCOM -> E statcom.

A data center's response to a fault can become part of the disturbance experienced by the wider grid. The immediate concern inside a facility is usually continuity of computing. The utility also needs to understand what happens to the demand it was serving.

Imagine several large loads drawing power before a fault. Generation and demand are in balance. A voltage disturbance occurs, and multiple facilities sharply reduce their grid draw at about the same time. Computing might remain available on stored energy or backup, but the grid has lost a substantial block of demand.

Generation does not necessarily change by the same amount at the same instant. That mismatch can cause frequency and voltage excursions and create further reliability concerns. The O C P specification cites a North American event involving fifteen hundred megawatts of simultaneous load loss as part of its motivation.

The animation is a qualitative sequence, not a prediction of a particular grid. It shows why the timing of many responses matters. We aren't assigning a frequency trajectory or assuming that every data center behaves identically.

The practical implication is that computing availability alone doesn't describe the facility's grid impact. A useful interface commitment must also address the power drawn during a disturbance and the way that demand returns afterward.
