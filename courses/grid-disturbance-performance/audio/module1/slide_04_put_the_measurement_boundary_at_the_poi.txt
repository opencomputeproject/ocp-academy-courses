# pronunciation: OCP -> O C P; POI -> point of interconnection; PFAPR -> post-fault active power recovery; p.u. -> per unit; PSU -> P S U; UPS -> U P S; VFD -> V F D; OEM -> O E M; SVC -> S V C; E-STATCOM -> E statcom.

Every performance statement needs a clear measurement boundary. This specification uses the point of interconnection, often abbreviated P O I, for the voltage that classifies the disturbance. Think of it as the agreed electrical interface between the facility and the utility system.

The connection may be at medium voltage or high voltage. The document deliberately takes a connection-voltage-agnostic approach. That lets the same normalized voltage bands describe different nominal voltage levels without prescribing one facility architecture.

Keep two questions separate. Where is the voltage measured? At the point of interconnection. Which load component carries the stated performance commitment? The computational load. A measurement boundary at the utility interface does not automatically expand that commitment to every motor, pump, fan, or auxiliary device within the site.

The specification allows a line-to-neutral or line-to-line voltage measurement, with implementation details addressed through the project's interconnection and protection coordination. We'll return to that choice later because different fault types can produce different measured results.

For now, use the boundary diagram to organize a review. Start with the utility connection, follow the facility power path, and identify the computational load whose response is being described. Don't substitute a convenient internal measurement point without checking whether it represents the agreed interface condition.
