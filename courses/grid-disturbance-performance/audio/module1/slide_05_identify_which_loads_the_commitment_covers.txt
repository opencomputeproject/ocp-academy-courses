The computational load is the center of the stated low-voltage ride-through, high-voltage ride-through, and recovery commitment. The specification says this component is typically about seventy to ninety percent of total data center load. That is contextual guidance, not a fixed allocation that every facility must reproduce.

Cooling equipment is discussed separately because it can behave very differently during and after a fault. Many cooling loads connect through variable-frequency drives. Their electrical limits and restart behavior differ from the rectifiers and power supplies serving computing. A cooling restart can take much longer than the recovery interval specified for computational demand.

Auxiliary loads form another category. The document describes them as typically less than one percent for large-scale data centers and makes no specific performance commitment for that subset.

These distinctions matter whenever someone presents a percentage or a power trace. A statement about ninety percent recovery needs a defined baseline. Ninety percent of pre-fault computational load is not automatically ninety percent of the site's total pre-fault meter reading.

In a project review, label each quantity before calculating. Identify the computational component, explain how cooling is treated, and keep auxiliary-load assumptions visible. That simple discipline prevents a precise-looking calculation from answering the wrong question.
