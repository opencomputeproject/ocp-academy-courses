Voltage ride-through describes behavior during a disturbance and the ability to remain connected through the required event. Low-voltage ride-through concerns voltage sags. High-voltage ride-through concerns swells. The specification ties the required behavior to both the voltage magnitude and the time spent in the applicable condition.

Post-fault active power recovery describes what happens next. Once voltage returns to the stated normal range, the computational load must return to the specified fraction of its pre-fault active-power demand within the allowed time, for the qualifying low-voltage events listed in the table.

The distinction is important because remaining connected does not always mean drawing constant power. Some voltage bands require constant power. Other bands permit power to reduce in proportion to voltage. For deeper sags, power may reduce to any value, including zero, while retaining the applicable recovery commitment.

Nor does permission to reduce grid draw mean the computing workload must stop. Internal stored energy and equipment behavior are separate parts of the facility design. The interface table describes the grid-facing performance that the design must support.

Read the timeline in order: the disturbance begins, the applicable ride-through interval is evaluated, voltage returns to the normal band, and recovery is assessed. Treating all four moments as one event makes it easy to start the recovery clock too early or apply the wrong power requirement.
