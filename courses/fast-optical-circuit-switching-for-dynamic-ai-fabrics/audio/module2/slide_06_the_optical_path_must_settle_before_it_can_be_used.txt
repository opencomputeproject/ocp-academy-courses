The switching stage changes the optical state and lets the path settle. Depending on the mechanism, that can involve mechanical motion, a temperature change, an electrical field, or a change in gain. A command being accepted is not necessarily the same event as the optical path reaching usable conditions.

For measurement, state both boundaries. Did the clock start at command receipt, at an electrical drive signal, or at the optical transition? Did it stop at the first detectable light, at a specified power level, or after the signal met a usable stability criterion? Numbers taken across different boundaries should not be placed in a comparison as though they were identical.

Even a settled path is not yet an end-to-end link. The new source can arrive with a different phase and amplitude, and the receiver may need to adapt. The switch's completion signal therefore needs to be interpreted as one condition in a larger readiness process.

Keep physical switching performance visible in the budget, but do not let it stand in for the whole budget. The next dependency is what the receiver can reliably recover from the newly established path.
