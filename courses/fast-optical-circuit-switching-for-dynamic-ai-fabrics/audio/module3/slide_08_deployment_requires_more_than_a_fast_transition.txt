A deployment decision combines several constraints. Radix means the number of switch ports. More ports can support different topology choices, but scaling a mechanism can change control complexity, optical paths, and manufacturing requirements. A small device demonstration does not establish a large switch with the same performance.

Insertion loss describes the optical power lost through a component or path. The receiving endpoint needs adequate signal quality after the complete path. A connection that switches quickly but cannot meet the optical budget is not useful for that design. The paper flags loss and port count as essential while leaving their detailed trade-off analysis outside its scope.

Endurance connects the physical mechanism to its duty cycle. Ask whether the relevant degradation depends on operating hours, switching events, temperature, optical exposure, or a combination. Then compare the evidence with the expected workload and maintenance model.

Integration adds another set of requirements. The controller must command and observe the device. Receivers must recover quickly enough. Endpoints must handle changing connectivity, and operators need telemetry and failure behavior that can be understood at scale.

Use an evidence matrix rather than a single speed ranking. State the required port count, optical conditions, switching pattern, readiness time, and reliability behavior. Then identify what is demonstrated, what is assumed, and what remains to be tested. This keeps an interesting mechanism aligned with a concrete system need.
