# pronunciation: OCP -> O C P
Turn the course into an evaluation you could actually run. Start with a workload and its communication window. Describe who communicates, when the pattern changes, how predictable it is, and what happens if a new path is late. That establishes a requirement before choosing a switching technology.

Next, define the complete timing measurement. State the request event, the usable-traffic event, and how scheduling, control, physical switching, receiver recovery, synchronization, and endpoint readiness are accounted for. Include the work performed in advance, because its validity and cost still matter.

Then compare useful outcomes. Measure delivered traffic, queueing, and the workload result against a fair baseline. For a training study, that might include iteration time or productive training throughput. For a recovery study, it might include the disruption and resources restored. Do not assume that one metric proves every kind of benefit.

Finally, exercise the conditions that make the claim useful: representative scale, different paths, timing variation, unsuccessful setup, failures, and sustained operation. State the optical, energy, and operational assumptions. The result should make clear what has been demonstrated and what remains conditional.

The Optical Circuit Switching subproject provides a community setting for open interfaces, integration, telemetry, interoperability, and demonstrations. The paper identifies an opportunity for technology-neutral system measurement. This evaluation sequence is a teaching framework derived from those concerns, not an existing O C P certification or approved benchmark. Use it to frame a contribution with a precise requirement, reproducible evidence, and an explicit boundary.
