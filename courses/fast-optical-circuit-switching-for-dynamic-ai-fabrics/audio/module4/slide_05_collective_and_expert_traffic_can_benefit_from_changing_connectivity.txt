# pronunciation: PCCL -> P C C L
A communication pattern describes which endpoints need to exchange data during a phase of the workload. If the useful pairs change between phases, a fixed topology may offer capacity in places that are not the current bottleneck. Reconfigurable connectivity creates an opportunity to respond.

Collective communication coordinates a group of accelerators. Different phases can use different patterns of exchange. Collective-aware optical designs explore whether prepared direct paths can reduce contention or improve the use of bandwidth. The photonic collective communication research called P C C L is one example of this approach.

Now change the communication phase in the illustrative network. Different pairs become active, so the useful connectivity changes too. A benefit requires the new paths to be ready before their traffic needs them. The schedule, receiver recovery, and endpoint release must therefore cooperate with the workload rather than operate as unrelated components.

Mixture-of-Experts workloads introduce another pattern: tokens move to selected experts, creating uneven all-to-all communication. MixNet explores a reconfigurable optical domain alongside electrical interconnects. Its published implementation uses millisecond-scale switching and hides much of that delay during computation. It is not evidence that every expert workload needs microsecond switching.

The two examples share an evaluation question: does adapting connectivity reduce the workload's actual communication bottleneck? Answer it with representative phases, complete timing, and useful training results. The animation is conceptual.
