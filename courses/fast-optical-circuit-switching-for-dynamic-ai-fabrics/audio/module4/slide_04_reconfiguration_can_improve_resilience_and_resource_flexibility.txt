# pronunciation: TPU -> T P U
Reconfiguration can improve service even when it is not happening at microsecond timescales. Production experience with optically reconfigurable accelerator systems demonstrates value in arranging topology, working around faults, and making resources available to jobs.

The T P U version four resiliency work describes software-defined control of an inter-chip fabric using optical circuit switches. Its recovery system detects failures and changes connectivity to route around machine, chip, or link problems. Mission Apollo provides broader evidence of optical circuit switching deployed at data center scale.

These are useful production examples, but the outcome needs to be named correctly. Improved availability means the system can keep more useful resources accessible despite faults. Resource flexibility means connectivity can support different allocations or topologies. Neither statement by itself proves that a running communication phase benefits from sub-millisecond switching.

This distinction protects the evaluation from mixing unlike claims. A recovery operation may tolerate a different delay from a topology change between two short communication phases. The same physical technology can contribute to both ideas only if the timing and supporting system meet each requirement.

When reading a production result, ask what was deployed, what event triggered the reconfiguration, and what service outcome was measured. Keep the demonstrated operational benefit, while resisting the temptation to assign it to a faster switching tier that the evidence did not test.
