# pronunciation: MFU -> M F U
Model FLOPs utilization, or M F U, relates useful model computation during training to the cluster's theoretical peak computing rate. It can help frame the value of reducing overhead, but it must be interpreted carefully.

An illustrative M F U of forty percent means that the counted productive model computation is running at about forty percent of the theoretical peak rate. It does not mean that the remaining sixty percent consists of completely idle accelerators. The remainder can reflect memory access, communication, synchronization, imbalance, scheduling, and other software or hardware limits.

Fast optical reconfiguration can only improve the portion that its complete system design actually affects. If a workload is dominated by another limitation, changing connectivity may have little effect on training throughput. Even when communication is important, a new topology must be usable at the correct time and must improve the relevant exchanges.

This is why an infrastructure value calculation needs workload evidence. Do not multiply the entire utilization gap by equipment cost and label the result recoverable networking savings. Separate the observed bottleneck, the intervention, and the measured change in useful work.

A fair study keeps the workload and comparison conditions explicit. Record training throughput or another meaningful outcome alongside the communication behavior and resource cost. Availability improvements and faster training are both valuable, but they are different results and should not be merged into one unsupported utilization claim.
