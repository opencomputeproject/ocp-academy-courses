A cold plate collects heat close to its source. A component transfers heat through its mounting interface into the plate. Coolant moving through the plate's internal passages receives that heat and leaves warmer in the single-phase example we're following.

The supply path delivers the required coolant conditions. A manifold distributes flow among the connected equipment. Hoses or piping carry the fluid, and specified connectors provide the interfaces between assemblies. The return path carries the warmed coolant toward the equipment that removes its heat.

The manifold distributes fluid; it doesn't perform the complete system's heat-rejection job. Likewise, a cold plate's performance doesn't establish that every branch receives the necessary flow. The actual installation must support the operating requirements of its connected loads.

Follow the supply and return paths in the animation, then follow the heat beyond them. This example keeps the working fluid liquid while its temperature changes. Some cold-plate systems instead use boiling and condensation. That is a different operating arrangement requiring its own fluid, controls and qualification. Both remain cold-plate architectures because of where and how they acquire the component heat.
