# pronunciation: PG25 -> P G twenty-five
The fluid is part of the cooling system's performance and reliability. Start with the actual loop requirements rather than choosing a familiar product name.

Water-glycol mixtures are water-based coolants. They aren't a completely separate category from water-based fluids. Adding glycol can provide freeze protection, while also changing heat-transfer and flow properties. The chosen formulation must balance those properties with the intended operating conditions.

P G twenty-five identifies a specified propylene-glycol mixture used in relevant cooling applications. That name doesn't mean the fluid is maintenance free or automatically protected from every biological problem. The qualified formulation, additives, cleanliness and ongoing management still matter.

Compatibility extends to every wetted material: metals, hoses, seals and other surfaces in contact with the coolant. Suppliers should provide the evidence needed for the actual assembled system. A fluid that meets a delivery specification still needs appropriate handling and operating stewardship.

Some two-phase cold-plate systems use dielectric working fluids selected for boiling and condensation. Those fluids aren't interchangeable with every immersion fluid or water-based coolant. Use the linked fluids courses when you need the selection, qualification and maintenance details. For this introduction, remember that fluid choice follows the system requirements.
