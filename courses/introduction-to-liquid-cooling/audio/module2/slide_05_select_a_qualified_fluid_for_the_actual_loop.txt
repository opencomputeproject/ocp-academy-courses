# pronunciation: DI -> D I; EG25 -> E G twenty-five; PG25 -> P G twenty-five; PG55 -> P G fifty-five
The fluid is part of the cooling system's performance and reliability. Start with the actual loop requirements rather than choosing a familiar product name.

You'll hear four common labels for water-based coolants. D I means deionized water: water treated to remove dissolved ions. Purity alone doesn't make it a finished, maintenance-free coolant.

E G twenty-five identifies an ethylene-glycol and water formulation with a nominal twenty-five percent glycol concentration. P G twenty-five identifies propylene glycol at a nominal twenty-five percent. P G fifty-five identifies propylene glycol at a nominal fifty-five percent. Confirm the product's concentration basis and complete formulation.

Water-glycol mixtures are still water-based. Higher glycol content can improve freeze protection while changing viscosity and heat-transfer performance. The percentage alone doesn't define additives, handling requirements or compatibility. These fluids aren't interchangeable or maintenance free.

Compatibility extends to every wetted material: metals, hoses, seals and other surfaces in contact with the coolant. Suppliers should provide evidence for the actual assembled system. The qualified fluid still needs appropriate handling and ongoing management.

Some two-phase cold-plate systems use dielectric working fluids selected for boiling and condensation. Those fluids aren't interchangeable with every immersion fluid or water-based coolant. Use the linked fluids courses when you need the selection, qualification and maintenance details. For this introduction, remember that fluid choice follows the system requirements.
