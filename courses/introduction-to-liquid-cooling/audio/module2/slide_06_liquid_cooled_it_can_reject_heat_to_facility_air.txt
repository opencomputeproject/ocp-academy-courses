# pronunciation: CDU -> C D U; IT -> I T; OCP -> O C P
Liquid-cooled I T doesn't always require a direct connection to a facility water loop. A liquid-to-air system can receive heat from a local coolant circuit and transfer it into the data center's air system.

O C P Project Brazos illustrates this retrofit approach. The I T equipment transfers heat into its coolant. A liquid-to-air heat exchanger receives the warmed liquid, and an air stream carries the heat away from that exchanger. The local liquid loop circulates back toward the I T equipment.

The building's existing air-handling and heat-removal systems still receive the load. Brazos changes the path between the equipment and facility; it doesn't remove the facility's cooling responsibility. The room needs enough capacity and suitable air conditions for the proposed installation.

This creates a useful option for an air-cooled site, subject to the actual power, airflow, temperature and space requirements. It isn't evidence that any available rack position can support any liquid-cooled load.

Compare the two paths by asking what receives the heat at the boundary. A liquid-to-liquid C D U transfers it into a facility liquid circuit. A liquid-to-air sidecar transfers it into air. The dedicated Brazos course covers its implementation and performance conditions.
