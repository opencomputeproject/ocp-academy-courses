# pronunciation: CDU -> C D U; TCS -> T C S; FWS -> F W S; OCP -> O C P
A coolant distribution unit, or C D U, helps connect two cooling responsibilities. In a common liquid-to-liquid arrangement, one side serves the technology cooling system, or T C S. The other connects to the facility water system, or F W S. Heat transfers across an exchanger while the fluids remain separate.

This separation lets each side have its own fluid requirements and operating conditions. Pumps, sensors and controls support the selected design. The C D U must deliver the required technology-side conditions while transferring heat to a facility side that can accept it.

Don't assume that any C D U can serve any installation. Available temperatures, flow, pressure, hydraulic resistance and fault behavior all affect the complete system. The interface requirements need to be agreed between the equipment and facility teams.

O C P Project Deschutes is an example of a facility-water C D U contribution. Its dedicated Academy course examines the design in much greater detail. Here, the important idea is the boundary: the technology loop and facility water loop exchange heat without becoming one fluid circuit. That boundary helps identify the requirements and the owner on each side. Next, we'll contrast it with a path that transfers heat into facility air.
