Check the site before ordering equipment. Start with the complete heat path and available electrical capacity. The proposed load must be supported under the relevant operating and environmental conditions, rather than only under a convenient nominal condition.

Next, examine temperatures, routing, distribution and space. Coolant lines need appropriate access and protection. Equipment needs a suitable supporting structure and room for service. Containment and leak-response arrangements must fit the actual installation. A rack position doesn't establish all of those requirements.

Apply these questions consistently across architectures. Facility-water integration needs a supported liquid interface. A Brazos-type liquid-to-air retrofit still needs enough facility air-handling and heat-removal capacity. An immersion installation adds tank weight, equipment-handling and bath-service considerations.

Consider the installation during normal operation and during planned service or a fault. The design and operating team should identify the relevant conditions and the evidence needed to support them.

You don't need to resolve every engineering question personally. Make the interfaces visible and assign the questions to the people responsible. That lets procurement proceed from a supported site requirement, rather than assuming that purchasing compatible equipment automatically makes the site ready.
