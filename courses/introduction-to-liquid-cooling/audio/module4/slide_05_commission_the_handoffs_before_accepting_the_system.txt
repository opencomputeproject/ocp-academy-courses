Commissioning demonstrates that the installed system and its handoffs work together. Component qualification is important evidence, but it doesn't establish the performance of the complete site installation.

Begin with the agreed installation checks. These confirm that the configuration, connections, access and protective arrangements match the approved design. Cleaning, flushing, filling and sampling then follow the procedures appropriate to the selected equipment and fluid. Record the baseline that future observations will be compared against.

Load testing examines whether the complete heat path delivers the required conditions. Fault-response testing examines the relevant protective and recovery behavior. The exact tests and sequence come from the design and acceptance requirements, rather than from a universal introductory checklist.

The animation shows evidence passing from one responsibility to the next. Acceptance depends on those handoffs being complete, including the operating documentation, training and ownership needed after the installation team leaves.

Ask what has actually been demonstrated, under which conditions, and who accepted the result. A successful factory test or an idle system running without alarms doesn't answer every installation question. Commissioning connects the product evidence to the operating system the site intends to rely on.
