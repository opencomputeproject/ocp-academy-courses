Build the business case across the installation's lifecycle. Capital work includes more than the cooling device. Consider facility changes, integration, commissioning, access and service arrangements. Operating cost includes energy, maintenance, fluid stewardship, supply continuity and the consequences of downtime.

The benefit may include enabling equipment that couldn't otherwise be supported at the site. It may also involve improved utilization or reduced cooling overhead. State which benefit the proposal is intended to deliver and how it will be measured.

Keep energy, water and carbon claims separate. They depend on different boundaries and conditions. A change in a facility ratio doesn't automatically establish a reduction in total emissions or water use. Compare a defined service and operating period against a suitable baseline.

Heat reuse needs a useful demand, suitable temperatures, an interface and a viable arrangement between the parties involved. Fluid end-of-life planning likewise needs a real service pathway. Recovery, treatment or disposal must fit the actual fluid and applicable installation requirements.

A credible case makes those assumptions visible. It doesn't promise a universal payback or sustainability result. It explains what will create value here, which conditions support that result, and how the organization will verify it over time.
