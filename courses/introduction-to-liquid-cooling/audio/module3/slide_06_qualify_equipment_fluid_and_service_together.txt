Equipment, fluid and service need qualification together. An air-cooled server doesn't become immersion-ready simply because it fits inside a tank. Its materials and construction must be suitable for the intended liquid environment and operating conditions.

Consider boards, cables, connectors, seals and labels, along with other parts exposed to the fluid. A material can change over time through contact with a fluid. Contamination can also affect the bath or the equipment. Compatibility evidence should address the actual combination rather than a generic statement about a fluid family.

Service introduces another set of interactions. People may need to lift equipment, allow it to drain, move it through a controlled handling area, clean selected surfaces and return it to operation. Each step needs an approved method for the installation. Tools, lifting arrangements, protective measures and training belong in that operating plan.

The purpose isn't to memorize a universal maintenance sequence. Different systems require different procedures. The practical question is whether the selected equipment, fluid and service process have been evaluated together, and whether the people responsible can carry out that process consistently. That evidence supports both initial adoption and the installation's continuing reliability.
