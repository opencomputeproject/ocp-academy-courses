An immersion tank is part of a larger heat-removal system. In single-phase operation, heat leaves through the selected circulation and exchange path. In two-phase operation, the condenser transfers heat to another cooling path. Either way, the energy still needs a final destination.

The supporting installation may include pumps, liquid distribution, heat exchangers and outdoor heat rejection. Its requirements depend on the selected design and site. Removing server fan duty doesn't eliminate every moving part or every facility cooling responsibility.

Physical integration also matters. Consider the filled weight, supporting floor, service access and the space needed to handle equipment. A tank's footprint alone may not represent the full area required for operation and maintenance. Routing and connection access affect how people work around the installation.

Ask the same heat-path questions you would ask of a cold-plate system. What receives the heat, under which conditions, and with what available capacity? Then add the requirements specific to the tank and its service process. A useful immersion review includes the equipment, the bath and the supporting facility. Evaluating only the cooling action inside the tank leaves important parts of the system unanswered.
