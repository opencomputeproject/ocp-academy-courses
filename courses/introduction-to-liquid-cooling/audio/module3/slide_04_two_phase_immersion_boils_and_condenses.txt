Two-phase immersion uses a different heat-transfer process. The selected working fluid boils at heated surfaces under the system's operating conditions. Vapor moves toward a condenser, releases heat there and returns to liquid. That liquid returns to the bath, continuing the cycle.

The energy carried through boiling and condensation is called latent heat. The fluid, condenser, containment and controls must support the intended cycle. A vapor path needs to be managed as part of the cooling system; it isn't simply an open invitation for fluid to escape.

The animation separates the movement of liquid and vapor. It also shows the condenser as a boundary where another cooling path receives the heat. The facility remains responsible for that downstream heat path.

Compare this with two-phase direct liquid cooling. In that case, phase change occurs within a cold-plate arrangement serving components through contained fluid connections. In two-phase immersion, the equipment operates in the bath arrangement. Both use latent heat, but that shared principle doesn't make their equipment, fluids or operating procedures interchangeable. Identify both the physical architecture and the phase behavior before comparing the details of a proposed installation.
