Imagine an immersion pilot proposal with an attractive efficiency claim. It identifies a tank and a fluid, but it doesn't document equipment coverage, a lifting process or the facility's heat-rejection capacity. Is the pilot ready?

The claimed efficiency doesn't answer those missing questions. Begin by identifying the actual equipment and fluid combination and requesting its compatibility evidence. Confirm the written warranty terms and responsibilities. Then review how people will install, handle, service and return the equipment to operation.

Follow the heat beyond the tank. The condenser or exchanger must connect to a supported facility path under the conditions expected during the pilot. Define what successful operation will look like and which evidence will demonstrate it.

A supported trial has clear acceptance criteria, monitoring, owners and a response to faults. It can then produce useful information about the installation, rather than just demonstrating that a component became cooler.

Use the optional Immersion Summit track to explore the community's work when you want more depth. For the decision in front of you, focus on the evidence missing from the proposal. Resolving those gaps makes the pilot more informative and easier to support.
