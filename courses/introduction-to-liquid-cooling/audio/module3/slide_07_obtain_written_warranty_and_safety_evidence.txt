# pronunciation: OCP -> O C P
Separate four kinds of evidence. A fluid test characterizes the fluid under specified conditions. A component evaluation addresses the tested part. A system safety assessment considers the defined system and applicable requirements. A commercial warranty states what a supplier agrees to cover.

These documents answer different questions. A test result or safety evaluation doesn't automatically create warranty coverage for every server, tank and fluid combination. Likewise, an open specification isn't a promise that any implementation meets every requirement.

Ask for written coverage for the actual installation. Which equipment and fluid are included? Which operating and service conditions apply? Who receives a claim, who investigates a failure, and where do responsibilities pass between suppliers? Clear answers are more useful than a broad assurance that immersion warranties are no longer an issue.

O C P guidance and independent safety programs can help structure the evaluation. Their role should be understood alongside, rather than confused with, supplier commitments. A supported pilot brings the technical evidence, safety requirements and commercial coverage together. Keep the documents specific to the configuration you intend to operate, and confirm that the operating team understands the conditions attached to them.
