# pronunciation: PUE -> P U E; IT -> I T
Cooling benefits need a defined measurement boundary. Power usage effectiveness, usually shortened to P U E, compares total facility energy with I T energy over a consistent period. A lower ratio can indicate less facility overhead relative to I T energy. It doesn't, by itself, measure useful computing output or establish the efficiency of a particular processor.

A liquid heat-capture percentage answers a different question: how much of the specified equipment heat enters the liquid path? That percentage isn't a P U E value. Neither number alone establishes water consumption or carbon emissions.

Energy results depend on pumps, fans, heat exchangers and the facility's final heat-rejection method. Water use also depends on that downstream method and the operating environment. Carbon results depend on the energy source as well as the amount of energy used.

Heat reuse has its own requirements. The heat must reach a useful temperature and a real demand through a supported interface. Capturing warm fluid doesn't automatically establish useful reuse.

When comparing options, use a measured baseline and consistent conditions. Ask what changed, what was counted and what service the installation delivered. That makes the claimed benefit meaningful.
