Consider two changes at the same site. One team wants to add dense accelerator equipment with a specified liquid-cooling interface. Another plans a general-purpose server refresh that remains supported with the existing airflow. The decisions may differ even though both teams work in the same data center.

For each proposal, begin with the workload and the equipment requirements. Identify the heat load, the required inlet conditions and the interfaces that the supplier supports. Then examine the site. Does it have a suitable transport and heat-rejection path? Is there sufficient capacity under the conditions that matter?

Operational readiness is another part of the choice. People need access, training, spares and clear responsibilities. A technically capable system can still be difficult to support if those arrangements aren't ready.

Finally, connect the proposal to a business need. That might be enabling a workload, increasing supported density, improving operating efficiency or preparing for a future installation. Ask what evidence will establish the benefit. The useful decision is the supported cooling path for this workload and this site. A technology ranking by itself can't answer that question.
