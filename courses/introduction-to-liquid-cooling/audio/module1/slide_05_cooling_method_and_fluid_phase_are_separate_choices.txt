Two independent choices help organize the vocabulary. The first is where heat enters the working fluid. In direct liquid cooling, a cold plate provides a contained coolant path close to a heat-generating component. In immersion cooling, qualified equipment is placed in a dielectric liquid. Dielectric means electrically insulating under the specified conditions.

The second choice is how the fluid behaves. In single-phase operation, it stays liquid while its temperature changes. In two-phase operation, part of the heat transfer uses boiling and condensation. The change of phase carries energy as latent heat.

These choices form four combinations. Cold plates can be single-phase or two-phase. Immersion systems can also be single-phase or two-phase. Boiling alone doesn't tell you whether a system is immersion or cold-plate cooling. You need to know the physical arrangement.

This vocabulary describes architectures, not interchangeable products. A fluid selected for one application isn't automatically suitable for another. Equipment, materials, temperatures, pressures and service methods must fit the selected system. Keep those questions separate as we examine the two cooling approaches in more detail.
