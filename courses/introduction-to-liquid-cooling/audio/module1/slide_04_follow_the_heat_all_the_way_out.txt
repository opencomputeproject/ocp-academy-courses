Follow a unit of heat through three jobs: acquisition, transport and rejection. Acquisition is where heat leaves the equipment and enters a cooling path. Transport carries it somewhere else. Rejection releases it to the surroundings, unless a useful heat-reuse application receives it instead.

In a cold-plate system, heat conducts from a component into the plate and then enters the coolant. The moving coolant transports that energy to a heat exchanger. Another fluid, or a stream of air, receives the heat across the exchanger surfaces. The fluids remain separate.

The facility then carries out its own part of the journey. Depending on the installation, that may involve a water circuit, air handling and an outdoor heat-rejection system. A cooling device inside the room doesn't make the heat disappear.

The animation follows heat across these boundaries. Distinguish the movement of heat from the movement of fluid: heat can cross an exchanger even though the fluids don't mix. Whenever you hear that equipment is liquid cooled, ask the next question. Where does its heat go after it leaves the equipment?
