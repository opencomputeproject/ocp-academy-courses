A cooling problem can begin with a small area of intense heat, a heavily loaded rack, or a limit elsewhere in the installation. Those are related problems, but they aren't the same problem.

A processor concentrates its heat over a limited surface. Its temperature depends on how effectively that heat crosses the package and reaches a cooling medium. Meanwhile, a rack adds together the heat from many components. The building must eventually remove that total load, even when each individual component stays within its temperature limit.

Accelerators and dense computing systems make these questions more pressing. But there isn't one rack-power number at which every installation must abandon air cooling. Component design, airflow, inlet conditions and operating requirements all matter. Air remains appropriate for many supported workloads.

Liquid becomes useful when it provides a suitable path from the heat source to the rest of the cooling system. Begin with the equipment requirement and the site conditions. Ask where the limiting step occurs. That answer is more useful than choosing a cooling method from a headline about density.
