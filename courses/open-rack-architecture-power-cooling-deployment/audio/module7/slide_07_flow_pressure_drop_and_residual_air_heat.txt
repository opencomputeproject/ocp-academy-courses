Flow depends on the hydraulic system, not a single component rating. Hydraulic impedance describes how a path resists flow. Equipment, couplings, hoses and manifolds contribute to the configured path, and parallel branches can receive different flows under the same overall system conditions.

Don't use a pump's headline rating or a coupling size as proof that each equipment branch receives what it needs. Establish the available conditions at the required boundary and verify the circuit under the supported equipment population. Changes to routing or components can change that result.

Keep pressure limits and heat rejection in the same review without treating them as interchangeable. Adequate flow doesn't by itself establish an acceptable receiving temperature, and a thermal rating doesn't prove mechanical pressure compatibility. Residual air-side heat still needs its own supported path. The linked thermal-boundaries course gives deeper context; here, retain the complete path and the evidence at each handoff.
