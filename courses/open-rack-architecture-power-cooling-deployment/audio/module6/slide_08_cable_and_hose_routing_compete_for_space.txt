The extra frame volume can fill quickly when equipment, network connections, power paths and cooling lines are integrated. Routing is a shared spatial problem, not a final cosmetic step. Reserve the required envelopes before filling the remaining space.

Check bend, support and strain requirements using the selected components and supplier guidance. Then review service movement: which item moves, which connections remain, and whether adjacent equipment can stay within its permitted state. A route that works at rest can become unacceptable during removal.

Include containment and residual airflow where required. A hose or cable rearrangement can affect both service access and cooling. Record the accepted arrangement and who approves changes after deployment. This prevents a later addition from quietly occupying the clearance that the original integration review relied on. Wider packaging can offer useful options, but it doesn't make all internal space available for unrelated functions at the same time.
