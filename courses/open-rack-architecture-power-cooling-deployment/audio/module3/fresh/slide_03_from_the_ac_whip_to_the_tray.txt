# title: From the AC whip to the tray
# pronunciation: spaced initials are spoken separately; Open U = OpenU; G P Yous = GPUs.
Start at the cable entering the shelf. It's an A C whip: a cable assembly that carries alternating current from the facility to the rack's power equipment. Meta's sixty-amp high-power whip is one example for Open Rack version three. The sixty amps describe the cable assembly's current rating. To know the output available to equipment, we need to go farther along the drawing.

Inside the shelf, power supplies convert the input to direct current, or D C. The shelf delivers that output to the busbar. Each tray makes its own connection to that shared conductor assembly. Conversion inside the tray then provides the voltages its electronics need. Trace the route backward and you'll encounter two quite different connections: the tray contact that takes D C from the busbar, and the whip that brings A C into the shelf.
