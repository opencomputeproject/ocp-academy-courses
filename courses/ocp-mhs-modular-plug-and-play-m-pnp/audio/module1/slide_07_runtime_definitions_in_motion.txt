Self-description changes where platform knowledge lives. Instead of making every hardware variation another hard-coded firmware branch, a component supplies structured information about itself and its connections. A programmable device supplies a corresponding description of its registers, blocks, signals and interfaces.

The B M C firmware still needs to understand the schemas and implement the management contract. A preferred discovery path can use the H P M description to establish programmable-device connectivity; legacy paths may depend on prior knowledge.

The useful outcome is less model-specific knowledge embedded in firmware. It isn't automatic support for any arbitrary board. Conformance, supported features, and validation of the assembled platform determine whether the system works.
