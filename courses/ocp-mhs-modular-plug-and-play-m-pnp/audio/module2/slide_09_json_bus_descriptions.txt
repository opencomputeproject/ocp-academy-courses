The current hardware-component schema defines seventeen bus types. The point isn't that every component implements seventeen buses. The schema supplies a common vocabulary for the buses that a component actually has.

That vocabulary includes management and control paths such as I squared C, I cubed C, L T P I, M-PESTI and S G P I O. It also includes host and data interfaces, display connectivity, debug paths and intrusion signalling. Devices, buses and connectors are described together so firmware can interpret the implemented topology.

Use the schema's field definitions and applicability markers rather than inferring a universal requirement from the presence of a type. The Fru specification also states an important limit: features, functions or buses that a vendor doesn't describe are treated as vendor-specific and are outside the M-PNP plug-and-play framework. A generic parser can't discover information that wasn't supplied.
