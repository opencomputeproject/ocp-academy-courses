Discovery expands generally breadth-first. Phase zero initializes the control module and its B M C. Phase one discovers the H P M and onboard components, including the primary programmable device. Phase two reaches intermediate, non-endpoint boards such as risers and backplanes. Phase three collects endpoint options at the leaves.

Each discovered description helps interpret or configure the next part of the topology. Nested peripherals make that dependency especially important: the controller cannot simply assume every downstream connection from the first board's identity.

Keep boot-time discovery separate from runtime rediscovery. The specification leaves H P M and non-endpoint runtime rediscovery outside its scope. Endpoint rediscovery may be supported when the endpoint's applicable industry standard defines hot-plug capability. A breadth-first boot diagram is not permission to hot-swap every component.
